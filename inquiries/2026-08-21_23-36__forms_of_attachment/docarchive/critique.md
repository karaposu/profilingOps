## Phase 2–3 — Adversarial Evaluation + Verdicts

### 1 · The projection finding

**Prosecution.** *External-anchor + substance sub-axes.* Show the mapping row by row — and it fails as stated. *Coerced* is not a gloss on *consequences of losing you*; it names what the **receiver** does. Same for *admiration*, *Transactional* and *Bonded*. The rows add content the variables do not contain, so the rows are not the variable list under other names.

**Defense.** The added content is not free content — it is **determined**. Given which variable dominates, the receiver-state follows; the corpus derives exactly this elsewhere, sourcing *depth* to Resonance and *coercion* to Fear-dominance. A set whose members' identities are fixed by another set's members, at mixed arity, with no admission gate of its own, remains a projection whether the names are identical or derived.

**Collision.** Prosecution wins on the wording; defense wins on the structure — and the corrected form is **stronger**, because it survives the objection that the names add content by explaining *why* they do.

**Verdict: REFINE.** *Target:* state it as **each row's identity is determined by which variable dominates**, not as *the rows are the variables renamed*. Everything downstream that cites "renaming" inherits this correction.

### 2 · The premise correction — compliance is a signature, not a member

**Prosecution.** *User-perspective sub-axis.* The user's *form* may simply **mean signature**. If so, compliance is a form, there are four others, and the question has a clean five-item answer — which the correction talks them out of.

**Defense.** The corpus does hold signature and membership apart, and the word *other* does presuppose a list.

**Collision.** **Prosecution largely wins, and productively.** *Signature* is a legitimate reading of *form*, and under it the question is answerable with an actual list. The correction should not replace the user's reading; it should **name both** and give the list under each.

**Verdict: REFINE.** *Target:* *"If you mean what generates the attachment — four. If you mean what it looks like from outside, which is where compliance sits — five, and compliance is one of them."* The correction survives as a **level-assignment**, not as a rejection of the premise.

### 5 · The coherence rule *(contradictory sender-intentions are coherence-stressed)*

**Prosecution.** *Specification-gap and external-anchor sub-axes.* The derivation says *will benefit me* and *will harm me* are contradictory. **That requires the threat-of-harm reading of Fear** — and the corpus has two readings and has explicitly declared rather than settled between them. Under **cost-of-loss** (*"consequences of losing you"*), Hope + Fear is not contradictory at all; it is mutually reinforcing — *they benefit me and losing them would cost me* describes an ordinary dependency. The rule evaporates under one of the two live readings.

Worse for the general claim: the two extrapolations (*Charm + Fear is coherent — a powerful person*; *Fear + Resonance is coherent — an intimate*) are **intuitions**, not derivations. One derived instance plus two intuitions is a heuristic, not a rule.

**Defense.** The corpus's worked case is specifically *manipulation wariness* — suspecting an ulterior motive — which is the threat-of-harm reading, so the corpus's own instance sits on the side the rule needs.

**Collision.** **Prosecution wins substantially.** The rule holds **conditionally on the threat-of-harm reading**, and its generality rests on unexamined intuition.

**Verdict: REFINE.** *Target:* ship it as **one derived instance, conditional on a fork the corpus has not settled**, not as a valence rule over the space. *(Noted: this is the second consecutive inquiry in which the unsettled Fear definition turned out load-bearing on the central result. ## Phase 3.5 — Assembly Check

Examining survivors and refinements together, one architecture emerges that none of them states.

> ### ASM-3 — The question has two good answers, at two levels, and the corpus's own table fuses them
>
> **If *form* means what generates the attachment: four.** Charm, Hope, Fear, Resonance — the property-belief categories, complete at the chosen resolution and complete for a structural reason available now.
>
> **If *form* means what the attachment looks like from outside — which is where compliance sits: five.** The persistence entries. **Compliance is one of them**, so on this reading the premise is correct and the question has an ordinary answer.
>
> **And the reason it felt like there should be one answer is visible in the table's own columns.** The attachment-type table has three: *Dominant variable · TYPE · Persistence under change*. Column one is a **generator**. Column three is a **signature**. Column two is a name that belongs to neither — it is a receiver-state word (*admiration, Transactional, Coerced, Bonded*) attached to a row **individuated by column one** and **behaving as column three**. The table is a **join of two enumerations**, and *TYPE* is the join key. That is why *type* feels like it should be the list of forms and never quite works as one.

**Adversarial evaluation of ASM-3.**

**Prosecution.** Two objections. **(1)** If the table is a join, the mixed fifth row (`Hope + Resonance`) breaks it — a join key should not have a compound on one side. **(2)** This makes the corpus's central output-dimension a bookkeeping artefact, which is a large claim from four rows.

**Defense.** **(1)** The mixed row is the join's own evidence: it is the one row where the generator side is a *pair*, and it is exactly the row whose name (*Collaborative*) is least like a receiver-state word and most like a relationship description — the join stretching under load. **(2)** The claim is not that the dimension is an artefact; it is that **one column of it is**. Columns one and three are load-bearing and survive intact; column two is where two enumerations were fused. Candidate 1's refined form says the same thing from the other side — the names are *determined*, which is precisely what a join key is.

**Collision.** Defense holds on both, and objection (1) supplies the assembly's best evidence.

**Verdict: SURVIVE — no caveats on any critical dimension.**
D1 quotes every row · D2 adds nothing and unifies two existing structures · D3 answers **both** halves of the question and gives the user two actual lists · D4 leaves the corpus lighter · D5 claims nothing empirical · D6 is unaffected (it describes a table, not a runtime read) · D7 is the best of any candidate — two short lists in the asker's own terms · D8 is satisfied by construction, since the generator side is explicitly resolution-indexed and the signature side is not resolution-sensitive at all.

**ASM-3 outranks every individual survivor and both prior assemblies.**
