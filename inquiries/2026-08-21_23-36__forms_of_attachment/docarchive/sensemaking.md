## Phase 2 — Perspective Checking

### Technical / Logical

Take the corpus's own sentence literally: type reads off the variable-weight **distribution**. A distribution over four variables is a vector, and reading a type off it is a projection of that vector onto a name.

Two readings follow, and they give different answers to the question:

- **Categorical reading.** A type is a *dominance pattern*. With four variables the non-null patterns are four singles, six pairs, four triples and one four-way — **fifteen**. The corpus names five. Ten are unnamed, and they are specific rather than abstract: Charm+Hope, Charm+Fear, Charm+Resonance, Hope+Fear, Fear+Resonance, and every triple.
- **Basis reading.** A type is a *weight vector* over four basis directions. There are **four** forms and an open space of combinations. Named combinations are labels of convenience, exactly as Hope's tiering was decided by *"operational frequency."*

The basis reading is better supported: *distribution* is vector language, and the corpus already made this exact call one level down for Hope's sub-flavours with a stated rationale. But it is a **transfer** from a variable-interior taxonomy to an output taxonomy — the same shape of move the immediately-prior inquiry had to flag at medium confidence.

*New anchor —* **KI9: under either reading, the five named types are landmarks in a space, not members of a list.** The disagreement between the readings is about how many landmarks are worth naming, not about whether the underlying object is a space. That is the more robust claim, and it survives whichever reading wins.

### Human / User

The user was handed one member and asked for the set. That is an entirely reasonable move — it is how anyone checks whether a category is real. And it is a *test*, not just a request: the framing statement says *"maybe it is wrong maybe correct, lets understand."*

What they will actually get is a correction plus something better than a list. That is the third consecutive answer of this shape in this lineage, and it is worth saying plainly rather than dressing up: **the intuition that there should be a set is right; the expectation that it is a short finite list is what the theory does not support.**

*New anchor —* **KI10: the question functions as a completeness probe on the type taxonomy, and the taxonomy fails that probe for a structural reason rather than an empirical one.** The corpus attributes its incompleteness to *pending empirical work*. Under the projection reading the incompleteness is not empirical at all — you cannot finish a list of landmarks in a continuous space by collecting more data.

### Strategic / Long-term

Three inquiries have leaned on the type taxonomy. If it is a projection of the variables rather than an independent dimension, what propagates?

Checking each: the anger inquiry used the Coerced row's persistence behaviour — unaffected, since the behaviour is real whether the row is primitive or projected. The leadership inquiry used types only in passing. The immediately-prior inquiry used the persistence column heavily — and this is where it bites.

*New anchor —* **SP6: the prior finding's independence claim is weakened by KI1.** That finding argued its two-family reading was confirmed by two *independent* derivations — one about **variables** (the unilateral/dyadic split) and one about **trajectories** (the persistence column) — on the ground that they were *"different objects… neither citing the other."* If the type rows are a projection of the variables, then variables and trajectories are **not two objects**; they are one object and its behaviour over time. The two-family result itself stands — it is derivable from either side alone — but the claim that two independent derivations confirmed it downgrades to **one structure appearing in two places**, which is evidence of internal consistency rather than independent corroboration.

### Risk / Failure

The most uncomfortable perspective, and it produces two costs.

The first is SP6 above — a correction to a finding written an hour ago, on the specific claim it was proudest of.

The second is about what a deflationary answer does to the theory's usefulness. If the types are the variables renamed, a practitioner gains nothing from the type dimension that they did not already have from the variable scores. The corpus presents magnitude and type as **two independent output dimensions**; under the projection reading they are two readings of the same four numbers — the sum and the mix. That is still a real distinction, but "independent" is doing less work than it appears to.

*New anchor —* **C9: the projection reading costs the type dimension its appearance of independence, and that must be stated rather than left to be discovered.**

*New anchor —* **C10: the deflation has a limit worth naming.** The mix genuinely predicts something the sum does not — persistence behaviour. So type is not redundant; it is *derived*. Derived-but-predictive is a real status, and it is the honest description.

### Resource / Feasibility

Nothing here is testable. No type row has a worked case beyond the corpus's own examples; the taxonomy's comprehensiveness is deferred to empirical work that has not happened; the implementation holds degrees only. Any answer is structural.

*New anchor —* **C11: the deliverable's ceiling is a structural account of what kind of thing the forms are, plus whatever enumeration follows from that account — not a validated list.**

## Phase 4 *(protocol Phase 3)* — Ambiguity Collapse

### Ambiguity A1 — Which level does *form* name?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** there is one level. *Form of attachment* obviously means *kind of attachment*, and the corpus has exactly one list of kinds — the five types. Multiplying levels manufactures complexity to avoid a short answer.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** the levels are not interpretive lenses; they are **architecturally distinct positions with different membership rules**. A generator is admitted by the generate-alone test; a sub-flavour by a structural unifier plus a discriminating axis; a modulator by eight criteria; a type by **nothing at all**, because it is read off. Positions in an architecture that admit members by different tests are different positions, whatever a reader means by *kind*. And compliance — the question's own example — sits at the signature level, not the type level, which the counter cannot accommodate without discarding the example the question is built on.

**Confidence: HIGH.**

**Resolution:** *form* is level-ambiguous across five in-scope positions — generator · composition · signature · relation-shape · sub-flavour. Every claim below names its level.

- **Fixed:** the answer must assign a level before it can enumerate.
- **No longer allowed:** any unqualified count of attachment's forms.
- **Depends on this:** the entire enumeration; the premise verdict; the completeness verdict.

### Ambiguity A2 — Is the type taxonomy a list of kinds, or a projection of the variables?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** it is a genuine taxonomy. The corpus calls type an output **dimension** independent of magnitude, gives the rows their own names, predicts distinct persistence behaviour per row, and flags the set as pending empirical validation — which only makes sense for a taxonomy with members still to be discovered.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** read the rows. Charm-dominant → *Status/admiration*; Hope-dominant → *Transactional*; Fear-dominant → *Coerced*; Resonance-dominant → *Bonded*. **Each row's identity is exhausted by which variable dominates**; the name adds a gloss and no content. The corpus states this itself — *"no separate mechanism is needed"* — and the table's fifth row is a **pair**, so the set is not even at one level. A taxonomy whose members are fixed entirely by another list's members, at mixed arity, with no admission gate of its own, is a projection.

The counter's strongest element survives and is re-sited: the rows *do* predict distinct persistence behaviour. But that follows from the variables' own properties — how removable each variable's ground is — which the immediately-prior inquiry established. Prediction confirms the projection is **informative**; it does not make it primitive.

**Confidence: HIGH.**

**Resolution:** the five attachment types are **four variables plus one named pair** — a projection of the variable list, not an independent taxonomy.

- **Fixed:** the type dimension is *derived and predictive*, not primitive.
- **No longer allowed:** treating the type list as a list with undiscovered members waiting to be found empirically.
- **Depends on this:** the completeness verdict; the tension noted at A5.
- **Changed in the model:** the question's most likely target turns out not to be the kind of thing that can be completed.

### Ambiguity A3 — Categorical cells or a basis?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** cells. The corpus presents a table with discrete rows and speaks of *"which variable dominated"* — dominance is categorical. The basis reading imports linear-algebra vocabulary from a variable-interior taxonomy into an output taxonomy, which is a transfer.

**Why the counter partly holds — and what survives:** the transfer is real and is flagged. What defeats the pure categorical reading is the corpus's own phrase — type reads off the variable-weight **distribution** — and its own precedent for exactly this decision one level down, where a partition presentation was rejected because instances are empirically multi-member and a single pick *"would lose the multi-flavor structure."* Concrete attachments are as multi-member as concrete hopes.

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** The transfer is argued, not cited, and the counter has structural merit.

**Resolution:** the basis reading is preferred and the categorical reading is retained as a live alternative. **KI9 is what both share and is asserted at HIGH confidence:** either way, the five named types are **landmarks in a space**, not members of a list. The disagreement is over how many landmarks deserve names — which C12 places on the blocked side.

- **Fixed:** the object under enumeration is a space.
- **No longer allowed:** claiming a definite count of types.
- **Depends on this:** how the unnamed cells are described — as *missing members* under the categorical reading, or as *unnamed regions* under the basis reading.

### Ambiguity A4 — Is compliance a form at all?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** yes, and the prior finding said so — compliance is a form in the *made-of-the-same-stuff* sense. So the question's premise is sound and this whole conflict is manufactured.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** it fails on the word *other*, which is what makes the question an enumeration. The prior finding's composition verdict says compliance is made of the same material; it says nothing about compliance being an **entry on a list**. Its other verdict speaks directly to that: compliance is the **signature** of the Fear-dominant case, and the corpus keeps signature and membership apart inside a single component — Self-Positioning has species *and* a failure signature, and devaluation appears among neither the species nor any list of Self-Positioning kinds.

So compliance is a form in the composition sense and **not a member** in the enumeration sense. Since the question asks for *others*, the enumeration sense is the one in play.

**Confidence: HIGH.**

**Resolution:** the premise is **correct in the sense the prior finding established and wrong in the sense this question needs.** Compliance is the signature of a form, not a form. Its cell — Fear-dominant — is the member; *Coerced* is that member's name; *compliance* is what the member looks like from outside.

- **Fixed:** the question's example belongs one level below its phrasing.
- **No longer allowed:** answering *"what other forms"* by listing signatures alongside compliance without saying they are signatures.
- **Depends on this:** the shape of the answer — it must correct the level before enumerating.
- **Changed in the model:** *"maybe it is wrong"* turns out to be right, in a precise and narrow way.

### Ambiguity A5 — What does the question's completeness ask actually get?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** it gets *"we don't know"* — the taxonomy says comprehensiveness is pending empirical work, so the honest answer is to wait for data.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** it misattributes the incompleteness. Under A2, the singles **are** complete, and complete for a structural reason available now: there are four variables, the variable list is orthogonality-gated, and each single row is one variable. The combinations are not incomplete either — they are **not a list**, so completeness does not apply to them. What is genuinely pending is *which combinations deserve names*, and that is an operational-frequency question, exactly as Hope's tiering was.

So the taxonomy's own flag is right that something is pending and wrong about what. No amount of data finishes a list of landmarks in a space.

**Confidence: HIGH** on the reattribution; **MEDIUM** on the claim that four is the final variable count, since the corpus itself names three candidate additional modulators and nine adjacent mechanisms with an absorption clause.

**Resolution:** at the single-variable level the set is **complete at four**; at the combination level completeness does not apply; what is pending is **naming**, and it is blocked on frequency data.

### Ambiguity A6 — Which enumeration method should an answer use?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** the sub-flavour framework, obviously — it is the only method the corpus named and the only one declared generalizable.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** the framework is declared generalizable **to variables**, and its four steps presuppose a variable. Its structural unifier asks *what makes a belief belong to this variable*; its discriminating axis is *Object-of-X*. Neither has a meaning for an output whose members are read off rather than admitted. Applying it to the type level would require inventing a unifier for a projection.

Counterexample-absorption has the opposite problem: it works anywhere, and it was never validated as a method — the species list simply grew that way.

**Confidence: MEDIUM.**

**Resolution:** **the method is level-indexed.** At the sub-flavour level the framework applies directly and is already parked for three variables. At the type level neither method applies, because the level is a projection and inherits its membership from below.

- **Fixed:** method follows level.
- **No longer allowed:** applying the sub-flavour framework to the type table.
- **Changed in the model:** the two-method surface dissolves rather than being adjudicated — they were never competing for the same level.

### Ambiguity A7 — Load-bearing concept test: *the four levels* and *projection*

### Ambiguity A8 — Specific-versus-pattern: is the projection finding about one table, or about how this corpus builds lists?

*(Required per the specific-versus-pattern recognition cue — the projection claim is built from four rows of one table.)*

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** four rows is a very small set. The corpus may have other projected lists, and calling out only this one may be an artifact of which table was read closely.

Where the counter holds: this is a check of four other lists, not a proof about list-building in general.

**Confidence: HIGH** that the projection is specific to the type table; **LOW** on any wider claim about the corpus's list-building habits.

**Resolution:** the finding is about **one table, for a stated structural reason**, and is not generalised.

The list the question most plausibly means — the five attachment types — is not an independent taxonomy. Its first four rows are the four variables under other names, and its fifth is a named pair. It has no admission gate because it needs none: types are read off, not admitted.

So the answer to *"what other forms exist"* is not a list of undiscovered members. At the single-variable level the set is **complete at four** — status-attachment, transactional attachment, coerced attachment, bonded attachment — and complete for a structural reason available now. At the combination level there is a **space**, in which the corpus has named one region, ruled one out, worked one without labelling it, and left the rest untouched. And at the sub-flavour level the corpus has done the work for **one variable of four** and parked the other three.

No longer viable: a definite count of types; the reading that the taxonomy is empirically incomplete; and any unqualified answer that does not name its level.

### SV6 — Stabilized Model

**The premise is wrong in the way that matters here, and right in the way the last inquiry established.** Compliance is made of the same material as attachment — that verdict stands. But compliance is not an **entry on a list**. It is the **signature** of one entry: what the Fear-dominant case looks like from outside, the way devaluation is what a Self-Positioning collapse looks like from outside. The member is the Fear-dominant cell; its name in the theory is *Coerced*; *compliance* is its outside view. So *"what **other** forms exist"* was asking for other members of a list its own example is not on.

**And the list it points at is not a list.** The theory's five attachment types are its **four variables plus one named pair**. Charm-dominant is *Status/admiration*; Hope-dominant is *Transactional*; Fear-dominant is *Coerced*; Resonance-dominant is *Bonded*. Each row's identity is exhausted by which variable dominates — the theory says so itself: *"TYPE reads directly from the variable-weight distribution — no separate mechanism is needed."* The fifth row, *Collaborative*, is a **combination** with a label. A set whose members are fixed entirely by another set's members, at mixed arity, with no admission test of its own, is a **projection** of that other set.

**Which is why it has no admission gate.** Every other list in this corpus does — a variable needs the generate-alone test, a modulator needs eight criteria, a sub-flavour needs a structural unifier and a discriminating axis. The type list needs nothing, and that is not an oversight. Nothing is admitted to a projection.

**So the answer to the question, level by level:**

- **Forms as generators — four, and complete.** Charm, Hope, Fear, Resonance. Complete for a structural reason available today: the variable list is orthogonality-gated and each single-variable type row is one variable. *(Held at medium confidence on finality, since the corpus itself names three candidate additional modulators and nine adjacent mechanisms with an absorption clause.)*
- **Forms as compositions — a space, not a list.** With four variables there are six pairs, four triples and one four-way case. The corpus **names one** (Hope + Resonance → *Collaborative*), **rules one out** (Hope + Fear was tested and absorbed by Coherence — contradictory mixes produce Model-Collapse rather than a type, so **not every combination yields a form**), **has worked one without labelling it** (the respected volatile figure, described as generating *"both at once — deference from fear, and admiration from charm"* — a Charm + Fear case), and leaves the rest untouched. Completeness does not apply to a space; what is pending is which regions deserve names, and that is an operational-frequency question — exactly how Hope's own tiering was decided — with no frequency data in the project.
- **Forms as signatures — where compliance actually lives.** Five are named, in the persistence column. This is the level the question's example belongs to.
- **Forms as relation-shapes — four named and never gathered.** Followership, peer, devoted-individual, movement. They sit in separate documents, none citing the others as members of a common list.
- **Forms as sub-flavours — seven for one variable, and three variables parked.** Hope has seven. The framework that produced them is *declared generalizable to any variable*, and Charm, Fear and Resonance sub-taxonomies are **named and deferred**. This is the level where the corpus has genuinely answered a *what forms exist* question, and it has answered it for a quarter of the variables.

**What this costs.** The type dimension loses its appearance of independence. The corpus presents magnitude and type as two independent outputs; under this reading they are two readings of the same four numbers — the sum and the mix. Type is not redundant — the mix predicts persistence behaviour that the sum does not — but it is **derived**, not primitive. *Derived-but-predictive* is the honest description.

**And one correction upstream.** The immediately-prior finding argued its two-family result was confirmed by two *independent* derivations — one over variables, one over trajectories, *"different objects… neither citing the other."* If the type rows are a projection of the variables, those are not two objects; they are one object and its behaviour over time. **The two-family result stands** — it is derivable from either side alone — but the independence claim downgrades to one structure appearing in two places, which is internal consistency rather than corroboration.

**What is not settled.** Whether a type is a categorical cell or a weight vector over a basis. The basis reading is better supported — *distribution* is vector language, and the corpus made exactly this call one level down for Hope with a stated rationale — but it is a transfer from a variable-interior taxonomy to an output taxonomy, and it is held at medium confidence. The distinction matters for how the unnamed cells are described: **missing members** under the categorical reading, **unnamed regions** under the basis reading. What both readings share, and what is asserted at high confidence, is that the five named types are landmarks rather than a membership list.
