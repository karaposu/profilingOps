## Stage 1 — Itemize

**count = 1** · **items:** `[I1]`

**I1 text:** *"if complience is form of attachment, what other forms of attachment exists? lets dive deep into this one, maybe it is wrong maybe correct, lets understand"*

**Itemize reasoning.** Three clauses, one item.

The first clause is a **conditional question** — a premise plus an enumeration ask. The second (*"lets dive deep into this one"*) is a **depth directive**, a modifier on how the work is done rather than a work item. The third (*"maybe it is wrong maybe correct, lets understand"*) is a **stance on the premise**, and its referent — what *"it"* names — is itself open.

**The split signal was checked and rejected.** There is a real one: *"what other forms exist"* has an enumeration shape (a list) while *"maybe it is wrong maybe correct"* has a verification shape (a verdict), and differing deliverable-types is the canonical two-item signal. It does not hold here, for two reasons. There is no conjunction joining two tasks — the second sentence is *about* the first, anchored by *"this one"*. And the two are not independently answerable: an enumeration of *other* forms presupposes that *form* has a fixed referent, which is exactly what the premise-question governs.

Asymmetric-failure bias toward keep-together applies and is not overridden. **Count = 1.**

### MQA — Meta-question alignment

**Two reconciles and one surface.**

**Reconcile 1 — joint axis `what fixes the enumeration's unit`.** MQ1's `establish-what-form-means`, MQ2's verdict sub-axis on which list *form* refers to, and MQ3's `produce-a-derivation` all span one question: **what kind of thing is being counted?** A TYPE row, a signature, a family, a variable configuration — these are different lists with different lengths, and *"other forms"* has no referent until one is fixed. The `produce-a-derivation` endpoint belongs here because a generating rule fixes the unit by construction, where a list presupposes it.

**Reconcile 2 — joint axis `is the premise inherited or re-opened`.** MQ1's `test-the-premise`, MQ2's verdict sub-axis on the prior verdict's status, and MQ3's `produce-a-premise-verdict` span one question: is the antecedent **material the answer builds on**, or **the thing the answer re-examines?** The statement's *"if"* reads as granting it; its *"maybe it is wrong"* reads as suspending it. Both readings are in the same sentence.

**Surface — irreducible overlap.** MQ1's `find-the-complete-set` and `test-whether-the-question-is-well-posed` share territory and resist a joint axis. *"What other forms of attachment exists?"* can be asking for **completeness within an assumed category** — how many are there, name them all — or it can be **doubting the category** — is *forms of attachment* something this theory has, such that a complete set could exist? The first assumes the enumeration is possible and asks for its extent; the second asks whether the enumeration is possible at all. Both fit the wording exactly, and forcing a joint axis would collapse a fork between *count the members* and *check that there are members to count*.

### Self-assessment verdict

**MED-FLAG**

*Clean self-check, high friction.* The flagged condition is not a defect in this bundle — it is a condition the downstream pipeline should see before it consumes the framing:

- **The statement's antecedent is already answered in session context, and the statement re-opens it.** The immediately-prior inquiry concluded that compliance is a form of attachment in the *made-of-the-same-stuff* sense, is **not** in the *species-of* sense, and that the relation between them is signature-to-state. Both readings are preserved in the variant set (variant 2 and variant 6 carry the re-opening; variant 1 carries the inheritance).

*Two further conditions carried without rising to flags:*

- **The enumeration has no unit until one is chosen.** *Form* could name a TYPE row, a signature, a family, or a variable configuration — four different lists of four different lengths. Reconcile 1 keeps that open.
- **The surface is live.** *"What other forms exists"* may be asking how many there are, or whether there are any to count. The first assumes the category; the second doubts it.
