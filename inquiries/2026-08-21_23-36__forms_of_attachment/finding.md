## Finding Summary

- **The question has two good answers, at two levels, and both are short.** If *form* means **what generates the attachment**: four — Charm, Hope, Fear, Resonance. If *form* means **what the attachment looks like from outside**, which is where compliance sits: five — the persistence behaviours. **Compliance is one of those five**, so on that reading the premise is correct and the question has an ordinary answer.

- **The reason it felt like there should be one answer is visible in the theory's own table.** The attachment-type table has three columns: *Dominant variable · TYPE · Persistence under change*. Column one is a **generator**. Column three is a **signature**. Column two is a name belonging to neither. **The table is a join of two enumerations**, and *TYPE* is the key that fuses them.

- **The five attachment types are not an independent taxonomy.** Each row's identity is **determined by** which variable dominates — the theory says so itself: *"TYPE reads directly from the variable-weight distribution — no separate mechanism is needed."* Four rows are single variables; the fifth is a pair.

- **The row-names are not the variables renamed, and that distinction matters.** *Status/admiration*, *Transactional*, *Coerced*, *Bonded/deep* all describe the **receiver's state or the relationship**, while the variables describe **believed properties of the sender**. The names add content — but the content is determined by the variable, which is what makes this a projection rather than a relabelling.

- **This explains a gap nobody had noticed.** Every other named set in the theory has an admission test — a variable needs the generate-alone test, a modulator needs eight criteria, a sub-flavour needs a structural unifier plus a discriminating axis. **The type list has none.** That is not an oversight: nothing is admitted to a projection.

- **The taxonomy's own incompleteness flag misattributes the problem.** It says comprehensiveness is *"pending empirical work."* At the single-variable level the set is already complete, and complete for a structural reason available today. At the combination level completeness does not apply, because a space is not a list. What is genuinely pending is which regions deserve **names**.

- **And the naming blocker is not what it looks like.** The corpus tiered its one completed enumeration by *operational frequency* — and frequency is a property of an application, not of a dataset. Nothing in this project applies the theory to anything yet. The block lifts when the theory is **used**, not when data is collected.

- **The count of four is resolution-dependent, and the corpus proved this on itself.** It split **one** of the four into **seven** sub-flavours and the count of four did not change. So *four variables* and *seven sub-flavours of Hope* are the same enumeration read at two grains. Charm's own definition already names three things — *status, competence, high-value signals* — and nothing but a decision keeps it one variable.

- **The combination level is a space, and part of its structure is derivable.** With four variables there are fifteen non-null mixes. The theory names **one** of them (*Hope + Resonance → Collaborative*), has **tested and ruled out** one, and has **worked one in detail without ever labelling it**.

- **Not every combination forms a type — and there is a reason.** *This person will benefit me* and *this person will harm me* are contradictory claims about one sender, and contradictory claims about one sender are what Model-Collapse is. **This holds only under one of the theory's two live definitions of Fear**, and the corpus has declared rather than settled between them.

- **Each region's behaviour is derivable in shape, though not in magnitude.** A Charm + Fear attachment has two externally-grounded variables clearing at different rates, so it degrades in two stages. A Hope + Resonance attachment has one removable ground and one not, so it falls to a floor rather than to zero.

- **Any region-assignment is unstable in a dimension the theory already measures.** Signal specificity scales **each variable separately**, so a single message can be specific about one variable and generic about another — which moves which variable dominates without anything else changing.

- **What this costs the theory.** The type dimension loses its appearance of independence. Magnitude and type are presented as two independent outputs; they are two readings of the same four numbers — the sum and the mix. Type is not redundant, because the mix predicts persistence that the sum does not. It is **derived**, not primitive.

## Finding

### 0. Why this question came up

The theory under discussion is APT, used in this project to profile people from conversations. Attachment is a sum of four belief-variables — Charm, Hope, Fear and Resonance — multiplied by three gates. The sum is additive, so each variable can generate attachment on its own; the gates are multiplicative, so any one at zero collapses the whole result.

An inquiry immediately before this one asked whether compliance is a form of attachment. It answered: yes in the sense of being made of the same material, no in the sense of being a *kind* of it, and the actual relation is **signature-to-state** — compliance is what a Fear-dominant attachment looks like from outside, the way *devaluation* is what a Self-Positioning collapse looks like from outside.

The user then did the natural thing with a verdict like that: they asked for the rest of the set. And they did something less common — they marked their own premise as possibly wrong in the same breath.

Both moves turn out to be productive. The premise is right under one reading and wrong under another; the request for the set surfaces a structural fact about the theory that nobody had stated.

---

### 1. The two answers

**If *form* means what generates the attachment, there are four.**

Charm — *this person is high-status, competent, impressive.*
Hope — *interaction with this person will produce future benefit.*
Fear — *there are consequences to losing them* (or, on the theory's other definition, *they can harm me*).
Resonance — *this person shares world-models and ways of seeing with me.*

These are complete at this grain, and complete for a reason available now rather than pending on data. The variable list is gated by an explicit test — a candidate must generate attachment on its own, at zero values of everything else — and each single-variable row of the attachment-type table is one of these four.

**If *form* means what the attachment looks like from outside, there are five.**

These are the theory's *Persistence under change* entries: *weakens as access to the Charm source recedes* · *dissolves when exchange ends* · *reverses on threat removal* · *persists through absence, maintained via shared model* · *high persistence; both transacts and connects*.

**Compliance is the third of these.** So on this reading the user's premise is correct, the question is well-formed, and the answer is an ordinary list of five with compliance among them.

Neither reading is wrong. They are answers to two different questions that one word was carrying.

---

### 2. Why one word was carrying two questions

Look at the theory's attachment-type table as a table rather than as a taxonomy.

| Dominant variable | TYPE | Persistence under change |
|---|---|---|
| Charm-dominant | Status / admiration | Weakens as access to the Charm source recedes |
| Hope-dominant | Transactional | Dissolves when exchange ends |
| Fear-dominant | Coerced | Reverses (relief, not attachment) on threat removal |
| Resonance-dominant | Bonded / deep | Persists through absence; maintained via shared model |
| Hope + Resonance | Collaborative | High persistence; both transacts and connects |

**Column one is a generator. Column three is a signature. Column two is a name that belongs to neither.**

*TYPE* is the key that joins them. Each row is *individuated by* column one and *behaves as* column three, and column two is a label sitting across the seam. That is why *type* feels like it should be the list of forms and never quite works as one — it is not a list of anything in particular. It is a join key.

The mixed row is the clearest evidence for this reading, not the exception to it. It is the one row whose generator side is a **pair**, and it is the one row whose name — *Collaborative* — reads least like the others. That is the join stretching under load.

---

### 3. The type list is a projection, and that explains its missing gate

Each row's identity is **entirely determined by** which variable dominates. The theory states this directly: *"Because `f` is additive, TYPE reads directly from the variable-weight distribution — no separate mechanism is needed."*

One correction is worth making carefully, because the obvious version of this claim is wrong.

The row-names are **not** the variables renamed. Charm is *"how much they look up to you — status, competence, high-value signals"*; the row-name is *Status/**admiration***, and admiration is a state of the receiver. Hope is *"belief they can get benefit from you"*; the row-name is *Transactional*, which describes the relationship. Fear is *"consequences of losing you"*; the row-name is *Coerced*, which describes what the receiver does. Resonance is *"shares world-models"*; the row-name is *Bonded/**deep***, and depth is attributed to Resonance elsewhere in the corpus but is not in the variable's own definition.

**Every one of the four names adds content the variable does not contain.** The variables describe believed properties of the *sender*; the names describe the state of the *receiver* or of the relationship.

So the honest statement is not *the rows are the variables renamed* but **each row's identity is determined by which variable dominates**. The names add content, and the content is a function of the variable. That is a projection. It is not a relabelling.

**And it explains something that would otherwise look like sloppiness.** Every other named set in this theory has an admission test that has visibly bitten. A candidate variable must pass the generate-alone test — that is what excluded signal specificity from becoming a fifth variable. A candidate modulator must pass eight criteria — that is what excluded Vitality. A candidate sub-flavour must have a structural unifier and a distinct object — that is what excluded Status-Hope and Pleasure-Hope.

**The attachment-type list has no admission test at all.** Not a weak one; none. That is not an oversight. Nothing is admitted to a projection — the members arrive from the list below, already decided.

---

### 4. What "complete" means here, and where the taxonomy's own flag goes wrong

The attachment-type table ships with a flag: *"Taxonomy is provisional and illustrative. Comprehensive TYPE classification pending empirical work."* Its own frontier list asks *"Are these the correct clusters? Are they exhaustive?"*

That flag misattributes the incompleteness. Take the levels separately.

**At the single-variable level the set is complete, now, and for a structural reason.** There are four variables, the variable list is gated, and each single-variable row is one of them. No amount of data would add a sixth row of that kind without first adding a fifth variable — which would be a change to the variable list, not a discovery about types.

**At the combination level completeness does not apply**, because the combinations are not a list. They are a space, and a space does not have a complete membership.

**What is genuinely pending is the naming.** Which regions of the space deserve labels is an open question — and it is the question the flag should have named.

Here the blocker turns out not to be what it appears. The corpus tiered its one completed enumeration — Hope's seven sub-flavours, split into a primary four and a secondary three — explicitly by **operational frequency**: primary members appear in most scenarios, secondary ones in specific contexts. Frequency is a property of an application, not of a dataset.

**Nothing in this project applies the theory to anything yet.** So the naming block lifts when the theory is *used*, not when data is *collected*. Those are different events with different timelines, and the current flag points at the wrong one.

---

### 5. The count of four is a choice, and the corpus has already made a different one

There is no resolution-independent answer to *how many forms attachment has*, and the demonstration is internal to the corpus.

It split **one** of the four variables — Hope — into **seven** sub-flavours, and the count of four did not change. So *four variables* and *seven sub-flavours of Hope* are the same enumeration read at two grains. Nothing about the theory changed; the description got finer in one place.

Charm's own definition already names three things: *status, competence, high-value signals*. Nothing but a decision keeps that one variable rather than three. And the framework that produced Hope's seven is explicitly **declared generalizable to every other variable**, with Charm, Fear and Resonance sub-taxonomies **named and deferred**.

One qualification, because the strong version of this is wrong. This does **not** mean the levels are illusory. Variables and sub-flavours are gated differently — a variable must generate attachment alone, a sub-flavour need not — and the corpus states explicitly that no sub-flavour passes the test that would make it a variable. The levels are real. **What is resolution-dependent is the count, not the structure.**

---

### 6. The combination level: a space, partly mapped

With four variables there are fifteen non-null mixes — four singles, six pairs, four triples, and one four-way case.

The theory currently **names one** of the eleven non-single mixes: *Hope + Resonance → Collaborative*.

It has **tested and ruled out** one. A candidate for negative interaction terms proposed that contradictory combinations reduce attachment through a negative term in the sum. That candidate was killed and absorbed by the Coherence gate instead: *Fear + Hope from a stranger → manipulation wariness*, which is Model-Collapse rather than a type.

And it has **worked one in detail without ever labelling it**. A separate inquiry described a respected volatile figure as generating *"both at once — deference from fear, and admiration from charm."* That is the Charm + Fear region, characterised in a paragraph and never entered as a row.

**Two things are derivable about the unnamed regions.**

**First, which ones are fragile.** The reason Hope + Fear collapses a gate rather than forming a type is that *this person will benefit me* and *this person will harm me* are contradictory claims about one sender — and contradictory claims about one sender are exactly what Model-Collapse names. By the same test, Charm + Fear is *not* contradictory (high-status and dangerous describes a powerful person coherently) and Fear + Resonance is *not* contradictory (shares my world and can hurt me describes an intimate).

This rule carries two honest limits. It depends on reading Fear as *threat of harm*; under the theory's other definition — *consequences of losing you* — the combination is not contradictory at all but mutually reinforcing, and the rule evaporates. And the corpus's own case was a **stranger**, with no model in which to hold the contradiction; with more information a receiver can form one (the mentor who could also fire you). So the rule is **coherence-stress, not exclusion**, and how much stress a case can absorb depends on a gate rather than on the variables.

**Second, how each region behaves.** The prior inquiry established that persistence is governed by two properties: whether a variable's ground is externally removable, and how abruptly removal happens. Applied per combination rather than per row, these give each region a decay profile. A Charm + Fear attachment has two externally-grounded variables clearing at different rates, so it degrades in two stages — the fear lifts abruptly, the admiration recedes gradually. A Hope + Resonance attachment has one removable ground and one that is not, so it falls to a floor rather than to zero, which is exactly what that row's *"high persistence"* already says.

These profiles are derivable in **shape** and not in magnitude, because which stage dominates depends on weights the theory has not calibrated. They also inherit a limit the prior finding recorded: the Resonance row's *"maintained via shared model"* clause names an active maintenance mechanism that neither parameter derives.

**One instability worth stating.** Signal specificity scales **each variable separately** — the theory's wording is *"each variable's effective magnitude is scaled by the specificity of the signal carrying it."* So a single message can be specific about one variable and generic about another, which shifts which variable dominates without the sender changing anything else. Any assignment of a case to a region is unstable in a dimension the theory already measures.

---

### 7. What this costs, and one correction to a neighbouring finding

**The type dimension loses its appearance of independence.**

The theory presents magnitude and type as two independent output dimensions. Under this reading they are two readings of the same four numbers: magnitude is the sum, type is the mix. That is a real distinction and the word *independent* is doing less work than it appears to.

The dimension is not redundant. The mix predicts persistence behaviour that the sum does not — a Fear-dominant attachment at high magnitude behaves differently over time from a Resonance-dominant one at low magnitude, and only the mix tells you that. **Derived-but-predictive** is the honest description, and it is a real status.

**And a correction to the inquiry immediately before this one.**

That finding argued its two-family result — externally-grounded Charm, Hope and Fear versus internally-maintained Resonance — was confirmed by **two independent derivations**. One was about the variables, from an iteration testing whether the theory needed a deeper substrate. The other was about the trajectories, from the iteration clarifying the output dimensions. Its ground for calling them independent was that they were *"different objects… neither cites the other."*

If the type rows are determined by which variable dominates, they are **not different objects**. They are one object and its behaviour over time. The two-family result stands — it is derivable from either side alone, and nothing here disturbs it. But its support is **internal consistency**, not independent corroboration, and the difference matters for how much weight it can carry.

---

### 8. What is not settled

**Whether Fear means threat-of-harm or cost-of-loss.** The coherence-stress rule in section 6 depends entirely on the first reading. This is the **second consecutive inquiry** in which that unsettled fork has turned out load-bearing on a central result, which makes it a pattern rather than a coincidence. The theory reserves a slot for a Fear sub-taxonomy and has never opened it.

**Whether a type is a cell or a weight vector.** The evidence leans toward vector — the theory's own word is *distribution*, and it made exactly this call one level down when it decided Hope's sub-flavours are *"a basis, not an exclusive partition."* But applying that to an output rather than to a variable's interior is a transfer, and it is held at medium confidence. The difference shows up in how the unnamed regions are described: as missing members, or as unnamed regions of a continuum.

**Whether which region a real case occupies can be determined at all.** The detection layer emits five categorical levels per variable, so ties are routine — and the taxonomy's own mixed row is itself a tie category. Reading the mix requires weights, and the theory's coefficients are on its own pending-calibration list. This is the same blocker the prior inquiry relocated, arriving here from a different direction.

**Whether four is the final variable count.** The theory's spec names three candidate additional modulators, and a separate list of nine adjacent mechanisms carries an explicit clause that some *"could be absorbed as additional modulators in an iteration-4 or iteration-5."* Section 5 makes this less consequential than it looks — the account is a rule rather than a list, so it survives a fifth variable — but the count itself is not closed.

## Reasoning

### What survived, and why

**The two-answer result survived** because it does not require choosing between the user's reading and the theory's. Both readings are legitimate, both have short answers, and one of them makes the premise correct. It was tested against the objection that giving two answers evades the question, and the objection fails: the two readings answer different questions, and the finding says which is which.

**The join reading survived its hardest objection and was strengthened by it.** The objection was that the mixed row breaks the join, since a join key should not have a compound on one side. But that row is the one whose generator side *is* a pair and whose name reads least like the others — the join visibly stretching, which is evidence for the reading rather than against it.

**The projection finding survived in a corrected form.** The version that failed was *the rows are the variables renamed*, and it failed on a direct reading of four rows: every row-name describes a receiver-state or a relationship, while the variables describe believed sender-properties. The version that survived is *identity is determined by the dominant variable*, which accommodates the added content by explaining that it is a function of the variable.

**The resolution-dependence result survived in a narrowed form.** The strong version — that the levels are illusory — loses to the objection that variables and sub-flavours are gated differently and are therefore genuinely different levels. The narrow version — that the *count* is resolution-dependent — holds, and narrowing it stopped it fighting the level structure it appeared to contradict.

### What was killed, and why

**A claim that the forms are "generatable" — killed.** The proposal was that three inputs suffice to generate any form: a resolution, a coherence rule, and the persistence parameters. It fails because the first input is not supplied by the procedure. Resolution is a choice, so the procedure **presupposes** the form-level rather than generating it. What survives is smaller and true: given a chosen resolution, the derived level's *shape* is computable.

**The unconditional coherence rule — killed and replaced.** The rule that contradictory combinations cannot form was tested against the theory's second definition of Fear and does not survive it: under *consequences of losing you*, Hope and Fear are not contradictory but mutually reinforcing. It was further weakened by its own case being a stranger — with model-support the contradiction is holdable. What replaced it is coherence-*stress* conditional on one reading, which is a weaker claim and a truer one.

**The automatic transfer of the corpus's closure statement — killed.** The theory's *"closure-via-combinatorial-structure, not enumerative"* was stated about **seven** belief-categories: four property-beliefs and three stance-beliefs. Forms map onto the four property ones. A closure claim about a set does not automatically hold of a subset, and no argument was given for the restriction. The underlying identity — the four variables **are** the four property-belief categories — survives on the theory's own wording.

**A five-level scheme for the word *form* — killed and replaced by a tighter one.** The proposal was that *form* names members at five architecturally distinct positions. Three of the five turned out not to be levels of *form* at all: signatures and relational descriptions are **descriptions**, and combinations are a **derived** status rather than a coordinate one. What replaced it is one form-level readable at more than one grain, one derived level, and two description levels.

### The correction that ran against this inquiry's own direction

This inquiry's most attractive intermediate result was that the forms are not enumerable but are generatable — a procedure replacing a list. It was killed on its own headline, by the very finding the inquiry had produced two steps earlier: if the count is resolution-dependent, a procedure that takes resolution as an input cannot claim to generate at any resolution.

Keeping the elegant version would have been easy and would have read better. The replacement is narrower and more useful, and it puts the resolution choice where it belongs — in the open, as the finding it is.
