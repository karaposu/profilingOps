### Key insights
- **K1 — the origin's intuition was consequence-shaped from the first minute.** Within one January conversation the Fear glosses ran "power dynamics" → "scared of consequences" → "what they might lose" → "consequences of losing connection"; the frozen definition is loss-shaped; the threat-shaped statement arrived only at iteration-3.4. The fork is two *formalizations* of one intuition, not two intuitions.
- **K2 — the thesis is right about content.** Every Fear-belief has the shape "this person can bring about an outcome I dread." Harm and loss are two *outcome-classes*; the sender is the agent in both. At the level of belief content, the fork merges.
- **K3 — but the two readings really do come apart — in their generators and removal events, not in content.** Threat-of-harm: the sender *acts* (capacity to hurt); the ground lifts when the threat ends; the receiver's counter-move is appeasement while present and exit when possible. Cost-of-loss: the sender *withdraws* (credible exit); the ground lifts when the dependency ends or alternatives appear; the counter-move is securing alternatives. The corpus's "they come apart cleanly" survives as a fork in **mechanism**, relocated from the belief statement to the generator/removal layer.
- **K4 — dependency is what turns fear into an attachment variable at all.** The street stranger who looks at you badly: fear with **no dependency** → you leave — avoidance, zero attachment (the null case; the approach/avoidance overlay). The boss who can fire you: the same dread, but you *cannot costlessly leave the field* → you stay — the Coerced engagement. Fear the *variable* = dread × cannot-leave. The user's "compensability" names the second factor from the receiver's side; the corpus's **outcome-independence** ("resolution doesn't depend on the counterparty's response") names it from the sender's side. Cost-of-loss is therefore not merely one of two Fear-kinds — it is the **admission condition** under which any fear becomes attachment-relevant.
- **K5 — the instrument mismatch dissolves under K4.** The four detection signals are *dependency conduct* — what a receiver who cannot leave does inside the field of a dreaded outcome. They were never measuring "the belief that he can harm me"; they measure "I am staying despite dread." That is why they fire on routine hierarchy (the over-report hazard): every hierarchy is a field one cannot costlessly leave.
- **K6 — "negative" splits in two.** The corpus already separates the belief's **valence** (negative — Pattern A) from the **sign of its contribution** to engagement (positive — Coerced engagement is real). The user's "minus / 1-over-fear" intuition tracks two things the corpus keeps outside the sum: the **avoidance overlay** (fear reduces approach where one *can* leave) and the absent **worth-having axis** (a Fear-dominant attachment is real and the worst kind to have). The corpus killed negative terms because contradictory beliefs about one sender are handled by the *Coherence gate* (Hope+Fear from a stranger → Model-Collapse), not by subtraction.
- **K7 — the magnitude layer the corpus lacks is a receiver-stakes layer.** Fear's size ∝ the *uncompensated* cost of the dreaded outcome in the receiver's full context (the rich employee; Turkey's job market; the habituated). This has no home — and it rhymes with two other receiver-side scalers the corpus already noted without unifying: Charm's **comparison set** (`11` §2, added 2026-08-31) and receiver availability (`50` §1). A possible cross-variable pattern: each variable's belief is scaled by the receiver's *stakes* in its object (Hope × need; Fear × uncompensated loss; Charm × comparison set).
- **K8 — fear of fearing is outside scope, and touches BDA twice.** As a belief about one's own future state it falls under C5 (outside). But (i) it produces **assumed threat** — Fear-beliefs pre-loaded from imagination and category with no sender evidence ("I assume he's aggressive") — the receiver-side analogue of social-proof pre-loading (f does not start at zero); and (ii) it names the **holding-cost** of a Fear-belief: holding "he can harm me" is itself aversive, so receivers avoid the sender to avoid the *feeling* — a route by which fear reduces approach independent of the outcome's probability (the user's "it really makes them not do it").
- **K9 — Fear beside Hope is not Hope's negative.** The corpus keeps Safety-Hope as an active hope (not Fear-absence) and places the loss of what one hopes for as Hope's own removal event. Cost-of-loss Fear is the *anticipation* of that removal held by a dependent receiver — "I stay because losing you would cost me" — which is why under loss the two *reinforce* (the corpus's own statement) and under threat they *contradict* (Model-Collapse). Whether loss-Fear is the risk-side of one expectancy or a second belief is a frontier, with a generate-alone constraint: the secret-holder generates Fear with no Hope at all.

The two are reconcilable: register the frontier field under F1 as sub-questions; record the fixable points as *observations*; leave F1 open.

## Phase 3 — Ambiguity Collapse

#### A1 · "Outcome" — the thesis's sense vs the architecture's "no outcome term"
**Strongest counter-interpretation:** they are the same sense — if fear is a forecast of outcomes, outcomes drive attachment, contradicting "attachment is generated by beliefs about a person, never by results."
**Why it fails (structural):** the architecture's commitment is about *results as evidence-independent causes* (whether the company succeeds); the thesis's "outcome" is the *content of a forecast belief about the sender* ("he can fire me"). A forecast is a belief; results act only through belief updates ("later and elsewhere"). The two senses never meet.
**Confidence:** HIGH. **Resolution:** the thesis's "outcome" = the dreaded consequence the sender can bring about (belief content); the architecture's exclusion stands. **Fixed:** the wording bound. **No longer allowed:** citing the thesis against the no-outcome-term commitment or vice versa. **Depends:** every frontier's wording. **Model change:** none — a collision avoided.

#### A2 · The fork's locus — belief content or mechanism?
**Strongest counter-interpretation:** the corpus is right as written — threat-of-harm and cost-of-loss are different *beliefs* ("he can hurt me" vs "losing him would cost me"), and the user's merge is a loss of information.
**Why it fails (structural):** at content level both beliefs have the form "this person can bring about an outcome I dread"; what differs is *how* (act vs withdraw), *what lifts it* (threat ends vs dependency ends), and *what the receiver does* (appease-then-exit vs secure alternatives) — three mechanism-level differences the corpus's own "come apart cleanly" examples exhibit. The instrument mismatch is the tell: the statement named a generator, the detector named the admission condition, and neither named the content. The counter keeps a point: the two generators *are* different beliefs about the sender's capacity (to hurt / to withdraw) — so "same content" holds at the outcome level and splits at the capacity level.
**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH (structural; the relocation is a reframing the corpus has not adjudicated).
**Resolution:** the fork **relocates**: merged at content (one dreaded outcome), split at generator (capacity to hurt vs credible withdrawal) with distinct removal events and counter-moves. **Fixed:** the three-object structure (content × generator × admission condition). **No longer allowed:** "the fork is about two definitions of the belief." **Depends:** the consumers (anger routing; coherence-stress rule; the Coerced referent) — each re-addressed by generator, not by content. **Model change:** F1's sub-taxonomy target becomes a taxonomy of generators × removal events.

#### A3 · What makes fear an attachment variable at all — dependency as the admission condition
**Strongest counter-interpretation:** threat alone generates attachment — hostages, abusive relationships, the Jobs-class harsh leader — without any "cannot leave" clause; the corpus's Fear passed generate-alone on "threat/dependency cases" as one class.
**Why it fails (structural):** each of those cases *contains* the cannot-leave clause — the hostage literally, the abusive relationship via isolation and trauma bonding (adjacent mechanisms that *manufacture* dependency), the harsh leader via the employee's stakes; and where the clause is absent (the stranger), the corpus itself says the product is avoidance, not attachment (the null case; the overlay). The generate-alone pass was earned by the dependency half; threat without dependency has never generated attachment in any corpus case. The counter keeps a point: dependency can be *manufactured* by the sender (isolation) — which is why it belongs to the honest/manufactured line as well.
**Confidence:** HIGH.
**Resolution:** Fear-as-attachment = dread of a sender-mediated outcome × the receiver's inability to costlessly leave the field (non-compensability); without the second factor, dread yields avoidance. **Fixed:** the admission condition. **No longer allowed:** treating the stranger case as a Fear-attachment case. **Depends:** A4 (magnitude), A5 (reflexive), the readout re-reading. **Model change:** cost-of-loss is promoted from "one of two kinds" to "the condition under which any fear is attachment-relevant" — the largest shift in the model.

#### A4 · Compensability — bearer and role
**Strongest counter-interpretation:** compensability is just Fear's magnitude on the sender's side — how much the sender controls; put it in the formula as Fear's coefficient.
**Why it fails (structural):** the same boss with the same power produces different fear in the rich employee and the poor one — the difference is *receiver-side* (alternatives, habituation, context), exactly the kind of parameter the corpus places outside the formula (receiver availability; the comparison set). Coefficients are context-dependent but they scale *beliefs*, and this scales a belief by the receiver's *stakes*, which is not a belief about the sender. The counter keeps a point: compensability is partly *set by* the sender (secrets held; a monopoly employer) — the sender can raise it (manufacture dependency) or lower it.
**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH.
**Resolution:** compensability is receiver-side stakes — the uncompensated cost of the dreaded outcome in the receiver's full context — acting both as the admission condition (A3) and as Fear's magnitude scaler; it lives outside the formula like receiver availability, with the sender able to move it (honestly by real value; dishonestly by isolation). **Fixed:** bearer = the receiver's situation; role = admission + magnitude. **No longer allowed:** a Fear coefficient inside f; "dependency" as a sender property. **Depends:** the cross-variable pattern (K7) as a frontier. **Model change:** a *receiver-stakes layer* appears as a candidate, outside the formula, touching three variables.

#### A5 · Fear of fearing — inside or outside?
**Strongest counter-interpretation:** it is inside — the fan who dreads the star's disapproval fears *the feeling*, and that fear is about the sender.
**Why it fails (structural):** the feeling is a belief about one's own future state — the receiver-availability precedent puts beliefs about self outside by scope — and it can exist with no sender at all (the swimmer). The counter keeps its point at two joints: (i) *assumed threat* — a Fear-belief about the sender formed from imagination and category with no sender evidence (the belief-frame's pre-loading, receiver-generated); (ii) *holding-cost* — holding a Fear-belief is itself aversive, so the receiver avoids the sender to avoid the feeling — a route by which fear suppresses approach independent of the outcome's likelihood.
**Confidence:** HIGH on the boundary; MEDIUM on the two joints (structural, untested).
**Resolution:** fear of fearing is outside BDA as a belief about self; it touches BDA at assumed threat (pre-loading without evidence) and at holding-cost (avoidance) — two frontiers with the boundary explicit. **Fixed:** the boundary. **No longer allowed:** a reflexive-fear term in the formula. **Depends:** the avoidance overlay (A6). **Model change:** the receiver's imagination becomes an *evidence source* for Fear — unbacked, self-generated.

#### A6 · "Fear is negative" — valence, contribution, operator, placement
**Strongest counter-interpretation:** the user is right — fear should subtract; a feared person is approached less, so attachment falls with fear; the additive-positive treatment is a mistake.
**Why it fails (structural):** Fear-dominant attachments exist and are real (the Coerced engagement: staying, deferring, careful wording — real engagement that *reverses with relief*); the corpus's kill of negative terms rests on a mechanism (contradictory beliefs about one sender collapse the Coherence gate — handled multiplicatively, not by subtraction). What the counter tracks is real but lives elsewhere: (i) the **avoidance overlay** — where the receiver *can* leave, fear reduces approach (the stranger) — kept by the corpus as a behavioural overlay, not a term; (ii) the **worth-having axis** — the corpus has no term ranking an attachment as good to have; the Coerced row is the worst-persisting kind, and the user's "negative" may be that judgment. Neither is a minus in f.
**Confidence:** HIGH that Fear's contribution sign is positive within the corpus; MEDIUM-HIGH on where the intuition lives.
**Resolution:** four separate questions — valence (negative, committed) · contribution sign (positive, committed) · operator (additive, committed; a divisor would revive the killed substrate) · placement of the intuition (the overlay; the absent worth-having axis) — the last two as frontiers. **Fixed:** the decomposition. **No longer allowed:** "1/fear" or "minus fear" inside f without meeting the kill's reasons. **Depends:** A3 (avoidance is what fear does without dependency). **Model change:** the "negative dimension" question becomes "which of four questions is being asked."
