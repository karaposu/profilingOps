### R1 · the stability/completeness axis disambiguation

- **engagement-type:** DEVELOP
- **Move:** build the two-axis distinction into APT as spec content — *stability* (consistency of the person; low end = erratic = Model-Collapse) versus *completeness* (how far the observer's model is built; high end = solved = pursuit ends).
- **Lands:** the dating observation is accommodated without amending Coherence, and the word "predictable" stops carrying two meanings inside the theory.
- **Touches:** Coherence's Stability aspect (the axis being protected) · the Model-Collapse failure signature (whose scope the disambiguation bounds) · the dating-lore anomaly (the case being resolved).
- **WHY:** the two axes are orthogonal and were fused by one English word — separating them is what lets the goal's verdict read *absorbed* rather than *amended*, which is the strongest verdict available and the one the corpus's bootstrap phase favors.
- **Priority:** HIGH · **Confidence:** HIGH · **Essentiality:** core
- **Guidance Mode:** compact
  - "state it as two named axes before any prose" *(bc the confusion is lexical, and naming is the whole repair)*
  - "keep the observation affirmed" *(bc the goal's NOT-list forecloses resolutions that deny it)*
  - **Meaning-gaps:**
    - whether completeness is one axis or two (breadth of the model vs depth) — **mid** — a two-part axis changes what "solved" means and whether saturation is ever actually reached
    - whether the axes are truly independent or weakly coupled (an erratic person may also be unsolvable) — **mid** — coupling would weaken the orthogonality claim the repair rests on
- **Depth-link:** none
