## Question

Attachment & Presentation Theory (referred to below as "the theory") models how one person becomes attached to another. It has two halves: four variables that generate attachment by addition — charm, hope, fear, resonance — and three modulators that gate the result by multiplication. Because the modulators multiply, any one of them falling to zero collapses attachment no matter how strong the variables are.

**Coherence** is one of those three modulators. Its belief statement is *"I can form a stable model of this person,"* and its failure is called **Model-Collapse** — the observer gives up trying to figure the person out, and attachment cannot consolidate.

The user raised two observations that seemed to contradict this.

**The first — the dating observation.** Common dating advice says you should not become predictable: the moment you are "solved" as a person, you become boring and the other person stops chasing you. The user stated plainly that they know this observation to be correct. But if being figured out ends the pursuit, and Coherence says being figure-out-able is what *enables* attachment, the theory appears to have the sign backwards.

**The second — the anger observation.** People with anger problems are often treated with more deference than calm people. The user asked whether a person with no Coherence can therefore find attachment by provoking fear, and stabilise an attachment that way.

The user also offered their own candidate explanation — that Model-Collapse does not fire when the model *is* unpredictability — and asked whether there might instead be "an elephant in the room," some other dimension the theory is missing.

**The goal** was a diagnostic analysis ending in an architectural verdict: is each observation absorbed by the theory, does the theory need amending, extending, or is it inadequate at the level of its basic categories? The analysis was bounded to Coherence and its relations to the other modulators and to the variables — not a whole-theory rewrite, and not an empirical study. One thing was ruled out from the start: no answer was allowed to work by denying that the dating observation is true.

## Finding Summary

- **The verdict is *absorbed*.** Coherence's mechanism survives both observations with no amendment. No new modulator, no new variable, no change to the formula.

- **The first observation was never about Coherence.** The word "predictable" carries two different meanings, and the two observations sit on two different axes. Coherence is about *consistency* — is this person the same person across time and situations. The dating advice is about *completeness* — have I finished figuring this person out. Those are independent, and the apparent contradiction was a collision between two senses of one English word.

- **The user's observation is correct; the user's proposed explanation is not.** The suggestion that Model-Collapse doesn't fire when the model *is* unpredictability is refuted by the theory's own published text, which lists *"They're different every time I see them"* as a Model-Collapse report. This matters because nobody advises a partner to become unreliable — the advice is to avoid being *exhausted*, which is the other axis entirely.

- **The second observation is already answered in the theory's existing text**, and the answer to the user's literal question is **no**. A person with genuinely no Coherence cannot stabilise attachment through fear, because the modulators multiply: Coherence at zero zeroes the product regardless of how much fear is present.

- **What such a person gets instead is compliance, not attachment.** The theory's existing taxonomy already names this: fear-dominant engagement is typed *Coerced*, and it *"reverses (relief, not attachment) on threat removal."* Deference that evaporates the moment the threat lifts was never attachment.

- **The reason both observations looked like problems is a layer the theory has but never named.** The theory computes how strong an attachment is and what kind it is, then stops. It never says what each kind *looks like from outside*. Three states get confused as a result: attachment (persists when satisfied), pursuit (ends when its gap closes), and compliance (reverses when the threat lifts).

- **That layer is not missing — it is present, provisional, and one table column wide.** The 3.2.1 iteration ships a "Persistence under change" column with five rows, and states outright that engagement type predicts behavioral trajectory. The work is to promote and name it, which is far cheaper than building it.

- **The "elephant" is real and is now placed.** Model-completeness is a genuine factor, and it is neither a variable nor a modulator. It is a **cross-variable epistemic modifier** — it describes how completely the observer knows the *other* terms, rather than being a term itself. Two attempts to file it in existing categories were tested and both failed on those categories' own admission tests.

- **Coherence needs one sentence rewritten, not a mechanism repair.** *"I can form a stable model"* should read as *reliable*, never as *finished*.

- **The theory's basic categories are not inadequate.** The standing question of whether attachment should be re-grounded in attention (an open item the corpus tracks) was reviewed and **not triggered** — the sixth such review.

## Finding

### Why this question mattered

The theory has thirteen iterations behind it and is used to interpret real conversations. When someone reports a pattern from ordinary life that seems to run against the theory, there are only a few honest outcomes: the theory already covers it, the theory needs a repair, the theory needs a new part, or the theory's basic categories are wrong. Deciding which one — with reasons — is the whole job here. And the user has repeatedly invited the strongest of those verdicts against their own theory, so the analysis was run without protecting it.

### 1. The dating observation: one word, two axes

The dating advice and Coherence are both stated using the word "predictable," and that is the entire source of the apparent contradiction. They are talking about different things.

**Coherence is about consistency.** Its question is: *is this the same person across time and across situations?* Its low end is the erratic person — someone whose behaviour has no through-line. The theory's published text is explicit that this is where Coherence fails. The Model-Collapse failure signature lists the observer's reports verbatim as *"I can't figure out who they really are" / "They're different every time I see them" / "Something doesn't add up."* The argument that admitted Coherence to the modulator set in the first place reads: *"Observer can't form stable mental model of erratic person → can't attach."*

**The dating advice is about completeness.** Its question is: *have I finished figuring this person out?* Its problematic end is the opposite one — the fully mapped person, the one with nothing left to discover.

These two axes are independent, and the theory's own material already proves it. A person can be perfectly consistent and still inexhaustible — the deep teacher, whose behaviour is entirely reliable and who nonetheless never runs out of depth. Attachment there is not merely preserved but heightened. If consistency and exhaustibility were the same axis, that case could not exist.

So the dating observation is true, and it was never a counter-example. It reports a fact about the completeness axis, and Coherence lives on the consistency axis.

### 2. Why the user's proposed explanation does not hold

The user suggested that Model-Collapse does not fire when the model *is* unpredictability — that "they are erratic" is itself a stable second-order model. It is a reasonable move, and this inquiry's own mid-stage analysis initially accepted it, citing a line from the 3.2 iteration's working notes: *"a stable hypocrite (consistently hypocritical) has stability but not alignment."* The reasoning was that consistency *of* a trait satisfies Coherence, so consistent unpredictability should too.

That reasoning does not survive contact with the source. The quoted sentence continues, in the same passage: *"A fluctuating sincere person has alignment in the moment but **not stability**."* The un-quoted second half adjudicates exactly the case at issue, and adjudicates it the other way. The hypocrite is *behaviourally predictable and internally misaligned* — the opposite corner of the grid from the erratic person. The precedent does not transfer.

Four separate places in the theory's material agree: erratic behaviour is a Stability failure, not a stable second-order model.

**This does not weaken the answer — it improves it.** The observation the user reported stands, because nobody advises a partner to become unreliable. The advice is to avoid being *finished*. Once the two axes are separated, no precedent transfer is needed at all, and the resolution rests on the theory's own published distinction rather than on a borrowed analogy.

### 3. The anger observation: already answered, and the answer is no

The user asked whether a person with no Coherence can find attachment through fear and stabilise it there.

**The direct answer is no**, and it follows from the theory's shape rather than from any new reasoning. The modulators multiply. If Coherence is genuinely absent, its term goes to zero, and a product with a zero in it is zero — no quantity of fear rescues it. Fear is on the additive side of the formula; it cannot compensate for a collapsed gate. This is the same mechanism that makes any modulator failure fatal, and it is why the modulators were separated from the variables in the first place.

**What the person actually gets is named in the theory already.** The 3.2.1 iteration's engagement-type taxonomy has a row for exactly this case: fear-dominant engagement is typed **Coerced**, with the behaviour *"Reverses (relief, not attachment) on threat removal."* Deference that vanishes the instant the threat lifts is not attachment that was won; it is compliance that was extracted.

**There is also a labelling problem worth separating out.** Most people with anger spikes are not incoherent. Someone who reliably explodes under a known set of conditions is *predictable* — the theory's own dissociation test names this case directly: *"a consistent hothead is Coherent but dysregulated."* That person's problem lives in Emotional Composure, the third modulator, not in Coherence. So the premise embedded in the user's question — "a person with no coherence" — usually does not describe the person the observation is about.

That leaves three different routes through the theory for one real-world phenomenon, and reconciling them is genuinely unfinished. It is recorded below as an open item.

### 4. The layer the theory has but never named

Both observations looked like contradictions for the same underlying reason: the theory computes an attachment and then stops. It produces *how strong* the attachment is and *what kind* it is, and it never says what each kind looks like from the outside. Without that, three different states are read as the same thing.

- **Attachment** persists even when it is being satisfied.
- **Pursuit** ends when the gap driving it closes. This is what the dating observation is about — the chase stops because the chase is finished, not because the attachment died.
- **Compliance** reverses when the threat lifts. This is what the anger observation is about.

Naming these three is what dissolves both observations at once.

**The important part is that this layer is not missing.** The 3.2.1 iteration already ships a table with a *"Persistence under change"* column, and it already contains the behavioural predictions:

| Dominant variable | Engagement type | Persistence under change |
|---|---|---|
| Charm-dominant | Status / admiration | Weakens as access to the charm source recedes |
| Hope-dominant | Transactional | Dissolves when exchange ends |
| Fear-dominant | Coerced | Reverses (relief, not attachment) on threat removal |
| Resonance-dominant | Bonded / deep | Persists through absence; maintained via shared model |
| Hope + Resonance | Collaborative | High persistence; both transacts and connects |

That same section states outright: *"TYPE predicts behavioral trajectory; MAGNITUDE predicts current intensity."* The claim is made; the column supplies it; nothing ever names it as a layer or applies it to a case.

**Two honest qualifications travel with the promotion.**

The table carries its own flag — *"Taxonomy is provisional and illustrative. Comprehensive TYPE classification pending empirical work."* Promoting the structure to a named layer must not silently promote the confidence of its rows. Some rows are already questionable: a mentor admired at a distance for years contradicts the first row.

And the layer is currently nameable but not usable. Nothing specifies how an analyst decides, looking at a real conversation, which of the three states they are seeing. That determination mechanism is the single largest piece of unfinished work this finding leaves behind.

### 5. Where the "elephant" belongs

The user asked whether there is some other dimension the theory is missing. There is a real factor, and it is neither of the things one would first reach for.

The factor is **model-completeness** — how much of a person the observer has finished figuring out. It appeared once before, in the original conversation that started this line of work, under the name "information asymmetry," and was dropped without ever being written into a finding.

Two placements were tested, and both were rejected on the theory's own admission tests.

**It is not a modulator.** The modulator set has eight entry criteria, and two of them kill this. The first is that a modulator must be able to generate attachment on its own — and a masked stranger, who is maximally incomplete, produces nothing. The second is that modulators must behave smoothly across their whole range; completeness saturating does not zero attachment, it only ends the chase. This is the same pair of criteria that excluded Vitality (physical energy and aliveness) from the modulator set, and those criteria were written to bite.

**It is not a sub-flavour of Hope either**, although this was the most promising candidate the analysis produced and it was pursued seriously. The theory's 3.4.1 iteration divides Hope into seven sub-flavours and documents a condition under which that list reopens: a hope-instance whose *attachment-generating* belief structure will not break down into the seven. "I hope there is more of you to find" does not break down into the seven — but it fails the other half of the test, and it fails on a precedent the same iteration set. That iteration already rejected a proposed eighth sub-flavour, Pleasure-Hope, on the grounds that it is *"a cross-variable affective specification, not a sub-flavor; pleasure can be associated with H_e, Charm, or Resonance depending on what specifically is being claimed."* Discovery behaves identically. Discovery of *what*? Their competence resolves to charm; their shared values to resonance; their regard for you to validation-hope. "More to find" names no outcome of its own — it describes a property of your knowledge about the other terms.

**That failure is the answer.** Model-completeness is a **cross-variable epistemic modifier**: it modifies how completely the observer knows the other terms, rather than being a term itself. That is a genuinely different kind of thing from the four the theory currently recognises — variable, sub-flavour, modulator, and documented degradation factor — and it explains cleanly why every attempt to file it in an existing category failed.

It should be documented alongside Vitality, in the space the theory reserves for factors that are real and consequential but do not qualify as modulators — with the difference that this one governs pursuit rather than degrading signal.

### 6. Coherence's one-sentence repair

The belief statement *"I can form a stable model of this person"* is doing two jobs. Read as *reliable*, it is exactly right and is what Coherence means. Read as *finished*, it drags in the completeness axis and produces the apparent contradiction this whole inquiry was about.

The repair is to the wording only. Both aspects — Stability and Alignment — survive unchanged, as does the Model-Collapse signature. Nothing in the detection catalog is affected.

### 7. Verdict on the theory's basic categories

The corpus tracks a standing question about whether attachment should be re-grounded in attention as its underlying substrate, with documented conditions for reopening it. Those conditions were reviewed here.

**Not triggered** — the sixth review, and the sixth time it has not fired. This inquiry adds no modulator, and the distinctions it introduces (three engagement states, one new filing category) operate above the variable-and-modulator ontology rather than replacing it.

One caveat is worth recording honestly. During idea-generation, one line of reasoning arrived at attention-allocation-over-time from an entirely different direction, and suggested the substrate question might dissolve into a question about *dynamics* rather than substrate. That is a reframe of the question, not a trigger of it, and it is logged as an open frontier.

## Reasoning

### What survived, and why

**The two-axis separation survived** because it explains both halves of the first observation at once, requires no new machinery, and matches how this corpus has resolved its hardest questions before — the 3.4 iteration resolved its central ambiguity by disambiguating *attachment is a belief* from *attachment is generated by beliefs*, and this inquiry's own sensemaking stage found four separate referents circulating for the word "model." Term disambiguation is this project's established resolution pattern, not an ad-hoc move.

**Promoting the existing persistence column survived** because the column was verified to exist with all five rows, and because it makes the answer cheaper: promotion rather than construction. It carries three caveats — the provisional flag must travel with it, the rows are untested, and the criteria for applying it do not yet exist.

**Importing the attachment-behaviour versus attachment-bond distinction survived** because the theory already cites the same source as an accepted vocabulary-only anchor in two separate iterations, under an explicit non-commitment to that tradition's full apparatus. Importing a distinction as vocabulary is the established pattern here, not a new move. It carries one scoping caveat: take the behaviour-versus-bond pair only, and do not re-import working-models, which is a separate referent already flagged as out of frame.

**The observer-task framing survived** — that consistency answers *"can I predict them"* while completeness answers *"should I keep looking"* — because it supplies the mechanism behind the lexical collision rather than merely restating it.

### What was killed, and why

**The eighth Hope sub-flavour (Discovery-Hope) — killed.** This was the strongest candidate the idea-generation stage produced, and it was pursued to the point of checking the reopening condition the relevant iteration documents for its own taxonomy. It passed one half of that condition and failed the other, on a precedent the same iteration set when it rejected Pleasure-Hope as *"a cross-variable affective specification, not a sub-flavor."* Discovery has the identical shape: it names no outcome of its own and resolves into charm, resonance, or validation-hope depending on what is being discovered. Filed as a sub-flavour it would corrupt a classification whose members are outcome-kinds with one that is a knowledge-state. The seed extracted from this kill is better than the candidate was — it is what identified the fifth architectural category.

**The user's proposed explanation — killed**, on four verbatim citations from the theory's own material, detailed in section 2 above. The observation it was offered to explain stands.

**Identifying attachment with the observer's mental model — killed.** One line of reasoning, pushed to its root, arrived at *attachment simply is a sufficiently invested model of someone*, which derives pursuit-as-model-construction elegantly. It contradicts the 3.4 iteration's deliberate choice between *attachment is a belief* and *attachment is generated by beliefs*. That choice was made knowingly, so the proposal was dropped and the surviving half kept — that pursuit tracks model-construction, weakened from an identity claim to a co-variation because someone can pursue a person they already understand completely, out of desire rather than curiosity.

**Compliance as the same thing as the supplication pole — killed.** Proposed as a way to need one fewer new concept. The supplication pole describes the *sender's* attention-direction; compliance describes the *receiver's* behaviour under threat. Different parties, different objects, and a person complying under threat need not be seeking approval at all. The kill was re-tested at the critique stage and upheld.

**A decay-modulator class — killed**, on the Vitality precedent's published wording about unprincipled criterion-loosening, with the theory's current absence of calibration data making the burden unmeetable. Deferred with a revival condition rather than closed.

**Phase-scoping the dating claim to courtship — demoted rather than killed.** Once the two-axis separation was in hand, this became largely redundant: the claim is not phase-scoped, it is axis-confused. Phase still does a smaller job — completeness saturates faster early on — so it survives as a timing note rather than a standalone explanation.

### The contradiction that had to be reconciled across stages
