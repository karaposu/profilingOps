## Phase 4 *(protocol Phase 3)* — Ambiguity Collapse

### Ambiguity A1 — Which sense of *artifact* is meant?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** the derivative sense, obviously. The sentence sets *a property of the person* against *an artifact of the attention-species* as two alternatives of the same grammatical kind, and the natural reading of the second is *a real thing that comes from the species* — not *an illusion*.

**Why the counter only partly holds:** the grammar does favour it, and **the corpus's usage runs entirely the other way**. Five findings use *artifact of X* to mean *a spurious appearance produced by X*, and none uses it derivatively. So the statement is either coining a sense or importing an ordinary-English one the corpus does not share.

**What decides it is that the two senses converge here.** Under the debunking sense: *the thing is not really there; it looks like a property because of how the species were defined.* Under the derivative sense: *the thing is real but predicated on something else.* **Both are refuted by the same evidence** — the two-way dissociation shows the thing is real (it survives the species varying) and not predicated on the species (it varies with the species held fixed). So the answer does not turn on the choice.

**Confidence: HIGH** on the convergence; **MEDIUM** on which sense was intended.

**Resolution:** state both senses, note the corpus's own is the debunking one, and answer in a way that holds under either. **Fixed:** the verdict is sense-independent. **No longer allowed:** using *artifact* in the finding without saying which sense.

### Ambiguity A2 — Is the subject a species artifact?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** yes, in the only sense that matters. Every species is defined as *attention on [object]*, so the object is fixed by the species — and something derived from the object is thereby derived from the species. The distinction is a technicality.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** **run the corpus's own test.** A two-way dissociation needs a case with species-and-not-outcome and a case with outcome-and-not-species. Both exist. *Species without outcome:* the leadership finding's own qualification — *"a topic exhausted for an expert is not exhausted for a novice"* — means the **same species with the same object** yields peer for one observer and follower for another. The species is held fixed and the outcome varies. *Outcome without species:* followership arises from Dialogue-Focus, from Mission-Focus, and from self-as-object. Both legs pass, which is exactly what the corpus accepted as establishing independence for Coherence, Emotional Composure and Resonance.

And the counter's premise is false in one case the corpus records: **Scenario 4 assigns one teacher to two species**, whose objects sit on opposite sides of the criterion. So the object is *not* fixed by the species even in the corpus's own material.

**Confidence: HIGH.**

**Resolution:** **not a species artifact.** The bearer is **object × observer**; the species is a **proxy** that works because species are named by their objects and fails where a species name covers two objects. **Fixed:** the disjunction's second horn is refused by the corpus's own test. **No longer allowed:** treating species and object as interchangeable. **Depends on this:** the whole `I1` verdict and the sorting in `I2`.

### Ambiguity A3 — Then is it a property of the person?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** yes, by elimination — if it is not the species, and the corpus admits *self-as-object* as a real case, then the person-located reading is back.

**Why the counter fails, and what survives:** the self-as-object case is not the person being the bearer; it is **the person's own becoming being the object**, and the finding is explicit that the distinction holds on generativity — *"a project rather than a position."* A person whose object is their own **position** is solved quickly; a person whose object is their own **project** is not. Same person, different object, opposite outcome. That is the object doing the work, not the person.

**But the counter lands one real point.** *"Definable on its own"* fails: the thing cannot be defined without reference to an object and an observer, which is what a two-place relation means. So the first horn's own stated criterion is not met, and that is a cleaner refutation than elimination.

**Confidence: HIGH.**

**Resolution:** **not a property of the person either** — and refused on the statement's own criterion rather than by elimination. **Fixed:** both horns fail. **What changed:** the statement's first criterion becomes the argument rather than a decoration.

### Ambiguity A4 — Does the thing port from the teacher to the leader?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** yes, and the leadership finding shows it — it runs both cases through one criterion and they both pass.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** it passes both cases **after** a choice the finding did not declare. Scenario 4 — the teacher case's own source — assigns the beloved teacher to *"Dialogue-Focus … **or Caregiver-Focus**"*. The leadership finding wrote *"a teacher attending to a **shared inquiry**"*, which is Dialogue-Focus's object. Under Caregiver-Focus the teacher's object **is the student**, the second clause fails, and the criterion's own worked example says that configuration yields **devotion without followership** — the opposite of the leader.

So portability holds **on one branch of an *or* the corpus never resolved**, and fails on the other.

**Confidence: HIGH** on the conditionality; the branch itself is not adjudicated here — Scenario 4 states both and nothing settles it.

**Resolution:** the portability claim is **conditional and its condition is undeclared**. **Fixed:** the statement's second criterion cannot be used as given. **No longer allowed:** citing teacher-to-leader transfer without naming the species assignment it rests on. **What changed:** a decorative criterion becomes a live test with a known failing configuration.

### Ambiguity A5 — What is `I2` actually asking for?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** a list. *"What other artifacts exists?"* asks for members, and answering with a test instead is changing the question.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** the class has **no first member**. Nothing in the corpus has ever been called an artifact of a species, so *other* has no anchor and a list would have nothing to be *other than*. And the corpus's own method for deciding membership is the two-way dissociation, which has been run three times and returned independent every time — so the class has no members **by the corpus's own test**, not merely by oversight.

What exists instead is a **pool** of roughly two dozen per-species attributions across four unconnected sources. A test applied to that pool produces a list; a list without the test could not be checked and would be exactly the kind of claim this corpus keeps having to correct.

**Confidence: HIGH.**

**Resolution:** `I2` is answered by **supplying the test and sorting the pool with it**, and the sorting is the list. **Fixed:** criterion before enumeration. **No longer allowed:** presenting a list whose membership rule is implicit.

### Ambiguity A6 — Specific-vs-pattern *(required cue)*

**The key insight built from few cases:** KI7 / KI8 — *the bearer is object × observer, and the species is a proxy* — built from **one** worked candidate (peer/follower) plus one corroborating derivation (Caregiver→Charm).

**Strongest counter:** one candidate is one candidate. Peer/follower may be unusually object-dependent because the leadership finding derived it from the object in the first place — of course it dissociates from the species; it was *built* that way. A conclusion about the whole pool cannot rest on the one member whose object-dependence was already established.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** it would hold if peer/follower were the only case, and it is not. **Two independent items sort the same way from unrelated starting points.** The Caregiver→Charm line comes from an **April specification table** written months before the object-criterion existed, and its derivation turns on a word — *"**others** admire the caregiving"* — that the table's own author put there without commentary. That is not a case built to be object-dependent; it is a case that turns out to be, and whose oddity the object reading explains. And **two further items sort onto the *level* axis** (character, the oscillation readings) rather than onto the object, which is what a real sorting looks like and what a foregone conclusion does not.

**Confidence: MEDIUM-to-HIGH.** The pattern generalises across four items on three axes; it has not been run over the whole pool, and the finding must say so.

**Resolution:** the sorting generalises and is **not complete**. **Fixed:** the method; **not fixed:** the full result. **What changed:** the answer's honest form is *the test, the axes, and a worked sample* rather than a finished enumeration.

### Ambiguity A7 — Load-bearing concept test: *predication axis* and *species-as-proxy*

*(Required — both are analysis coinages and both are load-bearing on the verdict.)*

**Strongest counter:** neologisms. The corpus says *species* and *level* and now *object*; adding *predication axis* on top is apparatus about apparatus.

**Why the counter partly holds:**
- **Proxy-vs-structural — survives.** *Species-as-proxy* names a real relation the corpus demonstrates: the species tracks the object because species are **named by** their objects, and the tracking fails at Scenario 4 where one name covers two objects. That is a structural fact with a worked failure case, not an incidental correlation.
- **Discoverability — passes, and this is the notable one.** The determination is one question — *hold the species fixed and vary this; hold this fixed and vary the species* — and it is **the corpus's own test**, already run three times. Nothing new has to be specified.
- **User-language alignment — weak, and the mitigation is available.** The user said *artifact of the attention-species*. The finding should keep *species*, *level* and *object* as the three names — all corpus words — and use *axis* only where the three need a collective noun.

**Confidence: HIGH** on the distinction; **MEDIUM** on the labels.

**Resolution:** keep the three corpus names, demote the coinages to connective tissue. **Fixed:** the finding's vocabulary is the corpus's.

### Ambiguity A8 — Is the species column empty?

*(Required by Risk's second concern — an emptiness that falls out of three cases is not a result.)*

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** no. Something must be a species artifact, or the species taxonomy would carry no predictive weight at all — and it plainly does; the corpus uses it to explain a dozen cases.

**Why the counter fails, and what it correctly protects:** the taxonomy carries weight **as a proxy**, which is exactly what KI8 says. Predictive weight and predication are different things: a proxy predicts well and is not what the prediction is about. So the taxonomy's usefulness is untouched by the species column being thin.

**But the counter protects something real, and it is not defeated.** The sorting has been run over four items. Whether *anything* is predicated on the species and not on the object-it-names is genuinely open, and there is one shape that would qualify: something predicated on **which object-kind** rather than on **the object's properties** — for instance, whether an object is *shareable*, which the corpus uses to separate movements from devoted individuals and which looks more like a species-level fact than an object-level one.

**Confidence: LOW on emptiness; HIGH on the distinction that makes emptiness testable.**

**Resolution:** **do not claim the species column is empty.** Claim what was tested: four items, none of them species-predicated, and one named shape (*shareability*) that might be. **Fixed:** the finding reports a sample, not a census. **No longer allowed:** the sentence *nothing is a species artifact*.

### SV6 — Stabilized Model

**Both horns fail, and each fails on one of the statement's own criteria.**

**It is not a property of the person, because it is not *definable on its own*.** The corpus's settled bearer takes two arguments: *"generativity is a relation between object and observer, usually dominated by the object."* A thing that needs an object and an observer to be specified is not standalone-definable, and the statement's first criterion is what rules it out — cleaner than ruling it out by elimination.

**It is not an artifact of the attention-species, because it dissociates from the species in both directions** — which is the corpus's own test for exactly this question, the one it ran on Coherence, on Emotional Composure and on Resonance. *Species held fixed, outcome varies:* the same species with the same object yields a **peer** for an expert and a **follower** for a novice, by the leadership finding's own qualification that *"a topic exhausted for an expert is not exhausted for a novice."* *Outcome held fixed, species varies:* followership arises from Mission-Focus, from Dialogue-Focus, and from self-as-object alike.

**What it is predicated on is `object × observer`, and the species is a proxy.** The proxy is a good one and it is not an identity: every species is defined as *attention on [X]*, so the species name **tracks** the object — and the tracking fails wherever one name covers two objects.

**The corpus records exactly one such failure, and it is the statement's own teacher.** Scenario 4 assigns the beloved teacher to *"**Dialogue-Focus** (attention on the shared inquiry) **or Caregiver-Focus** (attention on student's development)"*, as though the choice did not matter. Under the two-clause criterion it matters completely: a shared inquiry lies **outside** the receiver; a student's development **is** the receiver — and that second clause is exactly what separates followership from devotion-without-followership.

**Which makes the statement's portability claim conditional, on a condition nobody declared.** The leadership finding wrote *"a teacher attending to a shared inquiry"* — Dialogue-Focus's object, verbatim — taking one branch of Scenario 4's *"or"* without noting the fork. On that branch the thing ports from the teacher to the leader. On the other branch the two cases sit on opposite sides of the criterion and nothing ports.

**On the word *artifact*, the verdict is sense-independent.** The corpus's only settled theory sense is the **debunking** one — *"an artifact of how we defined the variables"*, *"an artifact of the corpus having no routing apparatus"* — where an artifact is a spurious appearance produced by the method. The statement's construction suggests the **derivative** sense: real, but not independent. The evidence refutes both readings of the second horn at once: the two-way dissociation shows the thing is **real** (it survives the species varying) and **not species-predicated** (it varies with the species held fixed).

**For the second question, the class has no first member.** Nothing anywhere in the corpus has been called an artifact of a species, so *"what **other** artifacts exists?"* has no anchor for its *other*. And the class is empty by the corpus's **own method**, not by oversight: the two-way dissociation has been run three times — Coherence, Emotional Composure, Resonance — and returned *independent* every time.

**What does exist is a pool and a test.** Roughly two dozen per-species attributions sit across **four sources that no document connects**: the specification's two titled sections (`Species × Attachment-Variable Amplification` and `Oscillation Dynamics`), the fifteen proof scenarios, iteration-3's definitions and correlates, and the leadership finding's object-derived consequences. Two of those four are April specification files whose content the findings chain has almost entirely not carried.

**Sorted with the test, four worked items fall on two axes and none on the species.**

- **Object axis — peer versus follower generation.** Shown above.
- **Object axis — the specification's oddest line, and the derivation explains its odd word.** *"Caregiver-Focus amplifies Charm via admiration (**others** admire the caregiving)."* The admirer is a **third party**. For a third party the caregiver's object — the cared-for person — lies **outside them** and is **generative**, so it passes both clauses. For the cared-for person the object **is** them, so it fails the second. One species, two receiver positions, two outcomes, both derived. The table's own *"others"* is the tell, and the table never remarks on it.
- **Level axis — character.** *"Over time, the observed Self-Positioning **bearing** becomes part of what others call 'character'."* The bearing is level-and-display sustained over time; character-attribution occurs across species and fails within a species on short exposure.
- **Level axis — the oscillation readings.** *That* a misreading occurs is a function of sustained purity — level and duration. *Which* misreading occurs — *cold / indifferent* versus *dependent / sycophantic* — is a function of the object.

**This is a sample, not a census, and the finding must say so.** Four items on three axes is enough to establish the method and not enough to claim the species column is empty. One shape would qualify if anything does: something predicated on **which kind of object** rather than on the object's properties — *shareability*, for instance, which the corpus uses to separate movements from devoted individuals and which looks species-level rather than object-level.

**Two things the corpus already has and has not used.** It wrote this question once as a trigger — Cluster 4's third reopening condition, *"adjacent mechanisms reveal themselves to be attention-pattern variants rather than independent phenomena"*, about a named nine-item list, never fired. And it carries the same wrong claim in two places — *Mission-Focus amplifies Hope* in the amplification table and in Scenario 9 — corrected once, in a document that cited the scenario and never touched the table.
