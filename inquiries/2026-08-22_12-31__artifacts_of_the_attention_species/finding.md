## Question

From the inquiry's framing document, two questions arrived in one statement:

> *"a property of the person — definable on its own, portable from the teacher to the leader — or an artifact of the attention-species? what other artifacts exists? lets dive deep into this one now"*

The first question poses a disjunction — notably, without ever naming its subject. It supplies two criteria for the first horn (*definable on its own*; *portable from the teacher to the leader*) and a second horn of a different grammatical kind (not a different bearer but a by-product relation). The second question asks for the remaining members of a class, where the word *other* presupposes a first member the sentence does not name, and *artifacts* is used without a definition.

**The goal:** for the first question, a classification verdict — which requires first establishing what is being classified; for the second, an enumeration — which requires first establishing what the class is and whether it has any members. Bounded to the two horns, the two named criteria, the teacher and leader cases, "the attention-species," and "the person." The declared layer is **Meaning** (what kind of thing this is, and what makes something a member of the class); applying the membership test case by case is scheduled as the next inquiry's Process work.

Background for a reader new to this material: APT (Attachment & Presentation Theory) models how one person becomes attached to another — four belief-variables that add (Charm, Hope, Fear, Resonance) gated by three multiplicative modulators. One modulator, Self-Positioning, tracks *non-extractive attention* and has a taxonomy of **species** — Self-Focus, Caregiver-Focus, Mission-Focus, Task-Focus, Dialogue-Focus, Transcendent-Focus — each defined as *attention on [some object]*. The "teacher" and "leader" in the user's question are the corpus's own cases: the deep teacher (inexhaustible, from the coherence inquiry) and the mission-driven leader (from the leadership inquiry).

## Finding Summary

- **Both horns of the disjunction fail, and each fails on one of the statement's own criteria.** The thing is not *a property of the person*, because it is not *definable on its own* — the corpus's settled bearer takes two arguments. And it is not *an artifact of the attention-species*, because it dissociates from the species in both directions — which is the corpus's own membership test for exactly this kind of question.

- **What it is predicated on is the pair: attention-object × observer.** The object supplies most of the variance; the observer supplies the rest. The species works as a **proxy** — a good one — because every species is *named after* its object, and the proxy fails exactly where one species name covers two objects.

- **The corpus records exactly one such naming failure, and it is the statement's own teacher.** The teacher scenario in the April spec assigns beloved teachers to *"Dialogue-Focus … or Caregiver-Focus"* — two species whose objects sit on opposite sides of the criterion that separates followership from devotion. So the statement's portability premise (*portable from the teacher to the leader*) is **conditional on a choice no document has ever made**.

- **The scenario's own next sentence explains why its "or" was harmless at home**: *"Both are non-extractive attention species — the genus property holds."* For the claim that scenario was making (devotion via the modulator), the species choice genuinely did not matter — the operative property belongs to the **genus**, non-extractive attention as such. The choice matters completely for the object-level question this inquiry asks. Two documents, two predication levels, each correctly indifferent or sensitive at its own level — a level-shift, not a contradiction.

- **The verdict on the first question is independent of which sense of "artifact" was meant.** The corpus's settled sense is the debunking one (a spurious appearance produced by the method — also the standard scientific sense); the statement's construction suggests a derivative sense (real, but not independent). The two-way dissociation refutes both readings of the second horn at once: the thing is *real* (it survives the species varying) and *not species-predicated* (it varies with the species held fixed).

- **The second question's answer forks by that same sense — and the two answers converge on the same items.** Under the derivative sense, the class has **no first member**: nothing in the corpus has ever been called an artifact of a species, and the corpus's own test has returned "independent" every time it has run. Under the debunking sense, the class **has members already**: claims whose species-level *form* is the spurious part — object- or genus-level facts written at the species address — and the corpus has already caught at least two. The items the debunking sense names are exactly the items the derivative-sense sorting re-addresses.

- **What exists today is a pool and a test, and a four-item worked sample.** Roughly two dozen per-species attributions sit across four sources no document connects. Sorted with the test, the sample lands: two items on the **object** axis, two on the **level** axis, two on the **genus** axis, none on the species axis — a sample, not a census, and the finding does not claim the species column is empty. One named candidate — **shareability** — could yet put a member there.

- **The census — the full case-by-case sort — is specified but deliberately not run.** It belongs to the next inquiry (the Process layer). Its worklist, instruments, and stakes are stated below; one of its two stakes is a reopening trigger the corpus wrote itself and has never fired.

## Finding

### 1. Why this question mattered, and what made it hard

The leadership inquiry (`inquiries/2026-08-21_18-37__leadership_and_inexhaustibility/finding.md`) did something the corpus had never done before: it took a property everyone had located in a person — inexhaustibility, the quality of never being "solved" — and relocated it to what the person's attention is on. The user's question probes that move at its joint. If a property can be relocated from a person to an attention-object, and attention-objects are what the species taxonomy is organized around, then maybe the property is really just a shadow of the species — an artifact. And if one thing is an artifact, what else is?

Two things made the question hard as posed. The first question names no subject — there is no noun in the sentence for the thing being classified. The articulation stage flagged this at high confidence and the flag was later discharged on a specific ground: all five plausible subjects (inexhaustibility, model-completeness, followership-generation, depth, charisma) live in the same document neighbourhood, and the verdict below is invariant across them. The subject is deliberately left unnamed here for the same reason the pipeline left it unnamed: naming it would commit to a reading the statement does not make, and the answer does not require the commitment.

The second difficulty is the word *artifact* itself, which turns out to have five distinct referents across this project and to carry, in the corpus's own theory writing, a settled sense that is probably not the one the statement intended. Section 5 takes this up directly.

### 2. Why the person-horn fails

The corpus itself already refuses this horn, in the leadership finding's own words: *"Not a personal trait."* A person of great depth whose attention is on their own standing becomes solved anyway — personal depth does not prevent it.

But the sharper refutation comes from the statement's own first criterion. The corpus's settled bearer for the relevant property is explicitly two-place: *"generativity is a relation between object and observer, usually dominated by the object."* A thing that needs an object and an observer to be specified is not *definable on its own*. The first horn fails on the very test the statement supplies for it — which is cleaner than failing by elimination.

The strongest counter-case was handled rather than buried. The corpus preserves **self-as-object** as a real subclass: a person whose attention-object is their own *becoming* — "a project rather than a position" — is genuinely inexhaustible, and the person-located reading looks vindicated there. It is not. The same person with their own *position* as object is solved quickly; with their own *project* as object, not. The person is held fixed and the outcome varies with the object — which is the object doing the work even when the object is the person. The subclass is real; the bearer is still the object.

### 3. Why the species-horn fails: the test, run in full

The corpus has a standing method for questions of the form "is X really its own thing, or a shadow of Y?" — the **two-way dissociation**: find a case with X and not Y, and a case with Y and not X. It has been run three times, on whether Coherence, Emotional Composure, and Resonance are independent elements. All three runs returned independent. One honest note travels with the method here: those three runs tested *element-independence*; this inquiry points the same instrument at *predication* — whether an outcome is predicated on the species. That is a one-step extension — the same co-variation-breaking form, aimed at a new target — and it is named rather than slid past.

**First leg — species held fixed, outcome varies.** Take the strongest candidate subject: what generates a follower rather than a peer. The same species with the same object yields a *peer* for an expert and a *follower* for a novice — the leadership finding's own qualification, *"a topic exhausted for an expert is not exhausted for a novice."* The species does not move; the outcome does. The variance comes from the observer.

**Second leg — outcome held fixed, species varies.** Followership arises from Mission-Focus (the leader), from Dialogue-Focus (the teacher on the relevant reading), and from self-as-object (the respected-grandparent case). The outcome does not move; the species does.

Both legs pass — which is exactly what the corpus accepted as establishing independence in its three prior runs. The corpus's own text agrees in as many words: *"Not a bare consequence of species membership either. Being in a particular species is not itself the mechanism."*

One calibration note, stated because it bounds what the streak licenses: the dissociation test is now four-for-four "independent." A test that only ever returns one verdict carries decreasing information. Either the corpus's elements really are independent (plausible — they were admitted through orthogonality gates), or the test has only ever been aimed at candidates already suspected independent. The census below is the test's first non-selected application, and doubles as its calibration.

### 4. What a species is, then: a proxy, and where it fails

If the outcome is not predicated on the species, why does the species taxonomy predict so well? Because every species is defined the same way — *attention on [X]* — so the species name **tracks** the object it is named after. The corpus states the identity relation itself: *"the object is already the individuating slot."*

A proxy is not a bearer. Something can track a variable perfectly without being what the variable is predicated on — and the tracking fails exactly where one species name covers two objects.

**The corpus records one such failure, and it is the statement's own teacher.** Scenario 4 of `apt_layer_proof_scenarios.md` (the April spec's fifteen worked validation scenarios) reads, verbatim:

> *"These teachers operate in **Dialogue-Focus** (attention on the shared inquiry) or **Caregiver-Focus** (attention on student's development without harvesting student approval). Both are non-extractive attention species — the genus property holds."*

Under the two-clause criterion, the choice between those species matters completely: a shared inquiry lies **outside** the receiver; a student's development **is** the receiver — and the outside-the-receiver clause is exactly what separates followership from devotion-without-followers.

But read the second sentence, because it changes the diagnosis. For the claim Scenario 4 was making — beloved teachers generate devotion through the modulator — the species choice genuinely did not matter, and the scenario says why: both species are members of the genus (non-extractive attention), and *"the genus property holds."* The "or" was **licensed at the genus level for the scenario's own claim**. What nobody noticed is that the license does not transfer: the moment an object-level question is asked of the same case — does the thing port from this teacher to the leader? — the "or" becomes a live fork. This is a level-shift between two documents asking questions at two predication levels, not the corpus contradicting itself. (An earlier stage of this inquiry read it as a contradiction; the correction came from reading the quoted passage one sentence further — the second confirmed instance of the read-the-quote-to-its-end failure mode this corpus has already documented against itself.)

**Nobody has ever engaged the fork.** This inquiry's own analysis initially wrote that the leadership finding *silently resolved* the "or" to Dialogue-Focus, since its text reads *"a teacher attending to a shared inquiry"* — that species' object, verbatim. Checked against the leadership finding directly: it cites Scenario 9 (charismatic leadership) and Scenario 15 (the respected grandparent) and never mentions Scenario 4; its teacher case came from the coherence inquiry's deep-teacher, not from the scenario file. So the wording match is an independent construction, not a resolution. The match is also no accident — the leadership finding needed a teacher who generates *followers*, and only the Dialogue branch yields followership — but motivated-and-unstated is different from silently-chosen, and different again from adjudicated.

**The consequence for the statement:** its portability premise — *portable from the teacher to the leader* — holds on one branch of an "or" no document has faced, and fails on the other. The "or" itself admits four readings, graded by how well the text supports them: a **fork** (one teacher-type, ambiguous assignment) and the **genus-license** (the choice doesn't matter for the scenario's claim) are text-grounded; a **distributive** reading (the scenario's plural *"These teachers"* — some teachers one species, some the other) is text-grounded; an **oscillation** reading (one teacher alternating objects, via the spec's own Oscillation Dynamics section in `new_apt_layer.md`) is constructed rather than cited and is carried at lower confidence. All four leave the premise conditional; they differ on what the condition is. The branch is left unadjudicated here — both assignments are the corpus's own, and nothing in the corpus settles which the beloved-teacher case is.

### 5. Which sense of "artifact" — and why the verdict survives both

The word *artifact* has five referents across this project: a spurious appearance produced by a method (the corpus's theory sense — six uses, e.g. *"an artifact of how we defined the variables"*); a document kind; a real-but-derived property (the statement's apparent sense); a discipline output (the pipeline's own word for what a pass produces); and an observable file. Only the first is a settled corpus sense in theory writing — and it is also the standard scientific sense (an effect produced by the apparatus). The statement's derivative sense has **no corpus instance**; the corpus genuinely has no term for "real but derived," which is presumably why the statement had to reach for one.

The two senses would give opposite-looking verdicts — a **debunking** (the thing isn't really there; the species-framing produced its appearance) versus a **relocation** (the thing is real but predicated elsewhere). For the first question, the choice turns out not to matter: the two-way dissociation refutes both readings of the second horn at once, because it shows the thing is *real* (it survives the species varying — a mere appearance would not) and *not species-predicated* (it varies with the species held fixed). The first question's verdict is sense-independent.

For the missing term, no coinage is needed. The forms-of-attachment inquiry (`inquiries/2026-08-21_23-36__forms_of_attachment/finding.md`) already built the distinction for attachment types: **primitive versus derived**, with "derived-but-predictive" as an honest, real status. That vocabulary is imported here with its scope stated: it was built for output-projections (a type read off the variable mix); extending it to cross-element predication (an outcome read off the object-observer pair rather than the species) is a one-step extension of the same relation — identity or behavior determined by something else in the theory. As an optional vocabulary-only overlay (the corpus's established pattern for external anchors): in measurement terms, the species is an *indicator*, the object-observer pair is the *construct*, and the pool's error-class below is *indicator reification*.

### 6. The second question: what exists, if the class has no members

**Under the derivative sense, the class has no first member.** Nothing anywhere in the corpus has been called an artifact of a species — no document names one, states a criterion for one, or counts them. So *"what other artifacts exists?"* has no anchor for its *other*. And the emptiness is not oversight: the corpus's own membership test has run four times (three prior element-independence runs plus this inquiry's) and returned independent every time. A list cannot be produced where nothing has ever been admitted; what can be produced is the **test** and a **sorted sample** — which is what this finding supplies. Enumerating, here, just *is* auditing: any member of any eventual list would be something currently filed elsewhere.

**Under the debunking sense, the class has members already.** A debunking-sense artifact of the species is a claim whose species-level *form* is the spurious part — an object- or genus-level fact written at the species address. The corpus has caught at least two. The claim *"Mission-Focus amplifies Hope"* (standing in two places: the April spec's Species × Attachment-Variable Amplification section, and Scenario 9) was corrected by the leadership finding — the participation component is Resonance plus access-Hope — and the correction reached the scenario's context but never the table; what the species-frame contributed was the *addressing* (the cell's specific falsity has a separate cause: Resonance did not exist in April). And iteration-3's argument that Mission-Focus collapses into Self-Focus was found invalid by the leadership finding on an object-level property (a personal priority can be completed; a cause cannot) — species-level reasoning undone by the object level.

**The two senses converge on the same items.** The claims the debunking sense names as members are exactly the claims the derivative-sense sorting re-addresses. One operation — sort each claim to the axis it is actually predicated on — answers the question under both senses. That convergence is why the finding can answer a question posed in a word the corpus uses oppositely without first adjudicating the word.

**The pool.** Roughly two dozen per-species attributions sit across four sources that no document connects: the April spec's two titled sections (Species × Attachment-Variable Amplification; Oscillation Dynamics), the fifteen proof scenarios, iteration-3's species definitions and correlates, and the leadership finding's object-derived consequences. Two of the four are April files written before the object vocabulary existed — the leadership finding coined the attention-object column in August — so per-species framing was the *only available address* at authoring time. The error-class is pre-paradigm addressing, not sloppiness, and the sources date-split cleanly across the vocabulary shift. The sorting below is partly a translation from pre-object vocabulary into post-object vocabulary. (The operation itself is not new either: the leadership finding's proposed object column and the anger inquiry's four-coordinate classification table are both axis-sortings in practice — what has been missing is the name and the connective tissue.)

### 7. What the test returns: the scheme, and the worked sample

**The axes.** A per-species claim can be predicated on five different things, all of them corpus words: the **species** (which named object-kind), the **genus** (non-extractive attention as such — common to all six species), the **level** (the modulator's high / calibrated / low / collapsed reading, with its display over time), the **object** (the thing attention is on), and the **observer** (whom the outcome is relative to — the second argument-place of the corpus's own bearer). The observer's place is not decoration: two of the four originally-sorted items used observer-side variation as their evidence, and a scheme without an observer place would under-represent the verdict it serves. Whether the observer appears as a fifth column or a per-item annotation is a presentation choice deliberately left open.

**The instruments — matched to axes, never conflated.** Species-versus-object predication is decided by the **two-way dissociation**, and it has traction only where the two actually come apart: fork cases (one name, two objects), observer-variation (object fixed, outcome varies), receiver-position variation, and cross-species comparison — everywhere else the species and its object co-vary perfectly and the question is underdetermined in principle. Genus-predication is decided by the **all-species check**: a claim true of all six species is genus-predicated, not species-predicated (an inference, not a deduction — bounded by the validation limit below). Level-predication shows itself in **duration and purity variation** — the evidence-type the level items below already used. And the **two-clause criterion** (generative + outside the receiver) is a different instrument with a different job — it decides followership-support, locally; it is not the membership test, and stretching it across the pool would be a category error.

**The sample — six items sorted, none on the species axis:**

- **Object axis — peer versus follower generation.** Dissociates from the species both ways (section 3). Predicated on the object-observer pair; the species tracks it as proxy.
- **Object axis — the amplification table's oddest line, whose derivation explains its odd word.** *"Caregiver-Focus amplifies Charm via admiration (**others** admire the caregiving)."* The admirer is a third party. For a third party, the caregiver's object — the cared-for person — lies outside them and is generative, so it passes both clauses of the followership criterion; for the cared-for person the object *is* them, and it fails the outside clause. One species, two receiver positions, two outcomes, both derived from one criterion — and the table's own word *"others"* is the tell it never remarks on. This item carries a falsifiable prediction: the cared-for person and a third party should respond differently to the same caregiving display.
- **Level axis — character.** *"Over time, the observed Self-Positioning bearing becomes part of what others call 'character'."* The bearing is the level and its display sustained over time; character-attribution occurs across species and fails within a species on short exposure. A level-times-duration fact wearing species-section clothing.
- **Level axis — the oscillation misreadings, which split across two axes at once.** *That* a misreading occurs (sustained pure Self-Focus reads cold; sustained pure Caregiver-Focus reads sycophantic) is a function of sustained purity — level and duration. *Which* misreading occurs is a function of the object. A mixed-predication item, recorded as such — the scheme must be able to file a claim across two axes.
- **Genus axis — the modulator amplification itself.** *"Any species of non-extractive attention produces `g(high)` … Supplication produces `g(low)`."* The quantifier says it: predicated on the genus, indifferent to species.
- **Genus axis — Scenario 4's own devotion claim.** *"Both are non-extractive attention species — the genus property holds"* — the corpus's own phrase names genus-predication, and it is what licensed the scenario's "or" (section 4).

**What the sample licenses, stated exactly.** Six items on three axes establishes the method and does not establish an empty species column. The sample was assembled by salience, with two mitigations: the items come from three unrelated sources, and two landed on an axis (level) the analysis was not seeking — which a foregone conclusion would not produce. Within the sample, none-of-six on the species axis is the test *discriminating* — an echo, at sample scope only, of how the modulator suite's admission criteria were read when they refused every candidate after the founding three. Whether *anything* is species-predicated remains genuinely open, and one shape is named that would qualify if anything does: **shareability** — the property that separates movements from devoted individuals (a cause can be shared; a self cannot) — looks predicated on *which kind of object*, which is a species-level fact, rather than on any object's own properties. It is the standing candidate that could overturn the pattern, and it is first in the census's queue.

**The validation limit.** The April spec's own amplification table carries the flag *"(Empirical validation pending — flagged in Open Questions.)"* Nothing in the pool is validated, so the sorting reports which axis a claim is *predicated on* — never whether the claim is *true*.

**The reusable part.** The method compresses to one question — *which axis is this claim predicated on?* — askable of every future per-species claim at writing time. Two retrodictions (labeled as such — both were caught by other means, later): the question would have caught the Hope-amplification mis-addressing, and would have surfaced Scenario 4's fork the day an object-level claim first leaned on it. The forms-of-attachment inquiry's proposed registry ("for each named set: what it is a set of, what admits a member, whether primitive or derived") is the natural home — the sorting supplies that registry's predication column, when the registry is built. A pointer, not a dependency.

### 8. Recorded, not acted: the census and three residues

**The census is specified and deliberately not run.** Running the sort case by case is the Process act the layer commitment schedules next; this finding hands it over complete. The worklist: the pool's roughly two dozen attributions (four named sources, shipped unsorted) plus the nine adjacent mechanisms iteration-3 scoped outside the theory. The instruments: the battery above, applied along the traction map. The stakes, kept separate because they are different questions: for the pool, predication hygiene — each claim re-addressed to its axis; for the nine adjacent mechanisms, something larger — **Cluster 4's third reopening condition**, the corpus's own standing trigger (*"Adjacent mechanisms reveal themselves to be attention-pattern variants rather than independent phenomena"*), written once, about that exact nine-item list, and never fired. The census is that trigger's determination procedure, run for the first time. And one reading rule: the census is the dissociation test's first non-selected application — if it returns uniformly, suspect the instrument's sensitivity before celebrating the result.

**Three residues, each with its reason for non-action.** The two standing copies of the mis-addressed Hope claim (the amplification table and Scenario 9) need two one-line corrections — a structural act outside this inquiry's layer and blocked on the unperformed iteration-3.2 migration; the executable is named here so the eventual actor inherits an edit, not a re-derivation. The **piecewise-substrate-shift** observation — each relocation (this is the fourth candidate examined) moves one item off the person/species axes, so the corpus's standing attention-substrate question is being answered piecewise, and its extrapolated endpoint is Cluster 4 firing by census rather than by argument — is recorded as a seed, deferred on the census's aggregate result. And the **detection bound**: the species is currently the only coordinate the instrument layer can read (`attention-object` appears nowhere in the detection specs; the display catalog is species-shaped), so the sorting corrects predication, not detection practice — per-species formulations stay load-bearing at the instrument layer until object-side detection exists. Current state, not impossibility.

## Inherited Commitments Re-test

**1. A property can be relocated from a person to their attention-object.**
- **Source:** `inquiries/2026-08-21_18-37__leadership_and_inexhaustibility/finding.md` — the relocation of inexhaustibility.
- **Re-test status:** **RE-TESTED — commitment confirmed but frame revised.**
- **Evidence:** The relocation had happened exactly once and had never been tested twice — which is what the second question asks. This inquiry ran it as a *test* (the two-way dissociation) rather than asserting it, and the test passed — but the landing point moved: the bearer the evidence names is the **pair** (object × observer), not the object alone. The observer's share is visible in the prior finding's own expert/novice qualification. The frame revision: relocations land on the bearer the evidence names, and that bearer can be two-place.

**2. The species enumeration is fixed and complete.**

- **Evidence:** The set of six was not challenged and did not need to be. What changed is the names' role: each species name is a proxy for its object, and the corpus's own Scenario 4 shows one name covering two objects — so the enumeration is intact while its individuating slot (the object) is confirmed as the deeper column, exactly as the prior finding's own proposed object column implied.

**3. Properties are had, not admitted, and owe no gate.**

**4. Model-completeness's ceiling is sender-side while the quantity is receiver-side.**
