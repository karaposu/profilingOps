## Question

From this inquiry's framing document, the object is a block quotation of prior assistant output, followed by the instruction *"lets dive deep into this."*

> *"APT computes how strong an attachment is and what kind, then stops — it never says what each kind looks like from outside. That's why three states kept getting confused: attachment (persists when satisfied), pursuit (ends when its gap closes), compliance (reverses when the threat lifts). That layer isn't missing, though: 3.2.1 already ships a 'Persistence under change' column with all five rows. It just was never named as a layer."*

APT — Attachment & Presentation Theory — is the model this project uses to read how attached one person is to another from what they say. It produces two things: a **magnitude** (how strong the attachment is) and a **type** (what kind it is — status-driven, transactional, coerced, bonded, or collaborative).

The passage makes four moves in sequence. A **gap claim**: the theory says how strong and what kind, then stops, never saying what each kind looks like from outside. A **causal claim**: that gap is why three states — attachment, pursuit, compliance — kept getting confused with each other. A **self-correction**: the layer is not actually missing, because a table column already ships it. And a **residual**: it was never named as a layer.

The goal, per the framing document, was a deep treatment of that account — which required first establishing what relation the treatment bears to it. Elaborating it, verifying it, naming the thing, explaining the confusion, and reporting its status were all live readings, and the passage's own wording does not choose between them. The inquiry was bounded to the engagement-state material, the persistence column and its five rows, the three named states, and what this corpus means by a *layer*. It declared **Meaning** as its working layer: what the thing *is*, ahead of what a specification section for it should look like or how an analyst would tell the states apart in practice.

## Finding Summary

- **The passage's residual is right and its category is wrong, and the second explains the first.** It was never named as a layer because it is not a layer. Every element this corpus calls a layer has something above it that consumes what it produces; this one is terminal.

- **What it is, the corpus already has a word for: a property.** Each modulator in the theory carries a *failure signature* — what that component looks like from outside when it fails. Each attachment type carries a persistence behaviour in exactly the same relation. Five types, five behaviours, one each.

- **That is a property relation, not a category membership.** The corpus lists *distinct failure signatures* among the architectural properties every modulator has. Properties are had, not admitted. So there was never a category question to settle, and no admission gate is owed.

- **The naming action item was written once and refined once — not three times.** This inquiry's own middle stages diagnosed a drift across three versions. Reading the third finding's full action list shows it contains no version of the item. The correction removed the premise three separate claims were resting on.

- **Neither version was carried out because nobody has edited the specification files yet.** All three findings were written the same evening, under six hours apart. There has been no editing pass, so there was no occasion for one.

- **The real blocker is that the iteration was never migrated.** The specification file `BASEFILES/profiling_data_extraction/pragma/core/new_apt_layer.md` contains zero occurrences of *MAGNITUDE*, *TYPE*, *Persistence*, *Coherence*, or *Emotional Composure*. The persistence column has no home to be named in yet.

- **So the passage's *"it just was never named"* has a smaller answer than four disciplines were reaching for: it was never written down — and neither was most of the iteration it belongs to.** Naming follows migration and is nearly free once migration happens, because the property already has the corpus's own word for it.

- **The residual is three steps, not one: migrate, then describe, then name.** The passage named the third. The first is a specification-editing act, deliberately outside this inquiry's declared scope.

- **The passage's own internal tension dissolves.** It says three states were being confused and that the column ships five rows. Three of the five behaviours are the three states. A subset and its superset, stated in one sentence — never in conflict.

- **One thing larger than the question, recorded and not pursued.** The main specification file states the attachment formula as `f(charm, hope, fear) × g(non-extractive attention)` — three variables and one gate — while describing itself as *"the current-state consolidated theory."* An unnamed property is a vocabulary problem. A specification that misstates the theory's own formula while claiming to be current is a different and larger problem.

## Finding

### Why this question was being asked at all

This project reads conversations and infers how attached one person is to another. The theory behind it, APT, has been developed across a numbered sequence of iterations, each recorded as a finding document in `inquiries/`. Iteration-3.2.1 — the finding at `inquiries/attachment_variable_interactions/finding.md` — added something it called **Two Output Dimensions**: the theory produces a *magnitude* (a number for how strong the attachment is) and a *type* (which of five kinds it is).

Alongside that, it shipped a small table. The table has three columns: which variable dominates, what type that produces, and — the column at issue here — **Persistence under change**: what happens to the attachment when circumstances shift. Five rows, one per type.

In a session five hours before this inquiry, an earlier finding used that column to resolve a confusion. Three things had been getting mixed up: **attachment**, which persists when the person is satisfied; **pursuit**, which ends once the gap it was chasing closes; and **compliance**, which reverses the moment the threat behind it lifts. Those three are three of the column's five entries. The passage quoted above is that finding's own summary of the situation, and the user quoted it back and asked to go deeper.

Going deeper meant, first, deciding whether to build on the passage or check it. Both readings were live. This inquiry did both, and the checking is what produced most of the result.

---

### 1 · *Layer* means two different things in this theory, and no document says which

The passage's operative claim is *"never named as a layer."* That claim has no truth-value until the word is pinned down, and in this corpus the word is doing two jobs.

In one use it names a **stage**: something that consumes what the level below it produces and feeds what the level above it consumes. The PRAGMA Signal Layer, the Interpretation Layer, and APT itself all work this way. (PRAGMA is the conversational-act analysis system this theory sits inside; it reads messages and produces signals that APT then consumes.)

In the other use it names a **part**: a piece a whole decomposes into, with no consumption relation involved at all. Domain 1 (attachment) and Domain 2 (presentation) are parts of APT. The internal and displayed halves of Self-Positioning are parts of one modulator. Neither consumes the other.

The reason to treat these as two meanings rather than one meaning at two scales is that **no element passes both tests**. Every usage in the corpus satisfies exactly one. A word applied to two disjoint sets is not one meaning stretched; it is two relations sharing a label.

This is offered as a description of what the corpus already does, not as a licence to redefine the word. That distinction matters, because the corpus demonstrably treats its own labels as revisable. In `inquiries/self_focus_generalization/finding.md` it restructured *Self-Focus* into a two-level naming — **Non-Extractive Attention** at the theoretical level, *Self-Focus* kept at the user-facing level — and, in the same decision, **killed** the alternative that would have kept one broadened label, on the stated ground that *"the label-definition mismatch is structurally incoherent."* Usage here is evidence of what is meant, not an authority about what should be meant.

**One more sense is held apart deliberately.** This inquiry's own framing document declares a "Meaning layer," and its sibling process documents speak of Structural and Process layers. Those name *kinds of inquiry*, not kinds of theory element. They are correct in their own domain and have nothing to do with the question here. Saying so explicitly is the only way this finding avoids equivocating in its own framing.

---

### 2 · The persistence column is neither a stage nor a part

**It fails the stage test on the second clause.** It does consume something — given a type, it yields a trajectory. But nothing consumes what it produces. No element of APT takes a trajectory prediction as input, and none of the theory's three declared outputs does either.

The corpus's own practice settles what to call something in that position. Its terminal producers are gathered under a section titled **`## Three Outputs from PRAGMA`** in the main specification file `BASEFILES/profiling_data_extraction/pragma/core/new_apt_layer.md`. The word *layer* is not applied to them. **Every element this corpus calls a layer has something above it.** The persistence column has nothing above it.

**It fails the part test too, but the reason needed correcting.** The earlier version of this argument said the whole it would be a part of is *a table*, and a table is a document object rather than a theory element. That is refutable by reading one heading. The column sits inside a section headed **"Addition 4 — Two Output Dimensions (MAGNITUDE and TYPE)"** — plainly theory content, not a bare table.

Individuating the whole correctly changes the argument and not the verdict. The Addition's parts are **magnitude** and **type**. Persistence is not a third coordinate sibling of those two: magnitude and type are two independent readings of the same four variable-weights — the sum and the mix — whereas persistence is *derived from type*. Once you know the type, the trajectory follows. A quantity derived from type is not a third dimension alongside type.

So the passage's *"it just was never named as a layer"* is **true, and true because it is not one.**

---

### 3 · What it is instead: a property, in the corpus's own sense

The theory has a group of components called the **Modulator Suite** — Self-Positioning, Coherence, and Emotional Composure — which sit between the two domains and scale the attachment result rather than generating it. The finding that established the Suite, `inquiries/apt_modulator_landscape/finding.md`, describes its members as

> *"all operating at the same structural position (between Domain 1 and Domain 2) with the same architectural properties (multiplicative gating, dual character, **distinct failure signatures**, orthogonality to each other)."*

Read that list carefully. A **failure signature** — what a component looks like from outside when it goes wrong — appears there as one of the *properties every modulator has*, sitting alongside multiplicative gating and orthogonality. It is not a set that things get admitted to. Each modulator simply has one: Self-Positioning fails as *devaluation*, Coherence as *Model-Collapse*, Emotional Composure as *Contagion-Drain*.

**Each attachment type has a persistence behaviour in exactly that relation.** Three modulators, three failure signatures, one each. Five attachment types, five persistence behaviours, one each. Same shape, same relation.

This is a **property** claim, not a membership claim, and the difference is not cosmetic. Three objections had been raised against the earlier, membership-flavoured version of this verdict, and all three lose their target once the category disappears:

- *"The signature category states no admission gate."* Properties do not have gates, because nothing is admitted to them.
- *"Signature may be an empty label."* A label the corpus uses consistently as a property-type is not empty.
- *"The category is only one-third migrated — two of the three failure signatures are absent from the specification files."* True (see §5), but that is a fact about the **modulators** being absent, not about the relation being weak. Their signatures are missing because they are.

The earlier finding in this chain, `forms_of_attachment`, called the persistence column "the signature level." That identification was right about *what stands in the relation* and wrong about *what kind of thing the relation is*. The restatement here preserves its content and drops the level language.

**A second, unrelated line of reasoning lands in the same place.** Library cataloguing distinguishes a *work* from an *expression* of it and from a *manifestation* of that expression. Applied here, the printed rows are the manifestation, the five listed behaviours are the expression, and the thing being named is neither — it is what they realise. That is the same answer the property reading gives, reached from a starting point that has nothing to do with this theory. It is corroboration, not a premise; the property reading stands on its own.

### 4 · Why it never got named — counted, not argued

This is a question about what happened, and questions about what happened are settled by checking rather than reasoning. Two of this inquiry's middle-stage conclusions did not survive the check.

**There are two versions of the naming action item, not three.** Version 1 is in `coherence_unpredictability_paradox`: *"Add the engagement-state layer as a named section — attachment, pursuit, and compliance — built by promoting the existing 'Persistence under change' column…"* Version 2 is in `compliance_versus_attachment`: *"Add the engagement-state names as a derived view of the persistence column, not as a level of their own — carrying the provisional flag, and carrying all five of the column's rows rather than three."*

The third finding, `forms_of_attachment`, was counted as producing a version 3 because one of its action items also concerns the same table. Reading its full MUST list shows five items, and **none of them is a version of the naming item**: they concern the two-level answer, the table's three columns, an incompleteness flag, resolution-indexing, and a correction to a neighbouring finding. Its item about the table's middle column is a different action about a different column, produced by a different question. It also *re-tested and confirmed* the derived-view commitment it inherited, and confirming an inherited commitment is not rewriting an action item.

**The single transition between the two versions is not silent churn.** The `compliance_versus_attachment` finding recorded it as a **MUST drift** — quoting version 1 verbatim, stating version 2, and supplying a rationale (the original compressed five rows into three members whose tests are not comparable). That is the corpus's own revision mechanism working exactly as designed. Two data points do not make a pattern, and the one transition on record is a documented improvement.

**Neither version was executed because nobody has edited the specification files yet.** This inquiry's Innovation stage argued that the action items were written in a form nobody could perform, and offered as evidence that "three performers had three descriptions over eight months and none performed." The three findings' file timestamps are **18:31, 23:28, and 00:23** — under six hours end to end, in a single working session. There was no editing pass between them, so there was no opportunity to perform anything. The eight-months figure originated inside this inquiry and is corrected here on the timestamps.

Executability remains a property worth having. A description of a change places work on whoever reads it; text-plus-destination does not. What is gone is the claim that its absence *caused* the non-execution.

### 5 · What actually blocks all of it: the iteration was never migrated

The findings in `inquiries/` are where the theory is developed. The specification files under `BASEFILES/profiling_data_extraction/pragma/` are where it is written down for use. Those two have come apart, and how far apart is checkable.

In the main specification file `BASEFILES/profiling_data_extraction/pragma/core/new_apt_layer.md`, an exact-case count returns **zero** for every one of: `MAGNITUDE`, `TYPE`, `Persistence`, `Coherence`, `Emotional Composure`, `Specificity`. The phrase *Persistence under change* does not appear anywhere under `BASEFILES/`. Neither does `Model-Collapse` or `Contagion-Drain`. The one failure signature that is present — `devaluation`, five occurrences — belongs to the one modulator that was migrated.

That same file states the attachment formula as

> `Attachment ≈ f(charm, hope, fear) × g(non-extractive attention)`

— three variables and one gate — and describes itself as *"the current-state consolidated theory."* Its own *"Other known absences"* section still lists **Resonance** as a *"possible additional modulator."*

Meanwhile a neighbouring file, `BASEFILES/profiling_data_extraction/pragma/core/apt_should_be_extended_with_resonance.md`, states the formula as `f(charm, hope, fear, resonance) × g₁(SP) × g₂(Coherence) × g₃(Emotional Composure)` — four variables and three gates — and carries a section on what TYPE implies. **That file is the only migration that has ever happened**, and it happened by adding a sidecar rather than editing the main specification. Which is why the main specification still lists Resonance as an absence while the file beside it treats Resonance as the fourth variable.

So there is a demonstrated pattern for carrying an iteration across — write a standalone file that cites its source finding — with exactly one worked instance, and a known cost: it leaves the main specification saying something false.

**This is what the passage was actually pointing at without seeing it.** There is no *Persistence under change* column in the specification to promote, no TYPE dimension to attach a derived view to, and no place a named property could sit. The naming step is not blocked on a decision about what to call the thing. It is blocked on the material not being there.

**And the gap is widening, not static.** The specification file is the entry point — it is what a reader is handed. Every new finding that develops the theory increases the distance between what that reader is handed and what the theory now says. A backlog item that sits still can wait; one that deteriorates cannot, on the same terms.

This is recorded and not pursued. Doing the migration is a specification-editing act, and this inquiry declared Meaning as its scope precisely to avoid reaching for the edit before settling what the thing is. Naming *where the difficulty lies* is a Meaning-layer result; performing the fix is not, and that crossing is named rather than made.

---

### 6 · The residual, restated: migrate → describe → name

The passage compressed the remaining work into one step. It is three.

1. **Migrate.** Carry iteration-3.2.1 into the specification file — the two output dimensions, the persistence column, and the four-variable formula with its three gates. Out of scope here as a specification-editing act.
2. **Describe.** State what persistence-under-change *is*: a property each attachment type has, in the same relation as a modulator's failure signature. That is what §3 above does, and it is the step this inquiry was scoped to.
3. **Name.** Give it the label. This is the step the passage named, and it is nearly free once step 1 has happened, because step 2 supplies the corpus's own word for the relation.

---

### 7 · The one hypothesis nobody had stated

Both prior versions of the action item, and the passage itself, assume there is something there to name. That assumption was never put forward as a claim and therefore never tested. It deserves stating: **perhaps there is nothing here to name — perhaps the five rows are a useful display and not the extension of any stable abstraction.**

It is rejected, and the reason is short. Under the property reading, what would be named is a property, and the **bearer** of that property — the attachment type — has been stable in this theory since iteration-3.2. A property of a stable bearer is not a floating abstraction.

It is worth saying why the rejection is stated this briefly rather than turned into a burden. An earlier stage of this inquiry proposed that any naming must first *demonstrate* the abstraction's stability. No criterion for what would count as a demonstration was supplied, and a bar with no criterion cannot be cleared — it is a permanent deferral wearing the clothes of rigour.

---

### 8 · The passage's internal tension dissolves

The passage says three states were being confused and that the column ships all five rows, without saying which number the answer should work with. There is nothing to reconcile. The three states are three of the five behaviours. It is a subset and its superset stated in one sentence.

---

### 9 · What this finding admits about itself

It is the third statement about this same item, and both predecessors were written with confidence and then revised.

What differs is not confidence. It is that this one reached its conclusions by **counting** — reading a full action list, comparing three file timestamps, running exact-case counts against the specification files — rather than by argument. Two of the things it corrects are things this inquiry's own earlier stages had asserted. Neither correction was available to reasoning; both required checking.

It can still be wrong in the ordinary way. If *property* is the wrong relation, this is the third entry and someone writes a fourth. What would make that visible is stated under Refinement Triggers below.

And the cost of being wrong is low, which is worth saying plainly. Nothing downstream of this consumes the label — no code reads it, no procedure branches on it. Getting the word wrong costs inquiry time and breaks nothing.
