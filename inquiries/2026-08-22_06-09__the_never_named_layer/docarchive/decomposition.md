**Hidden coupling found and surfaced: two.**

The first would have been fatal: **P2's verdict rests on individuating the whole as a table.** That is a choice about how to carve the object, not a fact read off it. A reader who takes the persistence column to be part of *the theory's output description* rather than part of *a table* gets a passing part-test and the opposite verdict. Surfacing it converts P2's first criterion from *apply the part test* into *name the whole explicitly and defend the individuation*.

The second is quieter: **P1 assumes its sense-list is complete**, and the inquiry has already been wrong about that once — a seventh sense turned up in its own framing document. The criterion exists because of the near-miss, not in spite of it.
