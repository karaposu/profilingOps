### Key Insights

**KI1 — The six senses reduce to two, and the reduction is decidable.** Senses 1 and 6 are one thing: a **stage** in a processing stack, defined by a **consumption relation** — it takes what the level below produces and produces what the level above consumes. Senses 2, 3 and 4 are one thing at three granularities: a **part** of a whole, defined by a **decomposition relation**. Sense 5 is a document convention and carries no structural claim. So the corpus uses one word for **stage** and for **part**, which are different relations, and never says which it means.

**KI2 — The candidate is a stage under one test and not a part under the other.** The persistence column is not a component of APT — the whole it belongs to is a *table*, which is a document object. But it does consume: given a type, it yields a trajectory. That is a function, and a function that consumes what the level below produces is exactly what the stage sense names. **So the passage is correct under one of the two senses the corpus uses and incorrect under the other, and nobody can tell which because the word is undefined.**

**KI3 — But the stage test has a second clause, and the candidate fails it.** A stage produces something the level above consumes. **Nothing in APT consumes a trajectory prediction.** The three named outputs — Behavioral Profiling, APT Inference, APT Profiling — do not take one. The candidate consumes and is not consumed: it is terminal.

**KI4 — And the corpus's own practice for terminal producers is to call them *outputs*, not *layers*.** The architecture diagram's terminal stage is titled *Three Outputs from PRAGMA*, and the word *layer* is not applied to it. Every element the corpus does call a layer has something above it.

**KI5 — Three candidate referents have been conflated, and that is why the action item drifted.** The thing to be named could be **the column** (a document artifact), **the function** it encodes (type → trajectory), or **the set of five behaviours** it lists. The three versions of the action item name a different one each: v1 names a set of three, v2 names a set of five, v3 describes the artifact. **The item was not progressively weakened — it drifted across referents**, and each inquiry picked one without noticing the others.

**KI6 — The corpus already has the right category, with three members, and has never connected it.** Each modulator has a **failure signature**: devaluation for Self-Positioning, Model-Collapse for Coherence, Contagion-Drain for Emotional Composure. A signature is not a layer, not an output, and not a part — it is a **characteristic property attached to a component**, naming what that component looks like from outside. Three modulators, three signatures, one each. **Five types, five persistence behaviours, one each. Same shape.**

**KI7 — Two later findings had already arrived at the edge of this without naming it.** One concluded that compliance is *the outside-view signature* of a Fear-dominant attachment and that the persistence column is a **non-failure** instance of the signature relation. The other called the column *the signature level*. Neither took the step of saying the column **is a signature set** of the same kind the modulators already have.

**KI8 — The three-versus-five tension dissolves rather than resolving.** Three states are three of the five behaviours. Same set, partial listing. The passage's two numbers were never in conflict; they were a subset and its superset stated in one sentence.

**KI9 — The residual has three steps and the passage named only the last.** At spec level: the structure must be **migrated**, then **categorised**, then **named**. The passage's *"it just was never named"* is true and is the third step.

## Phase 4 *(protocol Phase 3)* — Ambiguity Collapse

### Ambiguity A1 — What does *layer* mean in this theory?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** it means one thing — a level of description — and the six usages are that one thing applied at different scales. Multiplying senses is over-reading a word that is doing ordinary work.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** the usages are not one thing at different scales, because they satisfy **different and non-overlapping tests**. Every element the corpus calls a layer either consumes what is below it and feeds what is above (the PRAGMA stages, the Interpretation Layer, APT itself) or is a part of a whole with no consumption relation at all (Domain 1 and Domain 2 do not consume each other; internal and displayed Self-Positioning do not). No element passes both. A word applied to two disjoint sets is not one meaning at two scales.

**Confidence: HIGH.**

**Resolution:** *layer* names two relations — **stage** (consumption) and **part** (decomposition) — and the corpus distinguishes them nowhere. Every claim below says which.

- **Fixed:** the category question must be answered before the naming question.
- **No longer allowed:** any unqualified use of *layer* in the answer.
- **Depends on this:** the whole verdict.

### Ambiguity A2 — Which of the two is the candidate?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** the stage sense, plainly. The passage says the theory *"computes… then stops"*, which is stage language, and the column supplies the next computation: given a type, a trajectory.

**Why the counter partly holds and then fails:** the first clause of the stage test passes — the column does consume type. The second clause fails: **nothing consumes what it produces.** No element of APT takes a trajectory prediction as input, and the three named outputs do not. And the corpus's own practice for terminal producers is decisive: the architecture's terminal stage is titled *Three Outputs from PRAGMA*, and *layer* is not applied to it. **Every element the corpus calls a layer has something above it.**

**Confidence: HIGH.**

**Resolution:** the candidate is **not a layer in either sense**. It fails decomposition (its whole is a table, a document object) and it fails the second clause of consumption (it is terminal, and terminal producers are called outputs here).

- **Fixed:** *"never named as a layer"* is true, and true because it is not one.
- **No longer allowed:** naming it a layer without first overturning the terminal-producer practice.
- **Changed in the model:** the passage's residual is not an omission but a **category error**, which is why every attempt to execute it was rewritten.

### Ambiguity A3 — Then what is it?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** a third output dimension, alongside magnitude and type. It is terminal, the corpus calls terminal producers outputs, and *output dimension* is a category the corpus already has with two members.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** magnitude and type are **independent** readings of the same four numbers — the sum and the mix. A third dimension would have to be independent of both. Persistence is **derived from type**: given the type, the trajectory follows. A derived quantity is not a third dimension of the thing it is derived from.

What fits instead is a category the corpus already has three members of. Each modulator carries a **failure signature** — devaluation, Model-Collapse, Contagion-Drain — which is not a layer, not an output, and not a part, but a **characteristic property attached to a component, naming what that component looks like from outside**. Three modulators, three signatures, one each. Five types, five persistence behaviours, one each. **Same shape, same relation.**

The natural objection — that signatures in this corpus are about *failure* — was already answered by a prior finding, which identified the persistence column itself as the corpus's **non-failure instance** of the same relation.

**Confidence: HIGH** on the category; **MEDIUM** on the claim that the corpus's signature category is broad enough to hold a non-failure member without amendment.

**Resolution:** the five persistence entries are a **signature set** — persistence signatures standing to attachment types exactly as failure signatures stand to modulators.

- **Fixed:** the category is one the corpus already has.
- **No longer allowed:** treating this as a new architectural element.
- **Depends on this:** what the naming action item should say in its fourth version.

### Ambiguity A4 — What has been being named, across three attempts?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** one thing, named three times with decreasing ambition. The versions shrink — a named section, then a derived view, then a note — which reads as three inquiries converging on a smaller claim about the same object.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** read what each version's *object* is. v1 names a **set of three** — attachment, pursuit, compliance. v2 names a **set of five** — the column's rows. v3 describes an **artifact** — the middle column, which is not even the same column. Three different objects, one of which is a different column of the table. A convergence on one object would keep the object fixed while changing the claim; these keep the claim's form and change the object.

**Confidence: HIGH.**

**Resolution:** the action item **drifted across referents** rather than weakening. The three candidate referents are the **column** (artifact), the **function** it encodes, and the **set of behaviours** it lists.

- **Fixed:** the drift's cause is an unsettled referent, not diminishing conviction.
- **No longer allowed:** reading the sequence as convergence.
- **What changed:** the instability is explained, which is the precondition for a version that holds.

### Ambiguity A5 — Is the residual naming, or something before it?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** naming. The column exists, the states are described, the work is done — all that remains is a label.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** it is true of the findings and false of the spec. **Resolution:** the residual has three steps — **migrate, categorise, name** — and the passage named the third. Categorising is what A3 does; migrating is a Structural-layer act the framing document scoped out.

### Ambiguity A6 — Should it be named at all?

*(Required: the not-promoting precedent is live and a verdict that never considered it would be unexamined.)*

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** no. Vitality is on record as real, impactful and correctly left in a different architectural category. This candidate is provisional, has one row contradicted by a case the corpus recorded, and has no determination procedure. Leaving it as a table column with a note may be the correct outcome, exactly as leaving Vitality outside the Suite was.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** the Vitality precedent is about **admission to a category whose criteria it failed** — Vitality could not generate attachment alone and did not behave smoothly across its range, so it failed two of eight stated criteria. The correct outcome there was exclusion *because a gate was applied and not passed*. Here the proposal is not admission to a gated category: signatures have no admission gate, because a signature is not admitted but **observed** — the corpus did not vote devaluation into the Suite, it noticed that Self-Positioning collapse looks like devaluation. Applying an exclusion precedent from a gated category to an ungated one imports the wrong test.

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** The disanalogy is real but rests on a claim about signatures being ungated, which the corpus states nowhere — it is read off the fact that no signature was ever argued through criteria.

**Resolution:** naming is warranted, and warranted as **recognition rather than admission**. The Vitality outcome remains available for anything proposed as a new *element*; it does not govern the recognition of a property.

### Ambiguity A7 — Load-bearing concept test: *stage* and *part*

*(Required — both are Phase-5-bound and both are loop-coined.)*

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** neologisms. The corpus says *layer*; inventing two words to explain one is the analysis imposing structure.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):**
- **Proxy-versus-structural.** The pair is not an incidental property used as a proxy. Each names a **relation with a test**: consumption for one, decomposition for the other, and the tests partition the corpus's usages with no element passing both.
- **Discoverability.** The determination is one question: *does this consume what is below it and feed what is above?* Every existing usage answers it.
- **User-language alignment — weak, and stated.** The asker said *layer* and gets told the word means two things. That is more apparatus than they brought, and the mitigation is that the ambiguity is *why* the naming kept failing. The answer must lead with the verdict and introduce the pair as its explanation.

**Confidence: HIGH** on structural warrant; **MEDIUM** on the labels.

**Resolution:** retained; labels held provisionally.

### Ambiguity A8 — Specific-versus-pattern: is the referent drift about these three versions?

*(Required — KI5 is a Key Insight built from three cases.)*

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** three cases is a small set, and calling it a drift pattern over-reads a sequence that might be three independent decisions.

**Why the counter fails, and where it holds:** the three versions were written by three consecutive inquiries, each reading the previous, each rewriting rather than executing. That is a chain, not three independent decisions — the second cites the first, the third cites both. But the counter holds on scope: **three chained cases establish that the drift happened here; they do not establish that the corpus drifts whenever a referent is unsettled.**

**Confidence: HIGH** on the local claim; **LOW** on any general one.

**Resolution:** the drift is **established for this chain and not generalised.**

### SV4 — Clarified Understanding

*Layer* names two different relations in this theory — a **stage**, which consumes what is below and feeds what is above, and a **part**, which decomposes a whole. No element satisfies both, and no document says which is meant.

The candidate is neither. It is not a part, because the whole it sits in is a table. And it is not a stage, because although it consumes type, **nothing consumes what it produces** — and the corpus's own practice is to call terminal producers *outputs*.

What it is: a **signature set**. Each attachment type carries a persistence behaviour exactly as each modulator carries a failure signature — a characteristic property naming what a component looks like from outside. The corpus has had that category since iteration-3.2 and has three members in it.

So *"it just was never named as a layer"* is true, and true because **it is not one**. The naming attempts were rewritten three times not because conviction weakened but because the three versions named three different objects — a set of three, a set of five, and a different column altogether.

And the residual is not one step but three: **migrate, categorise, name**. The passage named the third.

No longer viable: naming it a layer; treating the version sequence as convergence; treating the Vitality precedent as governing; and any completeness claim about the five rows, which is empirical and flagged.

### SV6 — Stabilized Model

**The passage's residual is right and its category is wrong, and the second explains why the first never got done.**

*Layer* names two different relations in this theory, and no document says which is meant. A **stage** consumes what the level below produces and feeds what the level above consumes — the PRAGMA Signal Layer, the Interpretation Layer, and APT itself all pass this. A **part** decomposes a whole — Domain 1, Self-Positioning and Domain 2 are parts of APT; internal and displayed Self-Positioning are parts of one modulator. **No element passes both tests**, so the word is applied to two disjoint sets.

**The candidate is in neither set.** It is not a part: the whole it belongs to is a table, which is a document object rather than a theory element. And it is not a stage: it consumes a type, but **nothing consumes what it produces** — no element of APT takes a trajectory prediction as input, and the theory's own terminal producers are titled *Three Outputs from PRAGMA*, with *layer* not applied to them. Every element this corpus calls a layer has something above it.

**What it is, the corpus already has.** Each modulator carries a **failure signature** — devaluation, Model-Collapse, Contagion-Drain — a characteristic property naming what that component looks like from outside. It is not a layer, not an output, not a part. Three modulators, three signatures, one each. **Five attachment types, five persistence behaviours, one each.** Same shape, same relation. And the objection that signatures here are about *failure* was already answered by a prior finding, which identified this very column as the corpus's non-failure instance of the relation.

So the five entries are a **signature set**: persistence signatures standing to attachment types as failure signatures stand to modulators.

**Which is why it kept not getting named.** The action item was written three times, and the three versions name **three different objects** — a set of three states, a set of five rows, and a note about a different column of the same table. That is not one claim weakening; it is a referent drifting, because nobody had settled whether the thing to be named was the **column** (an artifact), the **function** it encodes, or the **set of behaviours** it lists. A version that fixes the referent is the precondition for a version that holds.

**And the residual is three steps, not one.** At spec level the phrase *Persistence under change* appears in no file, and neither does the column; the sole spec-layer trace of TYPE is a prose aside in a sidecar migrating a different iteration. So the order is **migrate → categorise → name**, and the passage named the third. Categorising is what this finding does. Migrating is a Structural-layer act, scoped out by this inquiry's own Meaning commitment.

**The three-versus-five tension in the passage dissolves rather than resolving.** Three states are three of the five behaviours — a subset and its superset stated in one sentence. They were never in conflict.

**What this finding must admit about itself.** It is the **fourth version** of an action item whose three predecessors were each written with confidence and each superseded by the next. What differs is structural rather than confidential: the prior three each named a different referent without noticing the others, so no two were comparable; this one identifies the drift's cause and assigns a category the corpus already has three members of. That is a difference in kind — and it can fail the same way. If *signature* is the wrong category, this is the fourth entry and someone writes a fifth.

**One thing larger than the question, recorded rather than pursued.** The main spec carries `f(charm, hope, fear) × g(non-extractive attention)` — three variables, one gate — while describing itself as *"the current-state consolidated theory."* Four structural additions are absent from it, and exactly one iteration has been migrated, by adding a sidecar rather than editing the spec. An unnamed category is a vocabulary problem. A spec that misstates the theory's own formula while claiming currency is a correctness problem, and it is the context this question was asked in.
