## Phase 2–3 — Adversarial Evaluation + Verdicts

### 1 · The two-relation split *(stage / part)*

**Prosecution.** A word applied inconsistently is sloppy writing, not a structure. Deriving two relations from it dignifies an accident, and the corpus demonstrably revises its own labels rather than treating them as evidence.

**Defense.** The split is a **description**, not a derivation of authority. The claim is only that the corpus's existing usages partition cleanly under two tests — which is checkable and was checked, usage by usage. Nothing in it says the usage is correct.

**Collision.** Defense holds. The prior run had already refined this from *licence* to *description*, and that refinement is what survives prosecution.

**Verdict: SURVIVE.** *Caveat:* it must ship as a description of existing usage, never as a warrant for redefining the word.

### 2 · The candidate is neither a stage nor a part

**Prosecution.** *Substance-axis, applied literally.* The part-test verdict rests on individuating the whole as a **table**. Read the source: the column sits inside *"Addition 4 — Two Output Dimensions"* — theory content. Individuate the whole there and the part test may pass.

**Defense.** Individuating at the Addition changes the argument and not the verdict. The Addition's parts are **magnitude** and **type**; persistence is not a third coordinate part but an **attribute of type's values**. The stage-test verdict is untouched — nothing consumes a trajectory prediction, and the corpus calls terminal producers *outputs*.

**Collision.** Prosecution wins on the argument; defense wins on the verdict — and the corrected argument is **stronger**, because it no longer depends on dismissing the whole as a document object.

**Verdict: REFINE.** *Target:* individuate at the Addition, and argue that persistence is not a coordinate sibling of magnitude and type. Drop the table framing.

### 3 · The signature verdict

**Prosecution.** *External-anchor sub-axis.* The verdict places the candidate **in a category**. The corpus has no such category: it lists *distinct failure signatures* among the **architectural properties** every modulator has, alongside multiplicative gating and dual character. Three modulators do not populate a set of signatures; each **has** one. A verdict of the form *X belongs to category C* is malformed when C is a property slot.

**Defense.** The mischaracterisation is in the framing, not the content. Restated as a property, the analogy is **cleaner**: each modulator has a failure signature as a property; each attachment type has a persistence signature as a property. Properties are **had**, not joined — which is exactly what the upstream recognition-versus-admission argument was reaching for and got half right.

**Collision.** Prosecution wins on *category*; defense wins on the underlying relation, restated. And the correction **dissolves three of the four objections raised against this verdict**: a property needs no gate; a property cannot be an empty label if the corpus uses it consistently as a property-type; and the migration objection turns out to be about something else entirely (candidate 5).

**Verdict: REFINE — and the refinement strengthens it.** *Target:* **persistence is a property of an attachment type, as a failure signature is a property of a modulator.** Not a category, not a membership claim, no gate required.

## Phase 3.5 — Assembly Check

The survivors and refinements together yield one architecture none of them states.

> ### ASM-3 — There was never a naming problem. There was a migration problem wearing a naming problem's clothes.
>
> **Persistence is a property**, not a category member. The corpus lists *distinct failure signatures* among the architectural properties every modulator has; each attachment type has a persistence behaviour the same way. Properties are had, not admitted — so there was never a category question to settle, and the four disciplines that tried to settle one were answering a malformed question.
>
> **The item was written once and refined once**, five hours later, with the refinement recorded verbatim and justified. That is the corpus's revision mechanism working, not churn.
>
> **Neither version was executed because no editing pass has happened.** All three findings were written the same evening.
>
> **And what actually blocks everything is that the iteration containing TYPE, the persistence column, Coherence, Emotional Composure and two of the three failure signatures was never migrated at all.**
>
> So the passage's *"it just was never named as a layer"* has a simpler answer than four disciplines were reaching for: **it was never written down — and neither was most of the iteration it belongs to.** Naming follows migration and is nearly free once migration happens, because the property already has the corpus's own word for it.

**Prosecution.** Two objections. **(1)** This deflates the inquiry to *update the spec*, which is a chore rather than a finding. **(2)** It reaches its conclusion by correcting two upstream facts rather than by argument, which makes it bookkeeping.

**Defense.** **(1)** It deflates the **naming** question — which is what was asked — and relocates the difficulty to a larger, checkable, actionable problem. A finding that correctly says *your hard question is easy and the easy one is hard* is more useful than one that elaborates the hard-seeming question. **(2)** Both corrections were **countable** — a MUST list read, three timestamps compared — and neither was available to argument. That the answer came from checking rather than reasoning is a property of the question, which was historical.

**Collision.** Defense holds on both, and objection (2) identifies the assembly's actual strength.

**Verdict: SURVIVE — no caveats on any critical dimension.**
