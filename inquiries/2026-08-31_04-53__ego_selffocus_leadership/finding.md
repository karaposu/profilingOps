## Vocabulary

- **Self-Focus (committed sense)** — the corpus's own term, from this finding's parent lineage: attention on one's own priorities *whose resolution doesn't depend on the counterparty's response* (outcome-independence); the Self-Positioning gate's internal axis; negative pole Supplication.
- **Self-observation** — awareness of self as object (the psychology construct); the reframe finding's explicitly *excluded look-alike* — not Self-Focus.
- **The standing self-claim** — this finding's mapping of the user's "ego": a stance-belief about oneself — "I am someone whose judgment/direction others should take" — held at **standing** claim-scope (a durable property-claim, not a momentary one), whose enactment is **position-occupancy**: taking the offered slot, claiming it when contested, bearing its maintenance costs. A named configuration of committed objects (stance-belief + claim-scope + role-occupancy) — no new machinery.
- **Occurrent vs standing advice** — a good answer in a conversation (a claim scoped to the moment) vs "I am your advisor" (a durable role-claim). The wedge the friend case turns on.
- **Grant × claim** — occupancy is bilateral: the slot must be *offered* (granted) and *taken* (claimed). Grant-without-claim = the friend; claim-without-grant = the try-hard.
- **Claim-cover** — person-following means accepting the leader's standing claim *over you* in some domain; their claim must cover yours there.
- **The consistency-resistance gradient** — the stress-tested survivor of the user's rule (§5): cover-acceptance against one's own standing self-claim meets resistance proportional to the scope-gap — a gradient (high-cost, rare, possible), not a law.
- **Term-attachment** — following routed through a shared term (`40`'s two-clause criterion: outside the receiver; generates content) — a mission, a product, a body of work, or an *institutional* term (an office). The follower's ego subordinates to the term, not the person.

### 5 · The rule, stress-tested: what survives of "ego eats ego"

Reformulated: person-following means accepting the leader's standing claim over you — and the follower's own standing self-claim is a *held position* carrying consistency-accrual. The surviving form is the **consistency-resistance gradient**: *cover-acceptance against one's own standing claim meets resistance proportional to the claim-scope gap.* Not a law — a gradient: the proud can convert, at high belief-revision cost, rarely. The folk observation ("will not be followed") is the gradient's typical outcome. Five scope-conditions bound it:

1. **Claims are scoped, not sized.** The ordering runs per-domain — a lattice, not a line: the strategy-scoped leader leads bigger-egoed specialists whose claims live elsewhere (the general's chef out-egos the general about cooking); **the vizier** holds a counsel-scoped claim under the throne-claim; leadership teams work by *complementary scoping* (one covering claim per domain — colliding same-domain co-founder claims grind). The linear ego-scale is dead twice over.
2. **Term-attachment escapes entirely.** The follower's ego subordinates to the shared term, not the person; the leader borrows the term's bigness. **The prescriptive product: to lead people with big claims, get a bigger term, not a bigger ego.**
3. **Compliance needs no ordering** — power compels regardless (case 2's elites).
4. **The claim must be backed at its scope.** Inflation manufactures performed importance — it wins short-term, decays on the mimicry arc, and the backing channel reads it. The rule cannot be run as "grow your ego."
5. **The follower-side restated:** your person-attached followers' standing claims sit under yours *in the followed domain*; your term-attached followers can out-claim you personally, without limit.

**Verdict:** the universal form ("can ONLY follow more ego") is **killed** (conditions 1–3 are three counterexample families). The **claim-cover gradient survives** for person-attached, domain-matched followership — entering the corpus as a structural derivation (from M4 + claim-scope + the two-clause criterion), stamped uncalibrated. The user caught something real; its honest form is smaller and stronger than its first statement.
