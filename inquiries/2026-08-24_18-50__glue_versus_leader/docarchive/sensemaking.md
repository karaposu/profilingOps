## Phase 4 *(protocol Phase 3)* — Ambiguity Collapse

### A1 — Is "glue" one concept?

**Strongest counter:** one concept — the person who makes the group stick, however they do it; splitting it is analytic fussiness.
**Why the counter fails (structural):** the statement's own two uses have different relata (*to a purpose/outcome* in the opening; *to each other* in the Sid case), and the two relations run different mechanisms — shared-term access versus co-attention manufacture. The corpus's kind-test is mechanism-distinctness (P5): two mechanisms, two roles. And the two have opposite removal-behavior (KI6) — one concept cannot have both signatures.
**Confidence: HIGH.** **Resolution:** glue is two-place ambiguous; both relations named. **Fixed:** the split. **No longer allowed:** "the glue" as one role. **Depends on this:** every downstream verdict.

### A2 — Is the glue a leader?

**Strongest counter:** connectors DO get followed — charismatic hosts accumulate followings, so the Sid-type glue can be a leader and the split's "not a leader" verdict overreaches.
**Why the counter fails (structural):** when a connector is followed, the object being pursued is their social *production become shareable term* (the scene, the salon) — which is the to-purpose configuration mounted on top of the to-each-other one (KI15). The followership arrives exactly when, and only when, an external shareable term appears — confirming the criterion's clause rather than breaking it.
**Confidence: HIGH.** **Resolution:** per relation — the to-purpose gluer IS the leader-function's holder; the to-each-other gluer is vital-not-leader (derived, KI5); the roles can co-occur. **Fixed:** per-relation verdicts. **No longer allowed:** a single yes/no to "is the glue a leader."
