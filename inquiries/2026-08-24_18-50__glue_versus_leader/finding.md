## Question

From the inquiry's framing document, two questions arrived in one spoken statement:

> *"should I be the glue, or to be a leader, you need to be something different, something else?"* — with the Ice Age case: Sid, the mammoth, and the tiger, where Sid's constant talk makes a team of animals who *"don't have common things,"* yet *"that doesn't make Sid a leader… It just makes it kind of like a vital part of the group. And I think it is an important distinction to make."*
>
> *"What makes a leader a leader? And what makes people to follow this leader?"* — with three offered claims under a closing *"I guess people maybe understand this. I'm not sure"*: (a) a leader can be harsh and careless of people yet effective — Steve Jobs; (b) the leader is *"automatically"* whoever brings investment, becomes the public face, takes the managing burden; (c) a company with a good CEO and mediocre technical people blooms, while a great technical founder with a bad CEO fails.

The statement carries an internal tension the analysis had to handle rather than hide: its opening asserts that a good glue *is* a good leader ("if you're a good glue… this means you're a good leader"), while the Sid passage asserts the opposite. Both are the user's own words.

**The goal:** for the glue item, a role-adjudication — the glue-leader relation decided or reconciled — with a conditional personal direction; for the leader item, a definition-plus-mechanism account with per-claim verdicts on the three offered claims. At the **Meaning** layer, with two standing bounds: the theory is per-dyad (its own open items say group dynamics are *"not modeled"* — all group claims here are compositions of dyads), and it has **no outcome term** (attachment is generated by beliefs about a person, never by results).

Background for a reader new to this material: APT (Attachment & Presentation Theory) models how one person becomes attached to another — four belief-variables that add (Charm, which includes competence; Hope; Fear; Resonance, the shared-world-model variable) gated by three multiplicative modulators. The corpus already has a leadership account (see Changes from Prior) and a three-way distinction of engagement: **followership** persists and pursues; **compliance** reverses when the threat lifts; **transaction** dissolves when the exchange ends.

## Finding Summary

- **The word "glue" names two different relations, and the user's two sentences are each right about one of them.** The opening — *sticking people to "some kind of purpose, some kind of outcome"* — is **glue(people → purpose)**. The Sid case — sticking people *to each other* — is **glue(person ↔ person)**. One form, sticking-people-to-X, with two placements of X; the placement decides everything.

- **Glue(people → purpose) is the leader-function itself.** The corpus's leader is exactly the one who holds people to a shared external term — attention on a generative, shareable object, with the leader as its access-point. The user's opening sentence defined leading without noticing.

- **Glue(person ↔ person) — the Sid-function — is a different, vital, non-leader role.** Sid manufactures the co-attention on which member-member bonds form (the mammoth and tiger lack "common things" — lack Resonance; Sid's talk supplies the shared attention-objects its emergence needs). Run the corpus's two-clause criterion on this configuration and **the user's verdict comes out as a derivation**: the attention-object is the group and its members — inside the receivers — so it yields devotion and belonging **without followership**. "A vital part of the group," exactly as the user said.

- **A removal test tells the two roles apart, and both halves were already committed.** Remove the leader → the follower-follower network persists (the corpus's movements-outlive-founders claim). Remove the hub-glue → the team collapses to acquaintances (the user's own "without Sid… they will not be a team").

- **Importance and leadership are different axes.** The removal test measures load-bearing-ness; followership tracks pursuit. A glue-person can be the group's most load-bearing member and still not be followed — which is the precise content of the user's "vital part, not a leader."

- **The Jobs claim decomposes; harshness comes out compatible but not causal.** The corpus's own scenario — which names Jobs — reads the charisma as *visible absorption in a cause*, not harshness. The harshness routes as deployed anger (aimed at standards; *fear plus Task-Focus reads as respect*) and contributes a real but **reversible** component; the durable following rides the cause-channel and the admiration-channel at once. The user's "somehow he was able": the somehow is the cause-absorption.

- **"Automatically the leader" is true in the org-chart sense and false in the followership sense.** Conferred position produces compliance, which reverses when the authority is removed. The reachable content of the claim: bringing investment and being the public face is **attachment-generation in the outward receiver-population** — corpus-native (the Brand Loyalty scenario confirms the mechanism *"operates at institutional/market scale"*) — real leader-work, distinct from being followed by one's own team.

- **The CEO-versus-founder claim is outside the theory, with an in-reach shadow that serves the user.** Company bloom/fail is an outcome claim and the theory has no outcome term. What it can say: following does not require results, and competence is one additive variable of four — so leadership does not require being the best, which is the user's own bracketing ("even if you don't have extreme competence") vindicated.

- **The technical founder — the statement's third figure — gets classified too:** Task-Focus on the craft, a generative external object, so the criterion predicts **apprentice-style followership, deep and narrow** (bounded by who can read competence). Stated as the standing prediction applied to a new case, with Scenario 12 as partial evidence; the followership half remains the corpus's open test.

- **An open corpus item advances.** The belief-attributes inquiry relocated "people gatherer" to an effect and left the capacity open with two candidates — legibility (why followers cohere) and social proof (why they accumulate), both leader-side. The Sid-function is a **third capacity, member-side**: cohesion manufactured among members independent of any cause. Pure glue exists — the trio has barely a cause and no crowd.

- **The personal answer is a conditional structure, not a directive.** Wanting to create something means having a shareable external term; attention on *it* is the follower-generating configuration — so the to-purpose gluing is not an alternative to leading, it *is* leading, and it runs on attention-direction rather than extreme competence. The to-each-other gluing is a separable vital role: a Sid can be found, shared, or grown, and one person can hold both roles at once.

## Finding

### 1. Why this question was worth a full run

The corpus reached leadership three days ago and answered what following is. The user's statement probes the account from a new side: not the leader's mechanism but the *other* roles a group runs on — the connector who makes a team out of strangers, the founder whose competence nobody can read, the executive whom everybody calls the leader. And it arrives carrying its own counter-example (Sid, asserted not to be a leader) and its own self-contradiction (the opening says glue implies leader; the Sid passage says it doesn't). A statement that argues with itself is usually a statement using one word for two things. That turned out to be exactly the case.

### 2. One word, two relations

Read the statement's own two uses of "glue" with their objects attached. The opening: *"you become the glue of the people, and you are the one that sticks people to some kind of purpose, some kind of outcome."* The relation is **people → purpose**: the sticking runs from the members to a shared outcome, through you. The Sid case: the mammoth and tiger *"don't have common things so much… What makes them a team is some kind of Sid."* The relation is **person ↔ person**: the sticking runs between members, through Sid.

Same form — sticking-people-to-X — two placements of X. And the two placements run **different mechanisms**. Sticking people to a purpose is *shared-term access*: holding a term outside the group where everyone can reach it. Sticking people to each other is *co-attention manufacture*: producing the shared attention-objects (talk, activity, occasions) on which member-member alignment forms. The corpus's rule for kinds is mechanism-distinctness — two mechanisms, two roles. The strongest unification attempt was run and fails in an instructive way: if the group itself were the shared term ("us"), the second relation would reduce to the first — but "us" fails the criterion's externality clause (the term contains each receiver), which is precisely why it generates belonging instead of followership. The reduction's failure *is* the split's content.

So the user's two sentences are each right about one relation. The opening tracked glue(people → purpose). The Sid verdict tracked glue(person ↔ person). No sentence has to be withdrawn.

### 3. The to-purpose glue is the leader

Put the opening's definition next to the corpus's leadership account and they are the same shape. The corpus: a leader's attention is on a **generative, external, shareable** object (a cause, a mission, a craft); followers resonate with the object *and with each other* (a movement is a network of follower-follower pairs sharing one term); the leader is the term's **access-point** — the one toward whom access-Hope points. That is what "the one that sticks people to some kind of purpose" *is*. The user's opening sentence defined leading without noticing — which is why it felt true.

What being followed is, on the standing account this finding builds on: **followership is attachment plus pursuit that cannot extinguish**, generated Resonance-primarily by the shared term. Not the title. Not harshness. Not maximal competence — competence (Charm-content) is one additive variable of four, and the corpus's belief-attributes finding opens with people following unsuccessful men.

One caution so the split sharpens the word rather than dissolving it: "leader" retains a unique referent through all of this — **the one the pursuit tracks**. The roles below are not a dissolution of leadership into functions; they are the other functions, distinguished *from* it.

### 4. The Sid-function: derived, not debated

The mammoth and tiger "don't have common things." In the theory's vocabulary that is a Resonance-lack: Resonance is the **dyadic-emergence** variable — *"emerges when both parties' values / worldviews / attention-objects align,"* and specifically *"when both parties are in Dialogue-Focus on the same object → Resonance emerges."* Sid's ceaseless talk supplies exactly the missing input: shared attention-objects, continuously. That is the Sid-function — **co-attention manufacture** — and it is a composition of existing theory pieces (a third-party position, attention-objects, the emergence condition), not a new element.

Now run the corpus's **two-clause criterion** on this configuration — its third application, after the teacher/leader cases and the Caregiver-Focus case. The glue's attention-object is *the group and its members*. For any member, that object **includes the receiver** — it does not lie outside them. (This is a partial-inclusion object — the criterion's earlier objects were single things; a group containing the receiver still violates "lies outside the receiver," and the extension is named here rather than made silently.) The criterion's output for internal objects is the corpus's own precedent, set by Caregiver-Focus: **devotion and belonging, without followership**.

Which is the user's verdict, derived: *"It just makes it kind of like a vital part of the group."* Sid is vital and Sid is not a leader — not as an intuition the finding agrees with, but as a theorem of the criterion the corpus already owns.

Three more things complete the portrait. **Glue-ness is a property of the person × group pair**, not of the person: the same Sid is load-bearing in a taciturn group and redundant noise in a talkative one — which explains, in one stroke, how Sid can be *"annoying"* and *"vital"* in the same breath: the two readings evaluate different bearers (the person alone; the person-in-this-group). **The role has value the leader-frame hides**: what persists through change, on the corpus's two-family reading, is the Resonance family — and the glue manufactures that family's raw material. The glue is the team's durability-infrastructure, not a consolation prize; team sports even institutionalize it ("the glue guy" — a named, valued, paid, explicitly non-leader role). And **the role has a pathological variant**: connection-performance without backing — the social climber who "connects people" while the genuine object is their own position; in network terms, a *broker* who spans the gap deliberately and keeps members connected only through themselves. The corpus's mimicry apparatus transfers whole: displayed versus backed, with costly-signal decay predicting exposure.

### 5. The removal test, and the difference between important and followed

The two roles come apart behaviorally at one observable: what happens when the holder is removed.

**Remove the leader:** the follower-follower network persists — the shared term is external and still there. This is the corpus's own committed claim (*"remove the leader-node and the dyads remain"* — movements outlive founders). **Remove the hub-glue:** the team collapses to acquaintances — the connective production stops. This is the user's own committed claim (*"without Sid… they will not be a team"*). One discriminator, both halves already on the record.

The test carries a sub-structure. A glue-person can be a **hub** (all bonds run through them; nothing member-member ever formed; removal collapses everything — Sid as described) or a **catalyst** (their co-attention manufacture seeded real member-member bonds; removal leaves residue). In the chemistry the user's own metaphor comes from: an adhesive stays in the bond; a catalyst enables it and can leave. The user's word "glue" is literally the adhesive reading — their vocabulary was tracking the hub-configuration all along. The catalyst-configuration is derived here, not yet exemplified; sorting real glue-people (the office connector, the community host, the family member everyone routes through) between the two is the census this finding hands forward.

And one correction to the test itself, found by inverting it: removal-collapse has **two causes**. A founder-led project can collapse on the founder's exit not because they were the glue but because *the shared term lived in their head* — it was never made legible enough to survive them. So the test's full form is two questions: did member-member engagement persist? and **did the shared term survive the removal?** Residue → catalyst-glue. Collapse with the term surviving → glue-loss. Collapse with the term dying → legibility-failure, a different diagnosis entirely.

This yields the finding's most user-facing sentence: **importance and leadership are different axes.** Load-bearing-ness (what the removal test measures) can max out — in a termless group, the glue-holder is the most load-bearing member there is — while followership stays zero, because there is nothing external being pursued. "Vital part of the group, not a leader" names two real axes at once, and the user had already separated them correctly.

### 6. The Jobs claim: harsh, followed — and the two facts are not connected the way they look

The statement offers Jobs as evidence that a leader can be harsh and careless of people. Decomposed with three corpus instruments, at the level of the case-class the corpus carries (the actual biography is not testable from inside this project):

**First, the corpus already read this case, and read it the other way.** Scenario 9 of the April spec — which names Jobs alongside Gandhi, Mandela, and King — says: *"Their charisma doesn't come from narcissistic self-absorption. They're visibly absorbed in a cause / mission / vision larger than themselves."* The followership channel is the cause-absorption, not the interpersonal manner. The user's own phrase *"somehow he was able to bring some outcomes"* marks the mechanism as unknown; this is the somehow.

**Second, the harshness routes as deployed anger, which earns rather than costs.** The corpus's anger routing: anger aimed at a specific standard, terminating, with composure intact — *fear plus Task-Focus — reads as respect* (versus leaked anger, which reads as exhausting, and performed anger, which reads as fake). And the respected-volatile-figure template says the two channels run **at once**: deference from fear AND admiration from charm. Harshness is compatible with leading. It contributes. But its contribution is the **reversible** part: fear-driven engagement carries the Coerced signature — *"reverses (relief, not attachment) on threat removal."* When the harsh boss loses the power to hurt, that component evaporates; what persists is the Resonance family — the cause-alignment. The durable following was never made of the harshness.

**Third, "his people" were never one population.** Scenario 9's own trio — *"followers / employees / supporters"* — splits across the corpus's three engagement senses: devoted followers (followership: cause-Resonance plus access-Hope), employees (a per-person **mixture** of compliance and transaction under conferred position, with mission-resonance running higher for some — early-team devotion is real), and supporters who never met the man (cause-Resonance at the network's far edge, no dyad with him at all). The harshness landed mostly on the population whose engagement was most mixed — which is why "harsh yet followed" can both be true without harshness explaining the following.

**Verdict on claim (a):** a leader *can* be harsh — the corpus agrees, and supplies the earning form (deployed, standards-aimed). But the harshness is not what makes people follow; the cause-channel is. Warmth is not the requirement the user feared it was; neither is harshness the trick it might look like.

### 7. The "automatically the leader" claim: true, false, and the real work it points at

*"The leader is automatically the person who manages, becomes the public face, takes this burden."* The claim forks on the sense of "leader."

**Org-chart sense: trivially true, and empty** — it restates the title.

**Followership sense: false, by a standing commitment.** Conferred position supplies attachment-variable content and institutional gating, *"whether threat or reward"* — and does **not** supply a generative external object, *"which is why it produces no followership."* Deference to the office reverses when the office is removed; that is compliance's signature, not followership. Holding the position does not make you the one the pursuit tracks.

**The reachable content, which is real:** bringing investment and being the public face is **attachment-generation in the outward receiver-population** — investors, press, public as receivers. This is not an extension this finding invents: the Brand Loyalty scenario (Apple, Rolex, Hermès) commits that the attachment mechanism *"operates at institutional/market scale, not just individual interpersonal."* The outward-facing role's distinctive work is generating attachment in people who will never be in the room — a different job from being followed by one's own team, and a real one. The burden/responsibility component has no mechanism home anywhere in the theory; that absence is flagged rather than papered over.

### 8. The CEO-versus-founder claim: the boundary, and the shadow that serves the user

*"Good CEO with mediocre tech blooms; great founder with bad CEO fails."* This is a claim about **company outcomes**, and the theory has **no outcome term** — attachment is generated by beliefs about a person, never by that person's results; no element models funding, markets, or blooming. The finding does not manufacture a verdict: the claim is outside the theory's reach, and saying so is the honest answer.

Two things are in reach, and both serve the user directly. **Following does not require results** — the corpus's belief-attributes finding is built on people following unsuccessful men, and its no-outcome-term derivation ("decisions don't have to be correct") is the same architecture speaking. And **competence is one additive variable of four** — a person weak in Charm's competence-content can still generate attachment through Hope and Resonance with intact modulators. The user bracketed competence twice ("even if you don't have extreme competence"); the theory's answer is that the bracketing is sound — leadership's mechanism never ran on being the best.

One bridge is carried at reduced confidence rather than asserted, because every one of its appearances traces to a single imported idea: that competence is *illegible* to most receivers (deep technical Charm lands only on those who can read it), and that the outward-facing role's work is partly **making value legible**. It surfaced three times in this inquiry (the outward-function's substance; leading partly delegable to a legible term; term-loss collapses) — but all three descend from one origin, so the convergence is not independent evidence. It stays a named open question at medium confidence.

### 9. The technical founder — the statement's third figure — classified

The founder's attention is on the **craft** (Task-Focus). The craft is a generative, external object — so the two-clause criterion **predicts followership**: the apprenticeship kind, deep and narrow, bounded by who can read competence. This is not a new claim; it is the leadership finding's own open prediction applied to the statement's own figure. What is new is the evidence-state: **Scenario 12** (audiences attach deeply to performers absorbed in the music) supplies the *attachment* half from the corpus's own spec — which also corrects the prior finding's "no such case" sentence (see Changes from Prior). The *followership* half — pursuit that cannot extinguish, the master-and-apprentice case — remains the open test, now with its shape supplied.

Put together, the finding's four classified roles form a small table — presented as a classification summary, extending the corpus's existing artifact-direction (the 2×2 diagnostic's mechanism-axes-to-folk-labels form):

| Role | Attention on | Generates | Removal signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Leader** | a shareable external term (cause/mission) | followership (Resonance-primary + access-Hope), inward and outward | network persists — the term is still there |
| **Adhesive glue (hub)** | the group and its members | belonging / devotion, member-side cohesion | team collapses to acquaintances |
| **Catalyst glue** | the group and its members | member-member bonds that outlive the manufacture | residue persists |
| **Technical founder** | the craft | predicted: deep-narrow apprentice-followership (attachment half evidenced; followership half open) | term-loss risk if the craft-vision lived only in their head |

(The strategic **broker** — the displayed-glue's network face — is the hub run deliberately; it enters the table when the displayed-variant is worked.) Every row carries its removal-signature so the table stays testable; the group-level composition behind all of it is dyads only, per the theory's own ceiling.

### 10. What this gives back to the corpus

The belief-attributes finding relocated "being a people gatherer" from a leader-attribute to an **effect in the follower group**, and deferred the leader-side capacity with two candidates: *"Legibility explains why followers **cohere**; social proof explains why they **accumulate**. The residual stays open with two candidates rather than one."* This finding adds a third capacity the two candidates missed — both of them are leader-side (about the cause and the crowd), while the Sid-function is **member-side**: cohesion manufactured among members, independent of any cause. The Ice Age trio is nearly a pure case — barely a cause, no crowd, full cohesion. The open item's candidate-set advances from two to three; the item itself stays open (this is an amendment, not a resolution). Separately, the glue-function is the strongest demand yet recorded for the theory's twice-deferred group-level machinery (the group-dynamics extension; dyadic-level modulators) — those open items now have a customer with a worked case.

### 11. So — should you be the glue?

The question's "or" partially dissolves: **the to-purpose gluing is not an alternative to leading — it is leading.** What remains is a conditional structure, not a directive:

- Wanting to create something means you already have the candidate shared term: the thing you want to create. Attention visibly on *it* — absorption in the work rather than in extracting anyone's approval — is the follower-generating configuration, and it runs on attention-direction, not on extreme competence.
- The to-each-other gluing is a different, vital, separable role. A Sid can be found, shared, or grown — and the annoying/vital duality means a group's own Sid may well read as annoyance from inside while being the reason the team exists.
- One person can hold both roles at once: the followed connector is someone whose social production has itself become a shareable term (the scene, the salon, the journey).
- Your own distinction was right, and it is now a theorem: the Sid-glue is a vital part of the group and not a leader — because what it attends to is the group itself, and what a leader attends to is something the whole group can reach.

Two graded riders, marked as extensions beyond the corpus's current statics: the roles may also be *sequenced* (glue-first to assemble the team; the created thing matures into the term; leading emerges), and if the corpus's recent pattern holds, the trainable quantities the companion question will ask about are likely to be **relational displays, not intrinsic traits** — four analyses in a row have landed on pair-predicated properties. This finding is the foundation that queued companion question (which framework quantities to train) consumes next; its strongest handoff is the legibility question, because legibility-work is document-shaped — a technical person's home terrain.
