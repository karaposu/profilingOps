## Question

From this inquiry's framing document, the object is a numbered block quotation of prior assistant output — item "4." of a list whose other items were not supplied — followed by two sentences of the user's own.

> *"The elephant is model-completeness: how much of a person the observer has finished figuring out. Some background, for a reader coming to this cold. **APT** — Attachment & Presentation Theory — is the model this project uses to read how attached one person is to another from what they say. It computes attachment as `f(charm, hope, fear, resonance)` — four variables that add together — multiplied by three **modulators** that gate the result. Getting into the modulator group requires passing eight stated criteria; the theory has turned candidates away on them before.

**Model-completeness** is a factor identified a day before this question was asked: how much of a person the observer has finished figuring out. Its job in the theory is to explain why **pursuit** ends — you stop chasing someone once you have finished working them out. It was tested against two homes, rejected from both, and the passage above turns that double rejection into a positive claim: it is a **fifth architectural category**, sitting beside the four kinds of thing the theory contains.

The question asks how to add that fifth category to the model, and gates the request on whether the account is still current. The inquiry declared **Meaning** as its working layer — *what kind of thing this is* — with the spec-writing (Structural) and the detection procedure (Process) deliberately out of scope and scheduled after.

## Finding

### 1 · Why this was asked, and what the short answer is

A day before this question, a finding identified a factor the theory needed and did not have a name for: **model-completeness**, how much of a person the observer has finished figuring out. It explains why pursuit stops. It was tested against two possible homes in the theory and rejected from both. The finding then said what it was — *"a cross-variable epistemic modifier"* — and said where to put it.

The question quoted that account back and asked how to add it. And it added a hedge: *"ofc if this is still relevant."*

The hedge turned out to be the more important half. **The account is current. The place it was told to go does not exist.**

That is the whole answer, and everything below is the evidence for it and the four corrections it required.

---

### 2 · What the source actually said, in two sentences that pull apart

The passage is a compression of section 5 of `inquiries/2026-08-21_16-35__coherence_unpredictability_paradox/finding.md`. Almost every phrase maps onto it, including *"cross-variable epistemic modifier"* and the four-item list.

It diverges at one point, and the two sentences that diverge sit next to each other in the source.

The **claim**: *"That is a genuinely different kind of thing from the four the theory currently recognises — variable, sub-flavour, modulator, and documented degradation factor."*

The **instruction**, immediately following: *"It should be documented alongside Vitality, in the space the theory reserves for factors that are real and consequential but do not qualify as modulators — with the difference that this one governs pursuit rather than degrading signal."*

*A different kind of thing from four things* and *a fifth thing beside four things* are not the same claim. The source's action item repeats the instruction, not the claim — *"Document model-completeness as a cross-variable epistemic modifier, in the space the theory reserves for real-but-non-qualifying factors."* So the instruction is the position the source acted on.

It is worth being fair about this. The source's own wording is ambiguous, and the passage is not misreading it so much as picking up one of two things it says. But only one of the two was carried into an action item, and only one is a claim the corpus can be said to hold.

And there is a plain counter-example to the escalation inside the corpus already: **Vitality is also a different kind of thing from the four, and Vitality did not become a fifth category.** It was put in a space.

### 5 · There is no set of four to be fifth to

Set aside the missing container for a moment. Even if the space existed, the request's arithmetic does not work, because the four things it counts are not four comparable things.

**Variable and modulator are two values of one axis.** A variable is a term that adds into `f`; a modulator is a gate that multiplies the result. One test separates them — *can this generate attachment with the other terms at zero?* — and a thing is one or the other. Nothing can be both.

**Sub-flavour is not on that axis at all.** It is a decomposition **inside** a variable: `inquiries/2026-05-01_12-30__hope_sub_flavors/finding.md` divides Hope into seven, and specifies them as a **basis** rather than a partition, so a single Hope-instance carries several at once. Hope is a variable *and* has sub-flavours. That combination is fine, and it is exactly what "variable" and "modulator" cannot do together.

**Degradation factor is not a kind of thing at all — it is a space.** And the corpus says so in its most explicit formulation, from `inquiries/2026-08-21_22-06__compliance_versus_attachment/finding.md`, proposing a placement of its own: *"Place behaviour-under-threat-with-zero-attachment in the space the theory reserves for real-but-non-qualifying factors, alongside receiver availability and Vitality, as a **residual placement rather than a category assignment**."*

So the four sit at three levels: two values of one axis, one decomposition-within, and one space. *A fifth alongside these four* is an argument from parallelism, and the parallelism is what is not there.

**One thing this is not.** The list is not a mistake. It is a perfectly reasonable inventory of the kinds of thing the theory talks about, and someone assembling it from the corpus would produce something like it. What it cannot do is license the move it is being used to license.

### 6 · And the space is a residual, which admits nothing

Is that space a category? It has a name, members, and a document calling it *"Different architectural category."* Three things say no.

**Its occupants have no shared structural role.** Look at the table in section 3: Vitality **degrades** the signal, receiver availability **gates** whether anything starts, coerced behaviour is an **output** with nothing behind it, and model-completeness **ends** pursuit. Four different structural roles. Compare the three modulators, which look heterogeneous in content — a stance, a consistency, a physiology — and are **identical** in role: each multiplies the same product, each has a dual internal-and-displayed character, each has a failure signature.

That contrast was tested rather than asserted. The strongest available counter-arrangement is a lifecycle: entry, continuation, degradation, exit around the formula. It fails on two of the four. Receiver availability and coerced-behaviour-with-no-attachment are **both** pre-process, so they collide at entry; and Vitality's threshold-switching is a property of its response curve, not a position in a lifecycle. The four share only *real, and not in the formula* — which is a negative.

**It states no admission criteria, and that is not an oversight.** Modulators have eight. Hope sub-flavours have a named test. This has none, because **nothing is admitted to a remainder** — things are left over into it.

**And the corpus's own most recent word for placing something there is *"a residual placement rather than a category assignment."***

### 7 · No category question arises anyway

There is a second, independent route to the same verdict, and it comes from the passage's own sentence: *"a property of how completely you know the other terms, **rather than being a term itself**."*

A **property**. And the immediately prior finding in this corpus, `inquiries/2026-08-22_06-09__the_never_named_layer/finding.md`, established that properties in this theory are **had, not admitted** — the corpus lists *distinct failure signatures* among the architectural properties every modulator has, and no gate is owed to a property.

So if it is a property, *which category does it constitute* is the wrong question and *a new fifth one* is the wrong answer.

**The scope of that argument needs stating, because unscoped it proves too much.** It does not dissolve placement questions in general — the theory has answered several correctly by placing things. What it dissolves is **admission**: the need for a gate. What it leaves standing is **attachment** — what does this property attach to? That question is answerable, and the answer is: **an attachment type's belief state.** Each of the five attachment types has one, and how far it has filled is what model-completeness measures.

The two routes are independent. The arithmetic in section 5 fails whether or not model-completeness is a property; the property argument holds whether or not the four are peers.

### 8 · The two rejections stand; three of their reasons do not

The passage gives three reasons across two rejections. Two of the three misreport their sources, and this matters because the corrections are checkable and the reasons were not.

**"It fails generate-alone."** *Generate-alone is not one of the eight modulator entry criteria.* The eight, as `apt_modulator_landscape` applies them to Coherence and to Emotional Composure, are: multiplicative on attachment · dual character · orthogonal to Self-Positioning · orthogonal to the other new modulator · not reducible to a species · not reducible to an adjacent mechanism · observable signals · a distinct failure signature.

The corpus does ask a generate-alone question, and it runs the other way. It is the **multiplicative-versus-additive test**: *"Can Resonance generate attachment WITHOUT any Charm/Hope/Fear present?"* A thing that **can** generate alone is thereby a **variable**, which is exactly how Resonance was classified. A modulator is not supposed to generate alone — being a multiplicative gate is what that means. So the argument runs backwards.

**"Vitality was excluded on the same pair."** It was excluded on one ground. `apt_modulator_landscape` states it plainly: Vitality is *"a threshold/switching factor"* — additive in the normal range, collapsing at clinical extremes — and *"Modulators in APT's architecture operate smoothly multiplicatively across their range. Threshold-switching behavior doesn't qualify."* One criterion.

**The modulator rejection survives on that same criterion, correctly applied.** Saturating model-completeness does not zero attachment; it only ends the chase. That is not smooth multiplicative gating, and it is the criterion that excluded Vitality.

**"It dies on the H_pleasure precedent."** This one is not wrong, but it is doing something different from what it appears to do. The precedent is quoted correctly: `hope_sub_flavors` killed a proposed eighth Hope sub-flavour on the grounds that pleasure is *"a cross-variable affective specification, not a sub-flavor,"* routing it to whichever variable carries the pleasure. The passage's parallel is clean — *discovery of what?* Their competence resolves to Charm, their values to Resonance, their regard for you to Validation-Hope.

But the kill's own attached note bounds the pattern: *"**affective valence** specifications cut across variables; future inquiries on cross-variable **affective** specifications (joy, comfort, excitement) should anticipate this pattern."* The author named three affective successors. Model-completeness is epistemic. So this is **reasoning by analogy from a precedent whose author scoped it more narrowly**, not applying the precedent — and the analogy is a good one, which is why the verdict stands at medium confidence rather than falling.

**One more thing, unflattering and true.** *Seven of the eight criteria have never been applied to this candidate.* The criteria are conjunctive, so failing one disqualifies and the verdict holds. But the corpus ran all eight on Coherence and on Emotional Composure and wrote *"All criteria passed."* This candidate received a different treatment from the ones that were admitted, and a reader should know that.

### 9 · Where it actually sits: an adjacency, not a home

The negatives are settled. Something positive is owed, and there is one — smaller than this inquiry first thought.

`inquiries/2026-05-01_12-00__apt_as_belief_theory/finding.md` (the theory's iteration-3.4) reframed APT as a theory of which receiver-side beliefs generate attachment, and in doing so divided its material by **role relative to the formula**: things that are inputs to it, things that are **belief-update mechanisms**, and things that are pre-conditions. That partition is not one of the four the passage lists.

The belief-update group has two members with stated roles: **Signal Specificity** is *"the evidence-quality measure for belief-update"*, and **θ(context)** is *"the channel-specific cutoff below which evidence does not count for update."*

Model-completeness looks like it belongs there. It is a statement about the belief-forming process rather than about the sender.

**It does not quite belong there, and the reason is worth more than the placement would have been.** Both existing members are properties of **incoming evidence** — how good is this signal, is it above the cutoff. Model-completeness is a property of **the accumulated state** — how much of the person has been worked out.

This inquiry's middle stages argued that `f` is *"a cumulative belief state"* and therefore has an extent for completeness to be an extent of. Reading that sentence in full defeats it: *"f is a cumulative belief state. The four attachment variables represent **the receiver's current belief about the sender**, formed from in-interaction signals AND from prior evidence… This is a clarification, not a new formula term."* **Cumulative** there means **history-dependent** — accumulated from evidence over time. What accumulates is a belief's strength. Nothing in the sentence gives `f` a domain to be complete over.

So the honest statement is: **model-completeness is adjacent to the belief-update machinery and is not a member of it, because every member is a property of incoming evidence and this is a property of the accumulated state — a distinction the theory has not drawn.** Drawing that distinction is what naming the factor would actually accomplish.

**A worked case shows the distinction is real rather than verbal.** `inquiries/2026-08-21_18-37__leadership_and_inexhaustibility/finding.md` treats the deep teacher: someone perfectly consistent and nonetheless inexhaustible. Their prior is maximally firm and the model of them is nowhere near finished. Firmness and extent come apart in the theory's own example.

**And one clarification no finding has stated in one place.** The quantity is **receiver-side** — *"a property of the receiver's model of the sender."* Its **ceiling** is **sender-side** — *"you do not become inexhaustible by being deep. You become inexhaustible by attaching your attention to something that does not run out."* Both are in the same finding, deliberately. The quantity and its bound sit on opposite sides, and that is why the belief-update neighbourhood is right for the quantity without the attention-object having to move there.

**A useful relation falls out.** Signal Specificity measures how good a piece of evidence is; model-completeness measures how much of the person the evidence has reached. Those vary independently — the deep-teacher case shows it — and it is the only stated relation between something inside the formula and something in the outside-the-formula practice.

Naming the container as the blocker is a Meaning-layer result. Writing it is the next inquiry's work, and the crossing is stated here rather than made.
