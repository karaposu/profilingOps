## Question

Attachment & Presentation Theory (referred to below as "the theory") models how one person becomes attached to another. Four **variables** generate attachment by addition — charm, hope, fear, resonance. Three **modulators** gate the result by multiplication. One modulator, **Self-Positioning**, tracks *non-extractive attention* — attention not aimed at harvesting approval from the person in front of you. It has six **species** (Self-Focus, Caregiver-Focus, Mission-Focus, Task-Focus, Dialogue-Focus, Transcendent-Focus) and a catalog of five **display signals** by which observers read it.

The user's input carried two separable questions.

**The first: what generates belief in a leader?** He observed that people follow unsuccessful men — belief decoupled from results — and named five attributes: being competent; being a people gatherer or merger; showing aggression toward a goal; being able to make decisions, *"they dont have to be correct"*; and being able to bail out of bad decisions, having a *"manuuvear allowing thinking style."* He then asked how world leaders who are *"really narcistic and self focused"* gather so many people behind them.

**The second: why is there no extremely soft famous leader?** He asserted a clear pattern, said *"People rewward correct aggressivenss,"* and extended it: *"same even in dating, (not aggressivenss but being masculan) woman like masculan man."*

## Finding Summary

- **The five attributes are receiver-side inferences, not leader properties.** The theory models beliefs an observer forms. That distinction turns out to explain the sharpest thing in the question.

- **They land on four different layers.** Competence is a variable's belief-content. Decisiveness and bail-out are display signals. Goal-aggression is a cross-layer composite. People-gathering is **not an attribute at all** — it is an effect in the follower group.

- **"They dont have to be correct" is derivable, and it is the strongest result here.** Decisiveness is read at the modulator layer, which represents *attention-direction* only. The theory has **no outcome term anywhere.** Deciding from your own premise displays non-extraction whether the decision is good or bad.

- **Two of the five attributes are one property.** Deciding without needing to be right and abandoning a decision without defending it are behaviourally opposite and identical underneath: both display **non-extraction toward your own prior commitment**.

- **The narcissist question is not a puzzle, and the corpus's two answers are not rivals.** The genuine species is Self-Focus; the mission is a **displayed overlay**. The difference is whether the display is **backed**.

- **That answers "so many."** A movement needs followers to resonate with *each other*, which needs a shareable object. A narcissist's real object — himself — is not shareable. **The displayed mission is, and display is sufficient.**

- **And it predicts the decay shape.** The mission display erodes as mimicry is detected; the Self-Focus persists. The following **contracts** rather than collapsing.

- **The softness claim is false as stated and true when narrowed.** Gentleness is *orthogonal* to the modulator axis. What fails to reach leadership is not gentleness but a **display that reads as Supplication** — hedging, over-explaining, self-justification.

- **"Correct aggressiveness" already has a name**, from one inquiry ago: **deployed** anger. And *correct* is descriptive, not moral.

- **The two extensions get opposite verdicts.** The dating parallel restates a verdict the corpus shipped in iteration-3 — **but the user's framing names a component that verdict left unnamed.** The gender framing adds nothing causal, and the corpus's own dating case shows why.

- **The theory names one leader attribute the user missed** — comfort with silence — and one it makes *ambivalent*, which is probably why he omitted it.

## Finding

### Why the question has an unusual shape

The user did not ask a theoretical question. He listed things he has noticed, in the vocabulary people actually use — *competent*, *decisive*, *soft*, *masculine* — and asked what they amount to. That is the normal way anyone arrives at a theory, and it exposed something about the theory itself, which is addressed in section 7.

### 1. The attributes are inferences, not properties

The theory models **receiver-side beliefs**: which beliefs about another person, when held, generate attachment. It has no layer in which a leader's properties exist independently of being read.

So the five items are not things a leader *has*. They are things an observer *concludes*. That sounds like a technicality and it is the key to the whole answer, because an observer concludes them from **display** — and display does not require the underlying thing to be true.

### 2. Where each one lands

| Attribute | Lands on | Which is |
|---|---|---|
| **being competent** | Charm's belief-content — *"this person is high-status / competent / impressive"* | a variable |
| **showing aggression toward a goal** | Fear at high Signal Specificity, displayed under Task-Focus, composure intact | a cross-layer composite |
| **being able to decide** | **Premise-Posture** | a display signal |
| **being able to bail out** | **Exit-Willingness** + low **Self-Justification-Density** | display signals |
| **being a people gatherer** | — | **not an attribute; an effect** |

Five items, four layers. That is the answer to the list-level question: **no single taxonomy could hold them**, because they are not the same kind of thing. The theory has variables, species, signals, and an adjacent-mechanism list — and folk trait-language cuts across all of them.

**People-gathering is the odd one.** It is not something a leader has; it is something that happens among followers. Section 5 gives its mechanism, and section 8 records what remains unknown about the leader's side of it.

### 3. Why correctness does not matter — the derivation

This is the sharpest observation in the question, and the theory produces it.

Decisiveness is read at the **modulator** layer. That layer represents one thing: **the direction of attention**. Someone who decides from their own premise, rather than seeking permission, is displaying attention that is not aimed at harvesting your approval. Whether the decision is *good* is not represented there — the theory contains **no outcome term at all**.

That is not an oversight. Attachment in this theory is generated by beliefs about a person, never by that person's results.

So when the user wrote *"they dont have to be correct,"* he was reporting an observation that the architecture predicts.

**Correctness does act — later, and elsewhere.** A leader whose decisions are consistently wrong eventually stops reading as competent, which lowers **Charm**; and if his account of himself stops matching what happens, **Coherence** collapses. Both are different components on a slower timescale. Neither touches the decisiveness signal.

*One caution, stated because the point is rhetorically attractive.* It is tempting to run this backwards and treat the user's observation as **evidence for** the no-outcome architecture. It is — but weakly. It is one unmeasured lay observation matching a structural choice made earlier for unrelated reasons. That is ordinary consilience, not confirmation.

### 4. Two attributes are one property

Deciding without needing to be right, and abandoning a decision without defending it, look like opposites. Underneath they are the same thing: **non-extraction with respect to your own prior commitment.**

The decider is not extracting permission from others. The bail-outer is not extracting vindication from his own past. Someone displaying both is showing that attachment-to-outcome is absent in *both* directions, which is exactly why the pair is diagnostic rather than contradictory.

**One precision matters.** The theory keeps Premise-Posture, Exit-Willingness and Self-Justification-Density as **separate entries** in its display catalog, and this finding does not propose merging them. The catalog is a **detection list** — it enumerates distinguishable observables. Two observables can index one underlying property, and that is what is being claimed.

### 5. The narcissist case, and why the corpus's two answers are both right

The user asked how *"really narcistic and self focused"* leaders gather so many people. His phrasing was more accurate than it looks — this corpus deliberately places narcissists inside the technical Self-Focus species: *"narcissistic attention **IS** self-focused … They fit the Self-Focus species **mechanically**."*

The corpus contains **two accounts** of these figures, neither citing the other. Iteration-3 says they are Self-Focus with five adjacent mechanisms amplifying. The immediately prior finding says they run **Mission-Focus as mimicry** over a self-aggrandising object.

**Both hold, at different layers.** The **genuine** species is Self-Focus — attention on their own greatness, exactly as iteration-3 describes. The **displayed** species is Mission-Focus, and the prior finding is right to call that mimicry.

**The distinction is *backing*, not visibility.** Both species are displayed — iteration-3 names Self-Focus's own signals (talking about self, selective engagement, not optimising for approval). What separates them is whether the display is backed by the thing it claims: the Self-Focus display is backed by attention that really is there; the mission display is not backed by the stated object.

**And this answers "so many."** Self-Focus generates attachment one observer at a time. A *movement* requires followers to resonate with **each other**, which requires a **shareable** object. A narcissist's real object — himself — cannot be shared. **His displayed mission can, and the display is sufficient**, because followers resonate with the cause as they understand it, not with his private object.

**It also predicts the decay's shape.** The mission display is mimicked, so it erodes as the unpaid cost is detected — the corpus's existing costly-signal arc. The Self-Focus is genuine, so it persists. The prediction is therefore **partial decay**: the following contracts toward a smaller, more personal core rather than collapsing.

### 6. Softness — false as stated, true when narrowed

*This section modifies a claim the user asserted as clear. That is stated openly rather than slipped in.*

Gentleness is **orthogonal** to the modulator axis. A gentle person may be **Caregiver-Focused** — non-extractive attention on another's wellbeing, which reads high — or **Supplicating**, which reads low. The corpus names the confusion directly: *"Supplication dressed as Caregiver-Focus."* If softness simply were Supplication, that phrase would be incoherent.

So what fails to reach leadership is **not gentleness** but a **display that reads as Supplication**: self-justification, hedging, over-explaining, permission-seeking. Gentle non-extractive figures do become famous leaders — which is why the corpus's own exemplar list contains several.

**The observation survives; only its predicate changes.** The user is seeing something real, and the narrowing explains both the pattern and its exceptions rather than waving either away.

Two supports, arriving independently. **Inside the theory:** Caregiver-Focus is gentle and reads high, so the axes are separate. **Outside it:** the standard framing of exactly this pattern in social psychology treats **agency** and **communion** as *two dimensions rather than one* — which means high-agency-and-high-communion is a real profile, and it is the profile most associated with admired leadership. **The user's soft-versus-aggressive framing is one-dimensional, and the cases that trouble it are precisely the high-both cases.**

*One honest limit:* the corpus asserts its exemplars but never characterises their interpersonal manner, so whether they display Supplication markers **cannot be tested from inside the project**.

### 7. What "correct aggressiveness" is

It already has a name, from one inquiry ago: **deployed** anger. Its signature is proportionate, aimed, terminating, non-spilling — displayed under Task-Focus, with composure intact and high Fear-specificity. Its alternatives are **leaked** anger, which collapses composure and reads as exhausting, and **performed** anger, which fails Coherence's alignment aspect and reads as *"he's putting it on."*

***Correct* is descriptive, not moral.** It is doing the work of *deployed* — a coordinate signature, with nothing in it about legitimacy.

### 8. The two extensions get opposite verdicts

**On the dating parallel: the corpus already committed to it — and the user's framing still adds something.**

Iteration-3's own inquiry asked whether the mechanism generalises across narcissist-followers, **the dating paradox**, and other phenomena, and answered yes. Its worked case: a self-focused man who does not chase generates attraction; the same man, attentive and pursuing, triggers dismissal. So *"same even in dating"* restates a shipped verdict.

**But that case is about not chasing** — pure non-extraction, with no aggression component named. The user's framing names one. *(A caution: the shipped case's own wording includes "compliance," which is engagement-state vocabulary the corpus elsewhere ties to fear-driven engagement. So the contribution is best stated as **naming a component the case left unnamed**, rather than adding one that was absent.)*

**On the gender framing: it adds nothing causal, and the corpus's own case shows why.**

The theory has no gender vocabulary at mechanism level. Its only gender content is an archived note saying the mechanism generalises while its **expression** varies by gender context — never promoted to a finding.

And the shipped dating case settles it. It holds the man's **looks and credentials constant** and produces opposite outcomes from attention-direction alone. If masculinity were doing work on the variable side, that case could not exist — the variable content is identical across its two halves.

So *masculine* most plausibly names a culturally-typical **expression-register** for non-extraction signals. Translated: *"women like masculine men"* becomes *"the display signals that read as non-extractive attention are, in this culture, coded masculine."*

**The claim survives translation with loss.** What survives is the mechanism. What is lost is any causal role for masculinity as such. What is gained is an explanation of why the pattern *looks* gendered. And it carries a prediction the corpus anticipated and never tested: **the same signals in a differently-coded register should produce the same effect.**

### 9. What the theory says he missed

The display catalog has five signals. The user's list claims three. The other two are informative.

**Rhythm-Comfort** — glossed in the corpus as *comfort with silence / pauses* — is a genuinely leader-relevant attribute the list omits.

**Withholding-Signal is the interesting one**, because the theory makes it **ambivalent**. An existing coupling rule states that withholding-mode display *"produces no evidence"* for the follower's belief that this person will attend to *them* specifically, producing a *"not for me"* read. So withholding **raises the modulator while suppressing an attachment sub-flavour** — it reads as high standing and as not-for-you at once.

That is probably why a folk list omits it. The theory can hold a double-edged item; ordinary trait-vocabulary has no slot for one.

### 10. What this exposed about the theory itself

Users arrive with folk trait-language. The theory speaks in variables, species, signals and adjacent mechanisms. Something has to bridge them.

The corpus already has bridges — **in two directions, without ever distinguishing them.**

The **2×2 diagnostic** has mechanism terms on its axes (*Self-Focus level*, *Investment-Asymmetry*) and folk labels in its cells (*Respected Expert*, *Try-Hard*). It runs **mechanism → folk label**: a naming device.

The **species-context-fit table** takes lay contexts (*cause-driven leadership*, *first-contact dating*) and assigns species. It runs **folk → mechanism**.

**Only the second is the direction a person with a trait-list needs**, and the corpus has exactly one instance of it. This finding's attribute mapping is a second.

## Reasoning

### What survived and why

**The cross-layer mapping survived a triple test.** The four display-side attributes share three properties competence does not: they are behaviours under **uncertainty**, they are **costly** to display, and they are read on the **fast** channel. Three lenses — timing, cost, uncertainty — partition the user's list at exactly the place component-assignment did, and none of them derives from that assignment.

**The narcissist layering survived its strongest objection**, which was that the visibility principle makes an internal-only species inert. It does — but both species are *displayed*. The distinction is backing, not visibility, and stating it the other way would have contradicted a committed principle.

**The softness narrowing survived because gentleness and Supplication come apart in a case the corpus itself specifies.** *"Supplication dressed as Caregiver-Focus"* is only coherent if the two are distinct.

### What was killed or corrected

**"Belief is residual rather than generated" — killed.** Proposed as the idea that you believe in whoever fails to display neediness. It fails: the theory requires variable content, and states that *any* non-extractive species **amplifies** rather than merely failing to collapse. Its seed survives as an open item — the modulator is nonetheless asymmetric.

**"The two accounts are about different cases" — killed on evidence.** Proposed to dissolve the narcissist tension by saying iteration-3 describes interpersonal narcissists while the prior finding describes political figures. Checked: iteration-3's case list explicitly includes *"charismatic political movements"* and cults; the prior finding's includes dictators, demagogues and cults. **The case sets overlap.** Killing it strengthened the layering, since the obvious alternative was tested and failed.

**"The theory predicts two attributes you missed" — corrected.** The derivation was valid for one and wrong for the other. Rhythm-Comfort is a clean omission. Withholding is **double-edged by an existing coupling rule**, so presenting it as a missing virtue would have been misleading. The corrected version explains the omission rather than treating it as an oversight.

**"The bridge already exists twice" — corrected on direction.** The 2×2's axes are mechanism terms and its cells are folk labels, so it runs the *opposite* way from what a trait-list needs. Only one existing instance runs the needed direction. Catching this prevented the finding from citing the wrong artifact as its own precedent.

**"Aggression is absent from the shipped dating case" — narrowed to "unnamed."** The case's own wording includes *compliance*, which is engagement-state vocabulary. The contribution stands; its size does not.

### The candidate that was generated because its absence was suspicious

The leader-side capacity behind people-gathering had exactly one candidate — an imported notion of making a cause legible enough for others to act on independently. A single candidate accepted without competition is the condition under which weak candidates get through, so competition was generated: **social proof** is on the corpus's own adjacent-mechanism list and requires no import at all.

Both survive, addressing different halves. Legibility explains why followers **cohere**; social proof explains why they **accumulate**. The residual stays open with two candidates rather than one.
