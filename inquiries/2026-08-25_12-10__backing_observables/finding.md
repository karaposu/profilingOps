## Vocabulary

Plain-language senses used in this document:

- **Display** — conduct shown to a counterparty that communicates an attention-state (interest, care, anger, conviction, willingness to leave). A display can be a single move, a scene, a sequence, cross-context conduct, or an artifact trail.
- **Backed display** — the shown state is actually there (`BDA/21` §5's definition, unchanged by this finding).
- **Performed display** — the shown state is put on: shown without being there.
- **Channel** — one place where backed and performed displays differ observably, together with when and where it can be read.
- **Tether** — how causally tied a channel's mark is to the state's reality. High-tether marks (costs actually paid) cannot be produced without approximating the real thing; low-tether marks (words, facial affect) can be produced freely.
- **Baseline** — knowledge of what this particular person looks like when *not* performing; reading against a baseline is contrastive and much stronger than reading a stranger.
- **Claim-scope** — what a display claims: an **occurrent** claim says the state is present *now* ("I am angry"); a **standing** claim says the state is a durable property of the person ("I care about you"). Backing is one thing; claims differ in scope; channels differ in which scope they read. This is a reading-layer distinction — it does not create two kinds of backing and it changes no definition text.
- **Frame-consent** — a context in which everyone knows the display is a performance (theater; role-expected professional warmth). Frame-consented performance deceives no one and is not the problem case.
- **Probe** — a receiver-side move that *creates* a reading-condition instead of waiting for one (asking a question whose answer cannot be pre-composed; going unresponsive to see whether the display continues).
- **Convergence** — agreement among *independent* channels read under different conditions. All confidence in this account lives in convergence; no single channel ever suffices.

## The Finding

### 1 · The verdict: backing is observable — gradedly, in expectation, never with per-instance certainty

The distinction between a backed and a performed display is **not unobservable**; it is observable the way slow-acting evidence is observable. Specific, conduct-level marks separate the two; each mark is individually defeasible and individually mimicable; but the marks fail independently, for different reasons, in different reading-conditions — so agreement across them supports a graded verdict that strengthens with time and access. What no observer gets, in any single moment, is certainty. The corpus's prior admission (the genuine/faked distinction is "currently unfalsifiable **in practice**") survives for the single short encounter and is **lifted everywhere else**: the distinction is now specified — which observations count against a backing-attribution, under which conditions they can be made, and how they combine.

### 2 · The mechanism: one root, two mark-families

Why would a backed display differ observably from a performed one at all?

**An absent state causes nothing by itself. Whatever the state would have caused, the performer must cause deliberately — and deliberate control has finite capacity.** That is the entire root. It surfaces as two families of marks:

- **Production-marks** (the display's manufacture, readable in-moment): a real state co-generates words, affect, timing, posture, and micro-reactions for free; a controller must *compose* them — with limited bandwidth (channels fall out of agreement), with feedback (the performance must monitor its reception to steer, and the monitoring is visible), and from prepared material (composed content is template-shaped and breaks off-script).
- **Consequence-marks** (the state's footprint in the world, readable over time and context): a real state causes conduct where no audience is, costs actually paid, and a lived history with the right shape; an absent state leaves those uncaused, and the controller can rarely afford to manufacture them all — the footprint is where faking approaches the price of the real thing.

### 3 · The instrument: seven channels, three reading modes, five rules

| # | Channel | What it reads | Scope read | Tether | Reading-condition | False-positive hazard |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Congruence & latency** — words/affect/timing agreeing; responses arriving at evoked speed (composed responses lag; fluency on the unrehearsable is a positive backing-mark) | production | occurrent | low | any live interaction | display-rule variation (culture, neurotype) breaks congruence with no performance present |
| 2 | **Monitoring-leakage** — attention flicking to the audience to check reception; content steering by reaction | production | occurrent | mid | audience present; watch the watcher | anxious reassurance-seeking mimics leakage (checking because the relationship matters, not to steer a con) |
| 3 | **Specificity & off-script survival** — target-generated vs template content; what happens when the interaction leaves the script | production | occurrent | mid | a perturbation occurs or is introduced | practiced-but-backed material *is* polished; polish alone is not performance |
| 4 | **Decay & load-lapse** — the display sagging under length, fatigue, cognitive load | production→consequence | occurrent→standing | mid | sustained or repeated observation | ordinary tiredness sags everything, backed or not |
| 5 | **Off-stage continuity** — the conduct persisting where the person believes no audience exists | consequence | standing | **high** | unstaged access or third-party report | privacy differences: some backed people are simply undemonstrative off-stage |
| 6 | **Cost-trail** — the advertised state's real costs actually paid (time, money, risk, forgone options), checkable in artifacts | consequence | standing | **high** | records, third parties, visible sacrifice | poverty of means: someone backed may lack the resources to pay large visible costs |
| 7 | **Sequence-shape & account-consistency** — the lived history having the shape the display claims; retellings staying stable | consequence | standing | **high** | history examinable; repeated accounts | a chaotic life breaks sequence-shape with no performance anywhere |

**Three reading modes.** *Passive observation* (wait for reading-conditions to occur); *perturbation and probe* (create them — the default probe is challenge-response: ask something specific, novel, and state-dependent that cannot be pre-composed; the aggressive form — going flat to see whether the display chases the missing feedback — sits behind the stakes-gate in §7); *forecast-check* (every display implies a conduct-forecast; log it, check it at the next contact — the cheapest high-tether read available to anyone, and the priced path of `BDA/21` §5 turned into a procedure).

**Five rules.**
1. **Convergence only.** No channel returns a verdict alone. The output is a standard met, never a certainty: *preponderance* (independent channels lean one way) → *clear-and-convincing* (multiple reading-conditions agree, at least one high-tether) → and nothing above that exists (the per-instance ceiling, stated as part of the scale).
2. **The discount.** Repeat sightings of the *same* channel are correlated evidence and count little more than one sighting. Three alignment-lapses < one lapse + one off-stage report + one cost-gap. (Dependency note: this rule's arithmetic leans on the corpus's correlated-evidence discount, whose own prediction-status is an open item — flagged, not hidden.)
3. **Tethered first.** Suspicion-order runs down the tether: check cost-trail and off-stage continuity before affect — a competent performer suppresses leakage first and attacks the high-tether channels last, because attacking them costs nearly what the real thing costs.
4. **Baseline where possible.** Channels read contrastively against the person's own unperformed conduct where a baseline exists ("he's putting it on" verdicts are baseline verdicts — the observers knew his real anger). No-baseline reading (strangers) is the weak mode; this one asymmetry explains why intimates detect easily and marks are weakest exactly where most wanted.
5. **The honest return.** When the window is short, the instrument says so: a walk-in or first meeting offers channels 1–3 only, in their weakest (no-baseline) form — the correct output is *"insufficient window: read specificity and latency, log the forecast, defer the verdict."* An instrument that always answers is broken.

**Context-cuts.** The canonical instrument is the full table; usable forms are cut from it: the **short-window form** (channels 1–3 + forecast-log + mandatory defer) and the **relationship form** (all seven, baseline-mode, cycles of forecast-checks).

### 4 · Claim-scope, the Stanislavski limit, and the boundary zone adjudicated

The display professions train **state-manufacture** — producing the *real* state on demand (`BDA/60` §5). A state-manufactured display defeats every origin-test *by actually having the state*. So the backed/performed line cannot be about how the state got there; it is about whether it is there (**presence, not origin**), and about **what the display claims**:

- The stage actor is **occurrently backed** (the manufactured state is really present — channels 1–3 rightly pass) while making **no standing claim** (frame-consent: the audience knows). No deception exists.
- The con artist using state-manufacture is also occurrently backed — which is exactly why good cons *feel real in the room* — but the con's display makes a **standing claim** (this care is a durable property, this partnership is real) that channels 5–7 will contradict. The deception lives at claim-scope, and detection lives in the consequence-marks.
- The calibrating performer is typically unbacked even occurrently — channel 2 catches the steering.

**The boundary zone (rehearsed forms) resolves in one sentence: preparation crosses into performance where the shown state is absent, not where the form was practiced.** A much-rehearsed opener delivered with the state present is a *polished backed display* (channel 3's hazard column exists precisely so polish is not misread). A practiced form run while the attention it advertises is not being paid is performance, and the production-marks will have something to say. Role-expected conduct (professional warmth, bedside manner) falls under frame-consent, not deception.

### 5 · The status verdict

The register's admission — "the narcissist account's central distinction is **currently unfalsifiable in practice**" — is superseded as follows:

- **Specified:** a standing-backing attribution is contradicted by (i) monitoring-leakage under a flat audience, (ii) off-stage discontinuity, (iii) cost-gap (advertised costs unpaid), (iv) template-match (content reused across targets), (v) forecast-miss (implied conduct not occurring at the next cycle). An occurrent-backing attribution is contradicted by channels 1–3's marks.
- **Graded:** verdicts are standards-met under convergence (rule 1), never per-instance certainty — the ceiling is structural, not a temporary shortfall.
- **Access-dependent:** reading-power grows with relationship duration, context-breadth, and perturbation-rate. In the single short encounter the old admission still holds in practice; everywhere else the distinction is now testable in structure. (Formal lag-parameters are follow-on work, not claimed here.)
- **The adversarial limit, stated plainly:** every channel is individually beatable, but beating all of them durably requires generating the state, paying the costs, maintaining the conduct unobserved, and accruing the lived history — at which point the performance has *become* the disposition. The instrument detects cheap performance well, expensive performance progressively worse, and complete performance not at all — because complete performance is backing. This is the account's outer boundary and it is not a defect: it is the mimicry decay arc's endpoint read in reverse.
- **Ecology notes (prediction-grade, stamped, unvalidated):** performance draws on one finite effort-budget, so wide-funnel operators (many simultaneous targets) should decay faster per target and lean harder on templates; and cheap performance should cluster in short-window contexts because that is where reading-power is lowest. Both are structural predictions awaiting field observation, like the corpus's other stamped predictions.

A deployed exit completes whether or not anyone follows.

### 7 · Hazards and guards

- **Every channel's false-positive column is part of the channel.** Anxiety mimics leakage; polish mimics performance; display-rule variation breaks congruence; chaotic lives break sequence-shape. Single-channel verdicts are the `BDA/31` over-reading hazard in new clothing — the convergence rule exists because of the false-positive terrain, not despite it.
- **The probe stakes-gate.** Probe intensity may escalate only with prior evidence: challenge-response (an ordinary specific question) is the default and is always available; the flat-audience probe (feedback-starvation) is reserved for high-stakes verification under existing red flags — a detected probe reads as a test and damages the relationship it was protecting.
- **The legibility dual** (for honest displayers): the same channels say how to be *readable* when backed — make costs visible, invite off-stage access, welcome perturbation rather than resisting it, keep the sequence-record, show the unpolished alongside the polished. The dual is safe to publish because every item is high-tether: faking legibility means paying the real costs.
- **Receiver-symmetry.** Reception is a display too, and the corpus already detects a performed reception: the false-license signals of `BDA/60` §10 (instant politeness-tempo yes / zero their-side specificity / zero recall at return) are channels 1, 3, and 7 applied to a buyer's "I'm convinced." Two independent derivations — budget-logic there, performance-detection here — land on the same three signals: a **convergence** that corroborates both (not a reduction of either).
