## Finding Summary

- **Where the line came from.** It is corpus content, paraphrased. The canonical row (`BDA/30` §1) says *"weakens as access to the Charm source recedes"*; it was inherited verbatim from the iteration-3.2.1 finding that first wrote the type table. The best_frame reader's key replaced *"the Charm source"* with *"the person"* — a narrowing the corpus never made.
- **The canon already disagreed with itself.** Its strongest Charm case — admiration of Mozart, a sender who is dead — has zero access of any kind, and `BDA/30` carries a standing caution that the mentor admired from a distance for years contradicts the row. The row was flagged "provisional" from the day it was written.
- **What "access" meant.** The origin's own draft glossed the row as *"weakens when Charm access is removed (celebrity retires, audience shrinks)"* — the source's **stream of evidence** ending, not the person becoming unreachable. Read that way, the row and the user's fame intuition largely agree: following a star *is* access.
- **What "weakens" meant.** The column is, by the canon's own reading, a *signature* — how the attachment looks from outside. What goes quiet at a distance is the attachment's **expression** (nothing to return to, nothing to engage with). The **belief** — "this person is impressive" — stays, stale but intact.
- **What distance actually does — three things.** It stops *maintaining* the belief (no fresh evidence), it removes *occasions* for the attachment to express, and it exposes the belief to *re-pricing* (the receiver's comparison set moves on). None of these removes the belief's ground. Only **counter-evidence** about the person does that.
- **The record keeps Charm alive without the person.** A person's work, output, public standing, and what others report are evidence that outlives access. This is the one parameter of the account: where a record exists, Charm is maintained; where none does (the ex-boss), the original row's signature reaches its maximum.
- **The fame case decomposed.** When a star retires, what dissolves is the fan's **Hope** (that the star stays present, keeps producing, might one day see them) — the corpus itself retired the celebrity case for a Hope residue. The Charm-belief freezes on the record. Scarcity can even *raise* it.
- **Disappear and return.** The same belief, **revived** — a stored evaluation brought back by fresh evidence, often boosted by scarcity, sometimes discounted by re-pricing, and gated by Coherence: the returning person must be coherent with their record.
- **The corrected row.** Cell: *"goes quiet without occasions; kept by the record; revived on return."* Row-note: the mechanism above. Same provisional flag as before.
- **For the user's own case.** Silence in outreach starves Hope and occasions, not Charm. The founder's product is the record — **grow it during absence; ship while silent.**

## Finding

### 0 · Why this was worth a full inquiry

The sentence the user challenged sat in a document meant to be readable alone. Tracing it found that it was not a slip of paraphrase only: the canonical row it came from names a cause the theory's own architecture does not support, and the canon had already logged a counter-case without explaining it. Correcting one sentence therefore meant understanding what the row was ever about. The pieces below are written so that a reader with no prior exposure can follow them; a short key of the theory's terms comes first.

**Reader's key.**
- **BDA (Belief-Driven Attachment)** — the project's theory: whether someone becomes *attached* to you (wants you around, follows you, returns to you) is driven by the beliefs they form about you. *Attachment* is a **disposition** — a tendency to engage with, persist toward, and return to a person. It is *generated by* beliefs; it is not itself a belief.
- **Charm** — one of four attachment variables: the belief *"this person is high-status / competent / impressive — I look up to them."* The others are **Hope** (this person's future presence or action will produce good outcomes for me — with flavors such as Attention, Continuity, Validation), **Fear**, and **Resonance** (they share my way of seeing).
- **The type table** (`BDA/30` §1) — names what an attachment looks like when one variable dominates. Charm-dominant = "Status / admiration". Its third column, *persistence under change*, says how each type behaves when circumstances shift. The corpus reads that column as a **signature**: an outside view of the receiver's state.
- **The belief-frame** (`BDA/10` §3) — beliefs accumulate from evidence (they can be pre-loaded by what others say; a strong prior moves slowly). The frame states how beliefs *rise*; it states no law for how they *fall*.
- **Backed vs performed; the backing channels** — a displayed state is backed when it is really there; receivers read the difference over time through conduct, costs paid, off-stage behavior, consequences. A person's **record** — their work, outputs, public standing, others' reports — is the footprint a real state leaves.
- **Scarcity of access, aspirational distance** (`BDA/50` §3) — "adjacent mechanisms": real attachment-generators the theory keeps *outside* its formula. Note their sign: less access *raises* perceived value.

### 2 · What the row actually says

Two words carried the confusion.

**"Access."** Six usages surfaced across the corpus: reaching the person (interactional); observing their conduct and work (evidential); none at all; scarcity of access (adjacent, sign-reversed); Hope's own access (the frozen spec defines Hope as "accessible high value"; the flavors Continuity and access-Hope are about the sender staying reachable); and the reader's access needed to judge backing. In the row, "access to the Charm source" means **evidential access** — the stream of evidence about the person's standing. Three things fix this: the origin's own gloss ("celebrity retires, audience shrinks"); the Mozart case (zero reachability, Charm present — contradicting the interactional reading); and scarcity of access (less reachability raises value — contradicting the interactional reading's sign).

**"Weakens."** The column is a signature — `BDA/30` §1's own words: *"column three is a signature (outside-view behaviour)."* So "weakens" is predicated of what an observer *sees*: the attachment's **expression**. It is not a claim that the belief erodes. The belief-frame has no decay law; its only fall-side statements elsewhere are un-maintenance (`BDA/41` §7) and backing-failure (`BDA/30` §6), and `BDA/41` §7's own listed signs of "decay" are *"warmth persists without motion … artifacts kept … responses friendly but slower"* — content intact, motion fading. The corpus was already describing an unexpressed disposition over an intact belief; it had not said so.

**The row's self-contradiction.** A signature row ("how it looks") stating a cause ("because access recedes") — a signature asserting a mechanism it does not have. That is the defect, and it is why the canon could hold both the row and the counter-case without resolving them.

### 3 · What happens to admiration at a distance

Once "weakens" is about expression and "access" is about evidence, the account writes itself from committed material:

- **The belief goes un-maintained.** No fresh evidence arrives. The belief's *content* ("they are impressive") does not change; its *currency* goes stale. In the belief-frame's terms: the frame updates only on evidence, and a belief that stops being evidenced is not thereby lowered.
- **The attachment loses occasions to express.** Attachment is a tendency to engage and return; with no access there is nothing to return to. An unexpressed disposition and a weakened one look identical from outside. (An **occasion** is any cue that makes the disposition actionable — contact, a mention, an encounter with the person's work, a third party's reference. The record supplies occasions too: hearing the music.)
- **The belief is exposed to re-pricing.** "Impressive" is comparative. While the person is absent, the receiver's comparison set can move — a field advances, the receiver's own competence grows — and the belief is re-priced against it with no new fact about the person. The childhood idol met again and found ordinary is this.
- **The ground is removed only by counter-evidence.** The canon already says so: *"persistent failure eventually lowers Charm"* — later and elsewhere (`BDA/10` §5). Distance is not counter-evidence.
- **The record maintains the belief in the person's absence.** Whatever carries evidence of the standing — the work, the output, the public standing, what others report — keeps the belief evidenced without the person. The belief's *object* is always the person (the theory's beliefs are about a sender, never about an artifact); the record is the **evidence-carrier**. What the record can do is bounded by the receiver's ability to read it (a work one cannot evaluate maintains nothing) and by how far the comparison set moves.
- **At total absence the standing freezes.** No access of any kind, but a record: the person's standing is fixed by what stands. The belief stays present-tense about a fixed standing — Mozart *is* a great composer — and re-pricing is the only mover left.
- **Two limits.** No record at all (the ex-boss, the old acquaintance): no maintenance, no occasions, free re-pricing — the original row's signature at its maximum, still without any belief-erosion. Maximal record with zero reachability (fame): the belief fully maintained, expression waiting for occasions.

### 4 · The fame cases, worked

- **The followed star.** Access to follow *is* evidential access. The Charm-belief is maintained by the stream; Hope is alive (new work to come; the star stays present); occasions abound. Nothing weakens — the row, read as the origin meant it, agrees.
- **Charmed with no way to follow.** The belief persists, frozen on the record; expression waits. This is the mentor admired from a distance for years, and Mozart. The user's "I guess yes" is what the belief-frame predicts, not a concession to it.
- **The star who retires or disappears.** What visibly cools is the fan's **Hope** — the corpus retired the celebrity case as decisive for exactly a Hope residue (the Validation flavor, which presupposes Attention and Continuity: the star being present in one's world). Expression suspends. The Charm-belief stays and freezes. If the absence reads as *chosen*, the corpus's own display-coupling ("impressive but not for me") and the adjacent scarcity mechanism can *raise* Charm; if it reads as decline, they do not.
- **The comeback.** The stored belief is **revived** at prior-strength — a strong prior brought back by fresh evidence, which is why re-engagement is instant rather than rebuilt from zero ("maintenance is not creation", the corpus's friend-with-many-dates case). Three modifiers: a scarcity boost (adjacent, for any absence with an intact record; native, for chosen withholding), a re-pricing discount if the comparison set moved, and a **Coherence gate**: the returning person must be coherent with their record — a return that contradicts the record fails at the Coherence gate before Charm is even consulted.
- **Mozart.** The decisive case, with one honest flag: it is Charm-*dominant*, not Charm-pure — "identifying" with a composer has a Resonance flavor, so part of that persistence may be the Resonance row's.
- **Same or different?** The same belief. What differed meanwhile was what was acting on it — staleness, missing occasions, re-pricing — not its content.

### 5 · What this changes in the theory

- **The persistence column, under the belief-frame.** Each row now carries a **maintenance-source** beside its committed **removal-event**: Charm — maintained by the evidence stream (conduct, record, third-party report), removed by re-evaluation (counter-evidence or re-pricing; gradual); Hope — maintained by the exchange's continuation, removed when it ends (abrupt, unchanged); Fear — maintained by the threat or dependency, removed when it lifts (abrupt, unchanged); Resonance — maintained by the receiver's own model (as asserted), removal event **unstated** — a gap the redesign exposed, registered as the twin of the open question "what maintains Resonance."
- **The two families stand, refined.** Charm, Hope, and Fear remain externally grounded (the ground is the sender's real standing, exchange, or threat); Resonance internally maintained. The refinement is in the removal events: *by events* (Hope, Fear) versus *by re-evaluation* (Charm). "Weakens, gradually" was gesturing at the second all along.
- **The Charm+Fear prediction survives, re-worded.** "Fear lifts abruptly, admiration recedes slowly" becomes: deference lifts at once when the threat ends; admiration persists on the record and goes quiet without occasions.
- **The Return Doctrine's decay premise is re-attributed.** Silence lets the exchange lapse (Hope) and removes occasions; it does not erode Charm. The doctrine's existing answer — zero-display channels: third parties, real usage — is the record doing exactly this work.
- **A stamped prediction.** Distance-decay of admiration tracks *record-poverty*, not person-access: absent colleagues with a visible record should stay admired longer than those without one. Structural, observation-blocked.

### 6 · What it means for the user's own case

The outreach season has silent stretches. Under the corrected account, silence starves two things and not the third: the live thread of the exchange (Hope) and occasions for contact (presence). Charm — the belief that the founder and product are impressive — is kept by the **record**: the product itself, shipped features, third-party usage, what customers say to each other. So the rule for silent stretches is not "stay visible" but **grow the record during absence — ship while silent** — and let real third parties and real usage carry the evidence, which is what the corpus's zero-display channels already prescribe.

At total absence the standing freezes on the record. Two limits: no record (the signature at its maximum) and fame (maximal record, zero reachability). In mixed fan attachments, what dissolves on the source's retirement is the Hope nest, not the Charm. Provisional, as before.

## Reasoning

**How the answer was reached.** The pipeline ran articulation → surfacing → warm re-articulation → sensemaking → decomposition → innovation → critique → routelisting. Surfacing located the row's origin and its archived draft (the dropped behaviour column; the "celebrity retires, audience shrinks" gloss), the canon's own counter-evidence, and six usages of "access." The warm pass found no premise conflict beyond one status mismatch: the user treated the row as a hard claim, the corpus holds it as a provisional tendency — so the inquiry adjudicated a hedge. Sensemaking stabilized the row as a signature mis-stated as a mechanism, with the three distance-effects and the record parameter; its hardest perspective — "the original authors were right" — survived as the signature half of the verdict. Innovation produced twenty-four candidates across all seven mechanisms; five converged on "the Charm source is the evidence-carrier; the column is maintenance × removal." Critique tested seventeen candidates against eight dimensions (canon-fidelity, architectural coherence, case-adequacy, structural-only status, ripple-safety, predication discipline, parsimony, fidelity to the user's own questions), quoting the canon verbatim where a candidate leaned on it.

**What was killed, and why.**
- *The belief itself decays with distance (forgetting).* Killed: the theory has no decay mechanism and no rate; adding one is forbidden by structural-only status; and the decisive case — an intact evaluation of a two-centuries-dead sender — would need re-explaining. The corpus's one line that says "belief decays" (`BDA/41` §7) lists, as its own signs of decay, *warmth persisting without motion* and *artifacts kept* — content intact, expression fading.
- *The "tense-shift" (at total absence the belief re-indexes from "is impressive" to "was impressive").* Killed by its own defense: the belief stays present-tense about a standing that no longer changes — the standing freezes on the record. The seed "the frozen standing" replaced it.
- *"The Charm source becomes the record."* Refined, not killed: the belief's object is always the sender (the theory's beliefs are about a person; `BDA/50` §4 forbids attachment to an object with no person); the record is the evidence-carrier. The origin's phrase "the Charm source" reads as the *evidence* source.
- *A new two-column persistence table.* Refined to a clause per row: re-tabulating five rows to fill one substantive cell doubles unvalidated content and touches the Hope and Fear rows' live consumers; stating the Hope and Fear cells verbatim keeps them safe.
- *Numeric rates (staleness half-lives).* Excluded — blocked by structural-only status; recorded as what the static/dynamic frontier would need.
- *Deleting the column; rewriting the origin or the insistence finding; a footnote under an unchanged row.* Rejected: deletion orphans the Hope/Fear consumers and the Charm+Fear prediction; rewriting breaks the corpus's correction discipline; a footnote leaves a signature row asserting a cause it lacks.
- *"Charm never weakens."* Never a candidate: the ex-boss limit and the user's own "or is it different?" both name real weakening of expression and currency.

**What survived, and why it held.** The account is a composition of committed content — the belief-frame's cumulative update, the decisive Mozart case, scarcity of access, the "later and elsewhere" counter-evidence route, the un-maintenance signs — with the origin's gloss as corroboration only; critique confirmed it stands without the gloss. The fan case's re-attribution to Hope held because the corpus had already recorded a Hope residue there and because pure-Charm attachments (Mozart) show no such cooling. The return case held once "the same person as the record" was softened to "coherent with the record." The one caveat on the winning candidate was on parsimony — keep the table cell short — which the corrected row's cell/note split resolves.
