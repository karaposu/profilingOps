## Phase 2 — Perspective Checking

### Technical / Logical

The formula is a product of a sum and three gates. Given that, three propositions are forced:

1. If `f`-Fear is the only nonzero variable and every `g` is intact, output is **nonzero**. There is an attachment, and its TYPE is Coerced. Denying it is attachment contradicts C2 and FP3.
2. If any `g` is zero, output is **zero** — and a zero output has no TYPE, because TYPE reads off `f`'s distribution *of a product that is zero*. There is no engagement state to name, because there is no engagement. **KI5(ii) is not a rhetorical case; it is what the formula returns.**
3. Removing the threat sets `f`-Fear to zero. If Fear was carrying the sum, the sum goes with it and the output collapses. **"Reverses on threat removal" is not a special behaviour of a special state — it is what any single-variable-dominant attachment does when its variable is zeroed.** The Hope row says the same thing in different words (*"dissolves when exchange ends"*), and the Charm row a third time (*"weakens as access recedes"*).

*New anchor —* **KI9: the persistence column is not describing three natures. It is describing one mechanism — variable-removal — applied to four different variables.** The apparent difference between *reverses*, *dissolves* and *weakens* tracks how abruptly each variable can be zeroed, not a difference in kind.

### Human / User

The user did not ask a taxonomy question. They reported a *feeling* — *"i have a feeling complience is form of attachment"* — and then, unusually, doubted the shape of their own hypothesis: *"or they are more complex involved?"* That second clause is the more informative one, and the anchors vindicate it: the honest answer is neither yes nor no.

There is also a lived observation embedded in the feeling. Someone who complies **is** oriented toward the other person: watching them, adjusting to them, not leaving. That is phenomenologically close to attachment, and C1's definition — *tendency to engage with, persist toward, return to* — does not obviously exclude it.

*New anchor —* **KI10: compliance satisfies attachment's stated definition on its face.** Engaging with: yes. Persisting toward: yes, while the threat stands. Returning to: yes. The definition as written does not discriminate. **Whatever separates them is not in the definition the corpus published** — which is why the question has no ready answer in the corpus.

## Phase 4 *(protocol Phase 3)* — Ambiguity Collapse

### Ambiguity A1 — Which *attachment* does the question mean?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** there is no equivocation. The corpus uses *attachment* consistently for the formula's output, and the engagement-state layer's *attachment* is just shorthand for *the attachment case that is not pursuit or compliance*. Treating a shorthand as a second sense manufactures a problem.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** the two uses stand in **different logical relations to compliance**, and both relations are asserted in the corpus. At output level, Coerced is a **row inside** the attachment taxonomy — compliance's referent is a *part of* attachment. At state level, compliance is a **co-member contrasted with** attachment — not a part of it, but an alternative to it. A shorthand cannot invert the part-whole relation of the thing it abbreviates. The two uses are not the same term used loosely; they are two terms.

**Confidence: HIGH.**

**Resolution:** the corpus carries two referents. Name them **attachment-as-output** (`f × g`, the level-3 quantity with MAGNITUDE and TYPE) and **attachment-as-state** (the level-4 engagement-state layer member). The question has different answers for each.

- **What is now fixed:** every subsequent claim must say which referent it uses.
- **What is no longer allowed:** any unqualified verdict of the form *compliance is / is not attachment*.
- **What now depends on this:** the whole adjudication; the audit of the quoted claim; the layer's status.
- **What changed in the model:** the question stops being one question.

### Ambiguity A2 — Is compliance a *species* of attachment?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** yes. Coerced is one of five named TYPEs of attachment; a named kind inside a taxonomy of kinds is what a species *is*. The corpus even calls it *"what kind of engagement"*.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** C2 is decisive and is the corpus's own sentence — *"Because `f` is additive, TYPE reads directly from the variable-weight distribution — **no separate mechanism is needed**."* The corpus's species criterion is exactly mechanism-distinctness: *"Species require distinct mechanisms … Self-Positioning has species because attention has genuinely distinct directional **modes**, each operating through different attention allocation."* Coerced has no mode of its own; it is a **weight configuration** of the one mechanism. A weight configuration is not a mechanism. **The corpus's own words for what Coerced is and for what a species requires are mutually exclusive.**

*Transfer warrant (C8), stated rather than assumed:* the species criterion was written about a modulator's candidate sub-kinds. Transferring it to an output-type is legitimate here because the criterion is stated as a **general structural marker** (*"This is the structural marker of aspects, not species"*), and because the discriminandum it uses — mechanism-distinctness — is defined at the architecture's mechanism layer, which outputs and modulators share. The transfer would be illegitimate if the criterion had been stated as a modulator-specific admission rule; it was not.

**Confidence: HIGH** on the species verdict; **MEDIUM** on the transfer warrant, which is argued rather than cited.

**Resolution:** **compliance is not a species of attachment**, in either referent. Under the *species-of* reading of *form of*, the user's hypothesis is **incorrect** — and incorrect for a reason that also explains why it felt right.

- **What is now fixed:** TYPE-membership does not constitute species-membership.
- **What is no longer allowed:** treating the 5-TYPE taxonomy as a genus/species tree.
- **What now depends on this:** the *made-of-the-same-stuff* reading now carries the whole hypothesis.

### Ambiguity A3 — Is compliance *made of the same stuff* as attachment?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** no — the corpus contrasts them, and things that are made of the same stuff are not routinely contrasted. Two states with opposite motivational directions (one inhibitory, one approach-oriented) cannot be the same material at different settings.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** the inhibitory/approach contrast is at the **behavioural-direction** layer, not at the compositional layer, and the corpus already ruled on that: approach/avoidance was tested as a substrate (candidate R-γ) and **killed** — *"a binary motivation distinction, not a finer substrate … useful as a one-axis overlay."* An overlay does not establish different material. Compositionally, both states are outputs of the **same** `f × g`; the Fear-dominant case differs only in which additive term carries the weight. FP3 states Fear is an independent attachment generator; *"being a threat to navigate"* appears in the corpus's own list of independent generators.

**Confidence: HIGH.**

**Resolution:** **yes.** Under the *made-of-the-same-stuff* reading, compliance and attachment are the same material at different variable-weightings. **The user's hypothesis is correct in this sense.**

- **What is now fixed:** the two senses of *form of* have **opposite** verdicts. This is the answer's core, not a hedge.
- **What is no longer allowed:** answering *"is compliance a form of attachment?"* without disambiguating.
- **What now depends on this:** the third option the statement invited is now *derived*, not invented.

### Ambiguity A4 — What relation, then, does compliance bear to attachment?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** none of the corpus's named relations applies, so the honest answer is *sui generis* — invent nothing, report that the relation is unnamed.

**Why the counter fails (structural grounds):** the corpus **does** instantiate the needed relation, twice, in two independent places. (a) The failure signatures: devaluation is not a kind of Self-Positioning, it is what its collapse looks like from outside; likewise Model-Collapse, Contagion-Drain. (b) The **Persistence under change** column itself: an outside-view description of an inside-state that is *not* about failure. (b) matters because it defeats the natural objection that the signature relation is failure-specific — the corpus already has a non-failure instance. The relation is instantiated; only its **name** is missing, and naming an instantiated relation is not invention.

**Confidence: HIGH.**

**Resolution:** the relation is **signature-of** — compliance is the **outside-view signature of a Fear-dominant attachment**, in the same way devaluation is the outside-view signature of a Self-Positioning collapse. Not species, not aspect, not sibling.

- **What is now fixed:** a fourth relation, already used twice, gets named.
- **What is no longer allowed:** listing compliance beside attachment as a co-member of one taxonomic level.
- **What now depends on this:** the engagement-state layer's structure.
- **What changed in the model:** the layer stops being a taxonomy of states and becomes a **signature catalogue**.

### Ambiguity A5 — Was *"was never attachment"* stated too strongly?

**Strongest counter-interpretation:** no. The quoted claim answers a question about a person with **no coherence**, and with `g₂ = 0` the product is genuinely zero. Zero attachment. The claim is exactly right for the case it was answering.

**Why the counter fails — and it partly does not:** on the case as literally posed, the counter is **correct**, and this is the run's most important correction to its own direction. Where the claim overreached is in its **grounds**: it cited the Coerced row, which describes a case where `g` is *intact* and Fear carries the sum — a **nonzero** attachment. The claim used a nonzero-output row to license a zero-output verdict. So: right conclusion for the posed case, wrong citation, and the wrong citation is what generalised the claim into *deference-under-threat is never attachment*, which does not follow.

**Confidence: HIGH** on the split verdict; the counter is not defeated but **partitioned**.

**Resolution:** the claim is **correct for the zero-coherence case and overstated as a general claim**, and its citation does not support it. Verified: the cited document uses *compliance* zero times and calls the same case *a Fear-dominant attachment*.

- **What is now fixed:** the correction is to grounds and scope, not to the original answer's verdict.
- **What is no longer allowed:** citing the Coerced row to establish that fear-driven deference is not attachment.
- **What now depends on this:** the finding's Corrections section.

### Ambiguity A6 — Is the engagement-state layer's sibling structure correct?
