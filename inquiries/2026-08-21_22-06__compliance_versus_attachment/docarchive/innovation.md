**Generic — the dependent-variable lens.** Current frame: APT is a theory of *attachment generation*. Shift to: APT is a theory of *what the receiver does next*. **Focused — the practitioner lens.** Shift from theory-internal coherence to use. To a practitioner the question *"is compliance a form of attachment?"* means *"should I treat this relationship as an asset?"* — and there the answer is **no**, whatever the structural verdict, because what matters for action is the persistence profile, not the ontology. → **L-b: the structural answer and the practical answer diverge, and the theory has nowhere to say so.** MAGNITUDE and TYPE are both descriptive; nothing in APT ranks an attachment as worth having.

> **FA-1 — the species verdict is criterion-relative, and the corpus chose a criterion without noticing it was choosing.**
>
> Under what conditions would mechanism-distinctness be the *wrong* test for speciation? When the taxonomy is **relational** rather than **causal**. Biological species are not individuated by distinct mechanisms — they run identical machinery — but by **reproductive isolation**, a relational property. Whole families of speciation criteria are like this. The corpus's criterion is **causal**: it asks what produces the thing. A **relational** criterion would ask how the thing behaves toward its neighbours — and under a relational criterion, *"same mechanism, different weights, non-interconvertible trajectories"* is exactly what a species looks like.
>
> This does not overturn the verdict — the corpus's criterion is the corpus's, and it was applied correctly. It changes how the verdict must be **stated**: not *"compliance is not a species of attachment"* but *"**not a species by the corpus's causal criterion — and the corpus has no relational criterion, under which it would be.**"* The user's hypothesis is then not merely right-in-one-sense; it is right under a criterion the corpus has simply never written.
