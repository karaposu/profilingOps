## Question

From the inquiry's framing document:

> *"and i think the question of what is the difference between compliance and attachment? i have a feeling complience is form of attachment, is this correct or not? or they are more complex involved?"*

The question arrived attached to a quotation of the earlier answer, which had said that fear-driven deference *"was never attachment."* So the question carries three asks at once: state what distinguishes the two, adjudicate whether one is a form of the other, and — implicitly, through that final clause — test whether the quoted claim was too strong.

**The goal:** a conceptual adjudication. A criterion that distinguishes the two, plus a verdict on the subsumption relation, bounded to the theory's engagement-state layer, its Coerced attachment type, and what the theory's formula actually outputs. Not the clinical psychology of coercion, not the ethics of compliance, and not a restatement of the answer being questioned.

## Finding Summary

- **The question had two answers because the word *attachment* has two referents in this corpus.** At one level it names what the theory's formula computes. At another it names one member of the three-part engagement-state layer that sits above that output. The question's hypothesis is right about the first and wrong about the second.

- **Is compliance made of the same stuff as attachment? Yes — decisively.** Both are outputs of the same computation. Compliance is what you get when Fear carries the additive sum. The theory's own belief-frame lists Fear among the independent generators of attachment, naming *"being a threat to navigate"* as one of them.

- **Is compliance a *kind* of attachment? No — by the corpus's own test.** The corpus says species require distinct mechanisms. It also says, in the same document that defines attachment types, that type *"reads directly from the variable-weight distribution — no separate mechanism is needed."* A weight configuration is not a mechanism.

- **The two answers diverge because the corpus answers them with two different tests, not because the question is being dodged.** The plain-language version of the whole result is one sentence: *compliance is what a fear-driven attachment looks like from outside — same machinery, different fuel, which makes it the same kind of thing rather than a kind of attachment.*

- **What actually holds between them is a relation the corpus already uses and has never named.** Devaluation is not a *kind of* Self-Positioning; it is what a Self-Positioning collapse looks like from outside. Compliance stands to a Fear-dominant attachment exactly as devaluation stands to that collapse.

- **The species verdict depends on a criterion whose type was never declared.** The corpus's test is *causal* — it asks what produces the thing. There are also *relational* tests: biological species run identical machinery and are individuated by the fact that they cannot interbreed. The corpus has no relational test, and whether one would flip this verdict turns on a question the corpus already lists as open.

- **The three states looked like siblings because a five-row table was compressed into three.** The theory's attachment-type table has a *Persistence under change* column with five rows. The engagement-state layer took three of them, dropped two, and presented the three as a level of its own.

- **Read literally, that column is not describing four kinds of engagement.** Three of its rows name an *external event withdrawing a ground* — access recedes, exchange ends, threat lifts. One names *internal maintenance* — persists through absence, maintained via shared model. That is a binary crossed with how abruptly removal happens, not four natures.

- **And that binary is one the corpus already committed to, one layer down.** A separate iteration established that Charm, Hope and Fear are *unilateral-evaluation* beliefs while Resonance is a *dyadic-emergence* belief. The persistence column reproduces that split without citing it.

- **So compliance's nearest relatives are not "attachment" — they are status-attachment and transactional attachment.** All three are externally-grounded engagement, differing only in which ground and how fast it can be pulled. What the engagement-state layer called *attachment* turns out to be the Resonance family alone. **The question was comparing a member of one family against the name of the whole taxonomy.**

- **The word *compliance* is doing three separable jobs**, and the earlier answer was about one while citing another. Job one is a Fear-dominant attachment with a real, nonzero output. Job two is behaviour with *zero* attachment, because a gate collapsed — which is the case the original question actually posed. Job three is going-along-with as an effect of attachment strength, with no Fear anywhere.

- **The earlier claim gets a split verdict.** *"Was never attachment"* is **right** for the case it answered — with Coherence at zero the product is genuinely zero. It is **wrong in its citation**: it invoked a row describing a case where the gates are intact and the output is nonzero. Using a nonzero-output row to license a zero-output verdict is what turned a correct narrow answer into an incorrect general one.

- **Fear's two unsettled definitions do less damage than expected, and different damage.** The relation verdicts hold under both readings. What does *not* hold is the identification of compliance with the Coerced row: under the cost-of-loss reading, that row describes dependency, and ordinary compliance may map to no row at all.

- **A repair that looked like it closed the theory's largest gap here does not close it.** Deciding which signature a case shows requires knowing which variable dominates, and the theory's coefficients are uncalibrated. The gap is **relocated** — from *no procedure exists* to *the procedure is blocked on a calibration item already on the roadmap*. That is progress and it is not a solution.

- **One hazard, recorded.** If compliance is the outside view of a real attachment, a profiling system reading a conversation will report coerced deference as an attachment. The theory has no term ranking an attachment as worth having, and the type dimension that would carry it is provisional, uncalibrated and unimplemented.

## Finding

### 0. Why this question came up at all

The theory under discussion is APT — a model of interpersonal attachment used in this project to profile people from conversations. Its core claim is a formula: attachment is a sum of four belief-variables (Charm, Hope, Fear, Resonance) multiplied by three gates (Self-Positioning, Coherence, Emotional Composure). The sum is additive, so each variable can generate attachment on its own; the gates are multiplicative, so any one of them at zero collapses the whole result.

An earlier inquiry answered a question about whether someone with no Coherence could stabilise attachment through fear. The answer was no — a gate at zero zeroes the product. That inquiry then said what such a person gets *instead* is **compliance**, and it introduced a three-part distinction to hold compliance apart from attachment: attachment persists when satisfied, pursuit ends when its gap closes, compliance reverses when the threat lifts.

The user read that answer back and pushed on it. Their intuition was that compliance is not the opposite of attachment but a *form* of it. What follows is what happens when that intuition is taken seriously and checked against what the corpus actually says.

The short version: **the intuition is right, in a specific and defensible sense — and the earlier answer's evidence does not support the claim it was offered for.**

---

### 1. The question was two questions

The word *attachment* is used two ways in this corpus, at two different levels of the architecture, and nobody has noticed.

**At the output level**, *attachment* is what the formula computes. The theory says so directly: *"Attachment has two distinct output dimensions"* — a magnitude (how strongly the observer is drawn to engage) and a type (what kind of engagement it is). Type is read off which variable dominated the sum. The five types are named: Status/admiration, Transactional, Coerced, Bonded, Collaborative.

**At the engagement-state level**, *attachment* is one member of a three-part layer that describes how the output behaves over time — attachment, pursuit, compliance.

These are not one word used loosely at two granularities. They stand in **opposite relations to compliance**:

- At the output level, the Coerced case is a **row inside** the attachment type table. Compliance's referent is *part of* attachment.
- At the engagement-state level, compliance is a **co-member contrasted with** attachment. It is not part of attachment; it is an alternative to it.

A shorthand cannot invert the part-whole relation of the thing it abbreviates. Two terms are in play.

The slide is visible in a single paragraph of the layer's own founding document. It writes *"the theory computes an attachment and then stops. It produces how strong the attachment is and what kind it is"* — the output sense — and then, two sentences later, *"Three states get confused as a result: attachment (persists when satisfied), pursuit… compliance"* — the engagement-state sense, now contrasted with two things the output sense contains.

**Everything below says which referent it means.** Where the distinction matters, this finding writes *the formula's output* and *the engagement-state named attachment*.

There is also a third, pre-formal sense worth naming, because it is probably what the user's *feeling* was tracking. Before any of this was formalised, the theory's domain one was glossed as *"why they stay"* and *"how do you make someone keep talking to you."* Someone who complies **does** stay and **does** keep talking to you. That intuition is not confused; it is reading the pre-formal sense correctly.

---

### 2. The answer, in both senses

The phrase *form of* also has two readings, and — this is the part that makes the question hard rather than merely ambiguous — **the corpus answers them with two different tests**.

**Made of the same stuff? Yes.**

Compliance and attachment are outputs of one computation. The Fear-dominant case differs from the Resonance-dominant case only in which additive term carries the weight. The theory's belief-frame is explicit that each of the four variables generates attachment independently, and it names Fear's contribution in exactly those terms: *"being high-status, being a future-positive prospect, **being a threat to navigate**, sharing world-models, are independent attachment generators that add."*

The obvious objection is that the two have opposite motivational directions — one is inhibitory, the other approach-oriented. That objection lives at the behavioural layer, not the compositional one, and the corpus has already ruled on it: an approach/avoidance framing was tested as a possible substrate for the four variables and **killed**, on the ground that it is *"a binary motivation distinction, not a finer substrate… useful as a one-axis overlay."* An overlay does not establish different material.

**A kind of attachment? No.**

The corpus's test for whether something is a genuine sub-kind is stated in its modulator work: *"Species require distinct mechanisms. Self-Positioning has species because attention has genuinely distinct directional modes… each operating through different attention allocation."* Where candidate sub-kinds produce the same outcome through the same mechanism, the corpus calls them *aspects*, not species.

Apply that here, and the theory's own sentence settles it: *"Because `f` is additive, TYPE reads directly from the variable-weight distribution — **no separate mechanism is needed**."*

Coerced has no mechanism of its own. It is a weight configuration of the one mechanism. **The corpus's words for what Coerced is and its words for what a species requires are mutually exclusive.**

One honesty note about that test. It was written about a modulator's candidate sub-kinds, and applying it to an output type is a transfer beyond where it was authored. The transfer holds because the corpus states the criterion without qualification — *"This is the structural marker of aspects, not species"* — and because mechanism-distinctness is defined at a layer that outputs and modulators share. But it is a transfer, argued rather than cited, and section 3's qualification depends on noticing that.

**So the two senses give opposite verdicts, and that is the answer rather than a hedge.** In plain language: *compliance is what a fear-driven attachment looks like from outside — same machinery, different fuel, which makes it the same kind of thing rather than a kind of attachment.*

---

### 3. The relation that actually holds, and a qualification on the verdict

If compliance is not a species of attachment and not merely a different thing, what is it?

**The corpus already uses the needed relation, twice, and has never named it.**

Each of the three modulators has a *failure signature* — devaluation for Self-Positioning, Model-Collapse for Coherence, Contagion-Drain for Emotional Composure. Devaluation is not a *kind of* Self-Positioning. It is what a Self-Positioning collapse looks like from outside. And critically, the corpus keeps the two relations apart **inside one component without comment**: Self-Positioning has species (Self-Focus, Caregiver-Focus, Mission-Focus and others) *and* a failure signature, and devaluation never appears in the species list.

The natural objection — that signatures in this corpus are always about *failure*, and compliance is not a failure — is answered by the second instance. The attachment-type table's *Persistence under change* column is itself an outside-view description of an inside state, and it is not about failure at all.

So: **compliance is the outside-view signature of a Fear-dominant attachment.** Not a species, not an aspect, not a sibling. Naming a relation the corpus already instantiates twice is bookkeeping, not invention.

**The qualification.** The species verdict rests on a criterion whose *type* was never declared. Mechanism-distinctness is a **causal** test — it asks what produces the thing. There are also **relational** tests: biological species run identical machinery and are individuated by reproductive isolation, a fact about how the groups relate rather than about what builds them. Under a relational test, *"same mechanism, different weights, non-interconvertible trajectories"* is exactly what a species looks like.

Does that flip the verdict? **It is open, and it is open on a question the corpus already tracks.** Relational speciation works because the groups cannot interconvert — and the attachment-type document's own frontier list asks, verbatim: *"TYPE evolution over time: Attachments can shift TYPE as relationships develop. Can Hope-dominant (transactional) attachment deepen to Resonance-dominant (bonded)?"* If types interconvert, a relational test fails here too.

So the verdict should be stated precisely: **compliance is not a species of attachment by the corpus's causal criterion. The corpus has no relational criterion; whether one would flip the answer depends on the type-interconversion question it has already recorded as unanswered.**

---

### 4. Why the two looked like siblings

The three-part layer that made compliance and attachment look like alternatives was built by promoting an existing table column. That column has five rows; the layer took three.

| Dominant variable | Attachment type | Persistence under change |
|---|---|---|
| Charm-dominant | Status / admiration | Weakens as access to the Charm source recedes |
| Hope-dominant | Transactional | Dissolves when exchange ends |
| Fear-dominant | Coerced | Reverses (relief, not attachment) on threat removal |
| Resonance-dominant | Bonded / deep | Persists through absence; maintained via shared model |
| Hope + Resonance | Collaborative | High persistence; both transacts and connects |

Three structural problems follow, and each is derived from material that **predates the layer** — the formula and this table — so none of them depends on second-guessing the layer's authors.

**First: the three members are not tested against the same change.** Attachment *persists when satisfied*. Pursuit *ends when its gap closes*. Compliance *reverses when the threat lifts*. Three different perturbations, three incommensurable tests — and therefore not three values of one axis. The consequence is sharp: **Coerced engagement passes attachment's own stated test**, since it does not end on satisfaction. It ends on a perturbation attachment was never tested against.

**Second: the partition drops two rows.** The Charm-dominant row (*weakens as access recedes*) maps to no member of the layer, and neither does the mixed row. Five rows became three members with no note that anything was lost.

**Third: the members compose.** A later inquiry described followership as *"attachment plus pursuit that cannot extinguish."* Co-members of a partition do not sum.

**A fourth problem sits underneath those three.** The factor whose closure ends each state sits at a different architectural location for each. Compliance ends when Fear — an in-formula variable — goes to zero. The engagement-state named attachment persists because Charm and Resonance, also in-formula, do not. But pursuit ends when **model-completeness** closes, and model-completeness is placed by the corpus *outside* the variable-and-modulator ontology entirely, as a cross-variable epistemic modifier.

That has a consequence worth stating on its own: **a single membership criterion cannot range over members whose ending-factors sit at different architectural levels, one of them outside the formula.** The layer's missing membership criteria were not an oversight waiting to be repaired. They were structurally impossible from the start. The repair is not *supply what is missing* — it is *stop requiring what cannot exist.*

**And yet the layer has been genuinely useful**, which needs explaining rather than explaining away. Three subsequent inquiries have used it without breaking. The reason is section 6: each of them quietly used a **different job of the word** *compliance*, and no two collided.

### 5. What the persistence column is actually describing — and the real cleavage

Read the column's four distinct rows literally, one at a time.

- Charm: *"Weakens as access to the Charm source **recedes**."*
- Hope: *"Dissolves when **exchange ends**."*
- Fear: *"Reverses on **threat removal**."*
- Resonance: *"Persists through absence; **maintained via shared model**."*

The first three all name **an external event that withdraws the variable's ground**. The fourth names **an internal maintenance mechanism** — not a failure to decay, but something actively keeping the variable up.

So the column encodes two things: a **binary** — is this variable's ground externally removable? — crossed with **how abruptly** removal happens. Fear can be zeroed in an instant by a third party's act, so its row says *reverses*. Hope discharges on delivery, so its row says *dissolves*. Charm recedes gradually, so its row says *weakens*. Resonance is not removable at all.

This is a useful sharpening on its own. It is more than that because **the binary is a split the corpus already committed to, one layer down, for entirely independent reasons.**

A separate iteration examining whether Hope and Charm are really distinct variables concluded: *"Charm/Hope/Fear are **unilateral-evaluation** belief-categories — the receiver evaluates the sender on value-flow dimensions. Resonance is a **dyadic-emergence** belief-category — both parties' world-models co-determine."*

That statement is about **variables**. The persistence column's binary is about **trajectories**. They were written in different iterations, for different purposes — one testing whether the theory needed a deeper substrate, the other clarifying the theory's output dimensions — and **neither cites the other**. Two independent derivations of one split.

Which yields the finding's most direct answer to the question:

> **Compliance's nearest relatives are not "attachment." They are status-attachment and transactional attachment.**
>
> All three are the same kind of thing — externally-grounded engagement — differing only in which ground holds them up and how fast that ground can be pulled away. What the engagement-state layer called *attachment* is, on inspection, just the **Resonance family**.
>
> **The question was comparing a member of one family against the name of the whole taxonomy.** That is why it had two answers.

The mixed row is the prediction rather than the exception: one removable ground plus one non-removable ground means the engagement falls to the Resonance floor rather than to zero — which is exactly what *"high persistence"* says.

One thing this does **not** derive, recorded rather than smoothed over: the Resonance row's *maintained via shared model* clause. The two parameters explain why a variable does not go to zero. They do not explain what actively keeps it up.

---

### 6. The word *compliance* is doing three jobs

Three distinguishable things travel under one word in this corpus, and the partition is closed — not because three were observed, but because the formula has exactly three relevant output states.

**Job one — a Fear-dominant attachment, with a real nonzero output.** All gates intact, Fear carrying the sum. This is the engagement-state layer's referent, and it is the case sections 2 through 5 are about.

**Job two — behaviour with zero attachment, because a gate collapsed.** This is the case the *original* question actually posed: a person with no Coherence. With a gate at zero the product is zero, and a zero output has no type, because type reads off the weight distribution of a product that is zero. There is no engagement state to name, because there is no engagement. **This job has no name in the corpus**, and it is the null case an earlier inquiry already flagged as missing.

**Job three — going-along-with as an effect of attachment strength, with no Fear anywhere.** Iteration-3's shipped dating case describes a self-focused man who does not chase generating *"strong attraction and compliance"* — no threat present. And a later inquiry found that conferred position produces compliance from `f`-side content *"whether threat **or reward**."*

Why three and not more: either the product is nonzero with Fear dominant, or the product is zero, or the product is nonzero with Fear not dominant. There is no fourth output state. *(The closure is over engagement-relevant referents, not over the word's lexical senses — the implementation layer uses *compliance* to mean schema validation, which the formula obviously does not close.)*

**The three downstream inquiries that used the layer each used a different job**, which is why it felt serviceable and why no two uses collided. And it locates the earlier answer's error precisely: **it was answering about job two while citing a row that describes job one.**

### 7. What was wrong with the earlier answer

The claim under question was:

> *"What such a person gets is compliance, which your taxonomy already names — Fear-dominant → Coerced → 'reverses (relief, not attachment) on threat removal.' Deference that evaporates the instant the threat lifts was never attachment."*

**The verdict is right for the case it answered.** The question was about a person with **no Coherence**. With that gate at zero the product is genuinely zero, so there is no attachment. Nothing in this finding disturbs that.

**The citation does not support it.** Checked directly against the document cited — the theory's iteration-3.2.1 finding:

1. It uses the word ***compliance* zero times.** The word entered the corpus from a *different* document — the engagement-state layer's own inquiry — which is where compliance and Coerced were joined.
2. It introduces the taxonomy as *"**Attachment** has two distinct output dimensions"* and type as *"what kind of engagement,"* so Coerced is a row in a taxonomy **of attachment types**.
3. It writes, in its section on why type is not reducible to magnitude: *"a **Fear-dominant attachment** at high magnitude is structurally worse than a Resonance-dominant attachment at low magnitude."* It applies the word *attachment* to the very case the quoted claim says was *never* attachment.
4. It lists Coerced among *"the provisional 5-TYPE taxonomy (Status, Transactional, Coerced, Bonded, Collaborative)"* — of attachment.
5. Its *"(relief, not attachment)"* sits in the **Persistence under change** column, describing what remains **after** the threat lifts.

**A nonzero-output row was used to license a zero-output verdict.** That is the specific mechanism by which a correct narrow answer became an incorrect general one — because the generalisation *"deference under threat is never attachment"* does not follow from anything above.

For completeness: the cited document is not innocent either. It calls the Fear-dominant case an attachment in one section and withholds the word in one table cell. The most economical reading is that the parenthetical is about the *post-removal residue* while the rest is about the state *during* — but the document does not say so, and the tension is real.

---

### 8. What Fear's unsettled definition does to this

Fear is defined two incompatible ways across the corpus, and the corpus knows it. An earlier inquiry recorded the fork explicitly and **declared** its choice rather than settling it, on the ground that settling it belongs to a dedicated inquiry — the theory reserves a slot for a Fear sub-taxonomy that has never been opened.

- **Threat-of-harm** (iteration-3.4's belief statement): *"this person can harm me / poses threat."*
- **Cost-of-loss** (the older spec and iteration-1): *"consequences of losing you. Power dynamics, secrets, dependency."*

**The relation verdicts are unaffected.** Sections 2 and 3 turn on how the formula *composes* — Fear sits on the additive side, Coerced is a weight configuration, no separate mechanism is needed — and none of that depends on what Fear's content is.

**The identification is affected, and this is the sharper result.** Under cost-of-loss, a Fear-dominant attachment reads as *"I stay because losing you would cost me."* That is **dependency**, and it is not what *compliance* means in ordinary use — compliance is doing what you are told to avoid harm. So under one of the two live readings, **the Coerced row stops describing compliance at all**, and ordinary-language compliance may map to no attachment type in the theory.

The fork does not narrow the gap between compliance and attachment. It **moves the referent**.

**And there is new evidence that the fork must actually be settled.** Fear's *belief statement* is threat-of-harm-shaped, but Fear's *detection signals* are cost-of-loss-shaped: *"Fear: consequences of losing this relationship. Signals: asymmetric control they don't challenge, reluctance to disengage even when disinterested, careful word choices, avoidance of confrontation."* **The instrument measures a different construct from the one the definition names.** This appears nowhere else in the corpus; it was produced by reading the definition and the detector side by side.

---

### 9. What this does to the engagement-state layer

The layer is **incorrect as a taxonomy and serviceable as a view.**

The repair is a re-description, not a rewrite. The three names stay — three inquiries cite them and each uses a different job of the word, so retention costs nothing and removal would cost three re-writes. What they stop being is a **level** with independent membership. They become **derived signatures**, read off the attachment-type table: which variable dominates, and whether that variable's ground is externally removable.

*(Retaining the names is a compatibility decision, not a structural finding. The structural finding is that the layer has no independent membership; keeping the labels is convenience.)*

Two consequences follow.

**The two dropped rows come back for free.** Once the signature is derived rather than enumerated, the Charm-dominant and mixed rows have signatures too.

**The layer's largest recorded gap is relocated, not closed.** The founding finding called the missing determination mechanism *"the single largest piece of unfinished work this finding leaves behind."* Deriving signatures from dominance looks like it supplies one — the theory already reads variable weights. It does not, for two reasons that are both anchored in the corpus. First, the detection layer emits **five-level categorical** values, so ties are routine — and the taxonomy's own mixed row *is* a tie category, which concedes the point. Second, type reads off the **weighted** sum, and the theory's coefficients are listed among what is *"pending empirical calibration."* Without coefficients there is no weighted argmax to compute.

So the honest statement is: **the gap moves from *no procedure exists* to *the procedure is dominance-plus-ground-externality, blocked on a calibration item already on the roadmap*.** An unbounded gap becomes a bounded one. That is worth more than the overclaim it replaces, and it is not a solution.

---

### 10. What is not settled, and one hazard

**Not settled, and honestly so.** Whether compressing five rows into three was a defect or a justified simplification is an empirical question, and the corpus has no data: the Coerced row has **no worked case** among the theory's fifteen validation scenarios, and none of this has ever been implemented. Every verdict above is structural — derived from what the documents say and how the formula composes — and cannot be more than that.

**Two external traditions reach the same verdict independently**, and both are recorded as corroboration rather than as premises, per the corpus's convention of importing vocabulary but never apparatus. Clinical attachment theory's *disorganised* pattern — where the caregiver is simultaneously the source of threat and of safety — treats the coerced case as a real attachment. And contract theory's hostage models treat staying-because-leaving-is-costly as a genuine commitment mechanism, though that parallel lands on the cost-of-loss reading rather than on compliance as such.

**The hazard.** If compliance is the outside view of a real attachment, then a profiling system implementing this theory will read coerced deference, score high Fear, and **report an attachment**. Worse, it will do so constantly: Fear's four detection signals — *asymmetric control they don't challenge · reluctance to disengage even when disinterested · careful word choices · avoidance of confrontation* — all fire on a routine employee-manager exchange. Signal Specificity does not mitigate this, because specificity scales *magnitude* while type reads *relative* weights; scaling everything down leaves Fear still dominant.

The theory has no term ranking an attachment as worth having. Type is what should carry that distinction, and type is provisional, uncalibrated and unimplemented. **The verdict is correct and it is not yet safe to operationalise.**

## Reasoning

### What survived, and why

**The two-referent split survived** because the two uses stand in *inverted* part-whole relations to compliance, and a shorthand cannot invert the relation of the thing it abbreviates. It survived a direct challenge — that the two are one term at two granularities, like "car" meaning both a vehicle and a model line — on evidence stronger than the challenge anticipated: the slide between the senses is visible inside one paragraph of the layer's own founding document.

**The signature relation survived** because the corpus holds the two relations apart within a single component without comment. Self-Positioning has species *and* a failure signature, and devaluation never appears among the species. The natural objection — that signatures here are always about failure — is defeated by the persistence column, which is an outside-view description that is not about failure at all.

**The composition verdict survived twice, from independent grounds.** Once from the receiver side (same computation, different weighting) and once from the sender side (generating compliance and generating bonded attachment are the same operation — supply variable-content while keeping the gates intact — differing only in content).

**The two-family reading survived its own prosecution and was strengthened by it.** The objection that the mixed row spans both families turns out to be the reading's prediction: one removable ground plus one non-removable ground falls to the Resonance floor, which is what *"high persistence"* already says.

### What was killed, and why

**The claim that this finding's repair closes the theory's determination gap — killed.** This was the most consequential claim produced during the inquiry, and it failed on two counts, both anchored. Deciding which signature a case shows requires knowing which variable dominates. But the detection layer emits five-level categorical values, so ties are routine — and the taxonomy's own mixed row *is* a tie category, conceding the point. And type reads off the *weighted* sum, while the theory's coefficients are on its pending-calibration list, so there is no weighted argmax to compute. The claim presupposed exactly the determination it claimed to supply. What survives is smaller and better: the gap is **relocated** from unbounded to bounded, onto an item already on the roadmap.

**A single-parameter "volatility" reading of the persistence column — killed and replaced.** It was reached by three independent routes and looked robust. Reading the rows literally broke it: three name an external event withdrawing a ground, one names internal maintenance. That is a binary crossed with abruptness, not one four-valued rate. The replacement is better than the original because it re-derives a split the corpus already holds.

**The claim that the Fear fork brings the user's hypothesis close to ordinary usage — killed and reversed.** Under cost-of-loss, a Fear-dominant attachment reads as dependency, which is *not* what compliance means in ordinary use. The fork does not narrow the gap; it moves the referent. This also corrected an earlier stage of this inquiry, which had held the whole verdict fork-invariant. The relation verdicts *are* fork-invariant; the identification is not.

**Deleting the engagement-state layer outright — killed on actionability, not on structure.** The proposal survived scrutiny: three inquiries cite the layer, but each uses a different job of the word, so the citations do not depend on it existing as a taxonomy. What killed it is that this inquiry cannot discharge a three-inquiry migration and its bounds do not reach one. The content survives inside the re-description: the names are kept, their independent membership is not.

**Identifying compliance with the Supplication pole — killed previously, and not revived.** Supplication describes the sender's attention-direction; compliance describes the receiver's behaviour. Different parties, different objects. Nothing here disturbs that.

**Dyadic asymmetry as a rival discriminator — deferred rather than killed.** It is computable from fields already specified, which is more than persistence can say. But it collides with a committed use of the same measurement — both-direction readings already indicate who is chasing whom — and settling the collision needs data the project does not have.

### The correction that ran against this inquiry's own direction

An earlier stage of this inquiry produced an elegant result: a single volatility parameter deriving the whole persistence column, which in turn appeared to supply the determination mechanism the corpus records as its largest gap here. Both halves failed on literal reading — the column encodes two parameters, not one, and the determination is blocked on uncalibrated coefficients.

It would have been tidier to keep the elegant version. The replacement is less impressive and more useful: a two-parameter reading that re-derives a commitment the corpus already holds, and a gap that is bounded rather than closed.

### The claim this finding deliberately did not settle

Whether Fear means threat-of-harm or cost-of-loss. The corpus has a reserved slot for that inquiry, an earlier finding declared its choice rather than settling it for exactly this reason, and settling it as a side-effect of an adjudication about compliance would be the wrong place. The relation verdicts are stated as fork-invariant; the identification is stated as fork-dependent. Both are conditional on that declaration.

### Blocked

- Whether compressing the attachment-type table's five rows into three engagement states was a defect or a justified simplification. This is empirical and the corpus has no data: the Coerced row has no worked case among the theory's fifteen validation scenarios, and none of this is implemented.

- Whether ordinary-language compliance maps to any attachment type at all. Blocked on the Fear fork: under threat-of-harm it maps to Coerced; under cost-of-loss the Coerced row describes dependency and compliance may map to nothing.

- Whether the signature determination can be made to work. Blocked on the theory's coefficient calibration, which is already on its roadmap.

### Research Frontiers

- **What actively maintains Resonance.** The persistence column says Resonance is *"maintained via shared model,"* which names a maintenance mechanism that neither of this finding's two parameters derives. Ground-externality explains why a variable does not go to zero; it does not explain what keeps it up.

- **Whether the theory should rank attachments as worth having.** The over-report hazard has no mitigation inside the current architecture. Adding such a term would be a genuine architectural change, and no other part of the corpus has needed one.

- **Whether compliance is a signature of Fear-dominance or the evidence for the Fear term itself.** Fear's detection signals and compliance's description pick out the same conduct from opposite sides of the formula. The available escape is that the signals are read per-message while a signature is a trajectory over time — but the theory discards trajectory data at its own boundary, so that escape is currently unavailable to it.

- **The two-family reading re-opens** if the attachment-type table's persistence column is revised or superseded — its rows are this reading's content.

- **The species verdict re-opens** if the type-interconversion question resolves in favour of types *not* converting. The specific blocking feature is the absence of longitudinal data on attachment-type change; re-open on that data existing, not on general reconsideration.

- **The identification of compliance with the Coerced row re-opens** the moment the Fear fork is settled, in either direction.
