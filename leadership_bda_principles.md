# Leadership BDA Principles

**What this is.** Twelve operating principles for leading — attaching people to a cause and holding a following that isn't rented — derived from Belief-Driven Attachment (BDA). This is the application series' eighth document, and the one the series was missing: the earlier leader-list existed only in conversation, and the ego/self-focus finding (`inquiries/2026-08-31_04-53__ego_selffocus_leadership/finding.md`) has since added a layer the list didn't have — the **standing self-claim** and its lattice. Each principle ends with its check-question.

**Why leadership needs its own derivation.** Four structural facts: (1) leadership is a **triangle** — you, the follower, and the cause — and durable followings attach *through* you to the cause, not to you as terminus; (2) the follower's attachment must survive your absence (the empty room is the only honest test — everything extracted by pressure or presence is compliance wearing your colors); (3) the leader-slot itself must be **occupied**: beyond the attention-gate every BDA domain shares, the person-leader holds a **standing self-claim** ("follow me on this"), granted by the room and claimed by the leader — capability without that claim produces a valued peer, never a leader; (4) followers with their own standing claims — the only followers worth having — cannot sit under a claim that doesn't cover theirs, so leading the strong runs on **scoping and shared terms**, never on out-egoing them.

**Priority structure:** **[GATE]** principles multiply — a zero on one zeroes the rest; the others add. Gates win conflicts.

**Provenance:** `BDA/40` (followership; the two-clause criterion; the claim-cover layer; roles), `BDA/21` (Self-Positioning; §8 the standing self-claim), `BDA/60` (the calculation program; verdict-timing; the claim-read), the compliance-versus-attachment, leadership-and-inexhaustibility, glue-versus-leader, leadership-as-calculation, backing-observables, confrontation-milestones, and ego/self-focus findings. ## I · Your own position (the gates)

### 1 · Be attached before you attach anyone — gate first, claim second [GATE]

The room reads your attachment to the cause before anything else, and it reads whether it's *backed*: a performed devotion monitors its audience (attention flicking to how the speech lands), doesn't exist off-stage, and has no cost-trail; a backed one was being paid for before anyone watched. And the **cultivation order is load-bearing**: gate first, claim second. Build the claim before the attention-gate and you manufacture the calibrating performer — a crown on an approval-harvester, caught in the room and decayed over time. The calculation program's order stands: cultivate the state, attend to the work, and let the claim grow on what's actually there.

**Check:** *am I recruiting to the cause, or to my ambition wearing the cause — and is my claim growing on backed ground or ahead of it?*

### 2 · Hold your standing claim at exactly the backed scope [GATE]

The leader-slot is occupied by a **standing self-claim** — "on this, follow me" — and the claim has one calibration rule: **exactly the backed scope**. Under-claim and you refuse the room's grant (the capable adviser who dismisses the role leaves followership orbiting a vacancy — capability alone leads nothing). Over-claim and you've manufactured performed importance: grandiosity wins the first meetings and rides the mimicry arc down, because the claim's backing is readable (defended-or-qualified under challenge; enacted-or-dodged when the slot is offered; persistent-or-absent off-stage). The claim is also **bilateral** — a slot must be granted *and* taken: accepting real responsibility when it's offered *is* the claim enacted; dodging it every time is the claim absent, whatever your talents.

**Check:** *what exactly is my claim's scope — and is every part of it backed by something I've actually done, held, or paid for?*

### 3 · Be the route, not the terminus — and let the term carry the bigness [GATE]

Point their attachment *through* you to the cause. A leader who collects attachment personally builds a fan club capped by his attention budget; a leader who hands it onward to the work builds something that runs in his absence. The ego/self-focus finding sharpened this into the series' most practical law: **to lead people with big claims, get a bigger term, not a bigger ego.** The follower's pride subordinates to the *shared term* — the mission, the product, the craft, even the office — not to your person; the term carries claim-cover your personal claim never could. (The two-clause test keeps you honest: a real term lies outside the receiver and keeps generating content. A manufactured mission is a performed one, and it reads.)

**Check:** *when this person lights up, am I taking it or routing it — and is the thing I'm routing it to real enough to generate on its own?*

### 4 · Attachment, never compliance [GATE]

Pressure, hierarchy, and fear all work — they produce compliance, and compliance evaporates when enforcement stops. The test is the **empty room**: what do they do when you're not in it? Keep exits cheap, count only volunteered contribution as evidence, and read the warning in every regime of mocked-then-obedient lieutenants: people who publicly reversed their verdicts under power are *complying* — the moment the power dips, the room you thought you led was never yours.

**Check:** *is this conduct volunteered or extracted — and what am I building for the day extraction stops working?*

### 5 · Pay first, pay more, pay visibly

The cost-trail is the one leadership signal that can't be faked without becoming real. First in, biggest personal stake, front of the risk — and legible: costs paid invisibly bind no one. You must be paying more than you're asking, and the room must be able to see it.

**Check:** *what am I asking them to sacrifice — and where's my larger, visible sacrifice?*

### 6 · Every expensive decision is the cause's biography

Nobody learns what the cause is from the manifesto; they learn it from what you decide **when it costs you** — the client fired, the star disciplined, the shortcut refused under deadline. One incoherent exception re-prices every prior statement retroactively, and the inner circle's version must match the stage's, because those audiences compare notes.

**Check:** *if this decision were the only thing anyone knew about the cause, what would they conclude it is?*

## II · What binds them

### 7 · Name what they already carry

You cannot install beliefs — only activate ones half-present. A cause that binds is an *articulation*: it says what followers felt but couldn't say, and their experience is recognition, not persuasion. Recruit by resonance; argued-in members are compliance in disguise.

**Check:** *am I about to persuade, or to name?*

### 8 · Keep the source unexhausted

Attachment dies toward exhausted sources: a leader fully known, a roadmap visibly ended, a cause achieved. Keep reserves the room can sense — the next horizon behind the current one, depth not yet spent, the answer that shows you've thought further than the question.

**Check:** *does this interaction imply there's more — or that they've now seen the bottom?*

### 9 · Give each person their own trajectory inside the cause

The cause's arc holds the crowd; the individual is held by *their own* arc within it — who they're becoming because they're here. Carry a hope-account per person: "here's what you'll be able to do in a year that you can't now" is the strongest retention sentence that exists.

**Check:** *what is this person's next chapter here — and do they know it?*

*(The mechanism, added 2026-08-31: trajectories are built by the **grant-craft** — scoped grants issued slightly ahead of the person's actual record, with conversion-occasions to grow into ("you ran the last two launches; run this quarter's"). Two rules keep it clean: **record-moored** (the grant references something real they did) and **portable** (it fills their own ledger — a claim they carry anywhere, not a dependence on you). And for your quietly-capable people who decline what their record supports: never argue with their self-no — arguing entrenches it; grant scoped and let the evidence speak. The milestone-ladder — scopes granted on evidenced record, never tenure — is this principle run as a system. `BDA/60` §8.)*

### 10 · Lead the strong by scoping, never by out-egoing

Your best people carry standing claims of their own — and the claim-cover machinery says they *cannot* sit under a claim that collides with theirs (the resistance is proportional to the scope-gap). The folk conclusion — "be the biggest ego in the room" — is exactly wrong: inflation decays, and the collision grinds. The working geometry is the **lattice**: your claim scoped to direction, theirs to their craft (the general's chef out-egos the general about cooking, lawfully); leadership teams built as *one covering claim per domain* (colliding same-domain claims are why co-founder pairs grind); the **vizier** configuration honored (the counsel-scoped claim that leads the king within its domain). And the standing warning runs both ways: a claim so wide it can accept no cover — yours or theirs — is the **too-proud-to-follow trap**: a general of nothing, coalition-less.

**Check:** *where exactly does my claim end and this person's begin — and am I trying to cover their domain instead of scoping around it?*

## III · Reading the room

### 11 · Make dissent cheap, or lose your eyes [GATE]

Followership can be performed, and a leader who punishes no manufactures performed followership at scale — then reads the nodding as attachment. A dissent-tolerant room is not a virtue purchase; it's your only functioning instrument. The fake-yes marks are the same three as everywhere: instant agreement at politeness-tempo, zero specifics from their side, zero recall later.

**Check:** *when did someone last tell me no and visibly pay nothing for it?*

### 12 · The cause doesn't chase — and verdicts are timed

Self-positioning scales: a movement that begs for members commoditizes itself. Standards make belonging carry information; be visibly willing to lose the wrong people. And the leader is the room's **verdict-timer**: never force premature public binaries (a room's declared no is maximally sticky — the audience multiplies crystallization); stage commitments as carried asks, redirect early forced votes (downgrade, split, defer with a date), and save the deliberate forcing for when the group's verdict is genuinely ready to be locked.

**Check:** *am I about to lower the bar to keep a body in the room — or force a verdict the room's evidence can't yet carry?*

## The advisor-side note (the case the finding was born from)

When *you* are the one offering someone a leader/advisor slot — the wise friend you want guidance from — know the machinery: capability plus pleasantness is not the composition; the slot needs their **standing claim**, and some excellent people (the dialogue-peers) will decline it every time, for good reasons (the role costs asymmetry, forecast-liability, and maintenance they may correctly not want). Their decline is itself evidence of genuineness — a refused status-slot is the opposite of status-seeking. The moves that remain: **read before you offer** (the claim-read, `BDA/60` §8: is their claim defended under challenge, enacted when slots are offered, off-stage persistent, backed?); consume their *occurrent* wisdom inside the symmetric frame (ask opinions peer-to-peer — that they give freely); and **retire declined offers** — standing-role declines accrue like any declined verdict, and the re-ask is an unchanged repeat.

## The compressed card

1. Backed attachment first; gate before claim — always.
2. The standing claim at exactly the backed scope; granted and taken.
3. Route through the term; the term carries the bigness your ego can't.
4. Empty-room test; volunteered conduct only; reversed-verdict obedience is compliance.
5. Pay first, more, visibly.
6. Expensive decisions write the cause's biography.
7. Name what they carry; recruit by recognition.
8. Reserves sensed; horizons chained.
9. A trajectory per person.
10. Scope around strong claims; never out-ego them; fear the too-proud trap in yourself first.
11. Cheap dissent is your instrument panel.
12. The cause doesn't chase; the room's verdicts are timed, not forced.

When principles collide: gates (1, 2, 3, 4, 11) beat everything.
