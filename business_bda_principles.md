# Business BDA Principles

**What this is.** Ten operating principles for commercial life — sales, negotiation, pricing, client relationships, partnerships, retention — derived from Belief-Driven Attachment (BDA). Not a filter of the general daily-life list: these are re-derived from the theory against the situations business actually produces. Each principle ends with the check-question to run before acting.

**Why business needs its own derivation.** The commercial frame changes three structural things: (1) your counterparty *knows you want something* — every display you make is pre-discounted for motive; (2) money and price are themselves a display-medium — every number you name is evidence about your beliefs; (3) the relationship runs in cycles (pitch → delivery → renewal), which makes it a ledger of checkable forecasts. The principles below are the theory routed through those three facts.

**Priority structure (the theory's own):** principles marked **[GATE]** multiply — a zero on any of them zeroes everything else you do; the rest add. When two principles conflict in the moment, the gate wins.

**Provenance:** `BDA/21` (Self-Positioning, backed vs performed displays), `BDA/60` (Return Doctrine, ask-the-no, false-license detection), `BDA/20` (Coherence, congruence, specificity), the insistence finding (composite verdict: manner × budget × horizon), the backing-observables finding (channels, tether, forecast-check), the compliance-versus-attachment and leadership-and-inexhaustibility findings. Status: structural layer — derived and internally tested, uncalibrated by field data. Treat as a lens checked against results.

## I · The standing frame (before any conversation)

### 1 · The seller's discount [GATE]

In any commercial conversation, the other side prices your displays knowing you want the sale. Your enthusiasm, your compliments, your "great question!" — all pre-discounted toward zero, because everyone performs those wanting money. Effort spent on discounted channels is wasted; belief moves only through evidence that **survives the discount**:

- costs you actually paid (time spent on their problem before any contract; misfit revenue you turned down),
- specificity that couldn't be templated (you visibly did the homework on *them*),
- forecasts of yours that came true (last meeting's small promise, kept).

The corollary is freeing: stop polishing the discounted channels. Charm-language, rapport scripts, and flattery are not just weak — the polish itself reads as performance.

**Check:** *does this act cost me something real or contain something they know I couldn't fake — or is it the kind of thing anyone wanting their money would say?*

### 2 · Price is a self-positioning display [GATE]

Every price move is read as evidence about what *you* believe your thing is worth — that's the receiver-side fact of pricing. Consequences:

- A desperation discount doesn't just cut margin; it zeroes the position the whole pitch stood on, and **re-prices your past statements retroactively** ("so the first number was fake").
- Concessions are traded, never given. A concession with nothing in return announces that more exist.
- A principled price — one number, with its reasons, held calmly — is a coherence display worth more than the deal it occasionally loses.
- Walking away from a bad deal, audibly, is the single most binding display available in commerce: it can't be faked without actually costing you the deal, which is exactly why it's believed — and everyone you *didn't* walk away from now knows being chosen means something.

**Check:** *what does this number, or this concession, tell them I believe about my own product?*

### 3 · Organizations don't attach — people do

There is no such receiver as "the client." Attachment forms inside individual humans: the champion, the skeptic, the economic buyer, the end user — each running their own belief-state, each needing their own evidence, each attaching (or not) separately. Two rules follow:

- **Map the room per person.** The champion needs a trajectory (what backing you makes *them* become internally); the skeptic needs discount-surviving evidence (principle 1); the economic buyer needs a forecast they can hold you to (principle 7).
- **One story, always.** They compare notes. The version the champion hears, the version procurement hears, and the version at the demo must be the same story, because any two of them in a hallway is a coherence-check you're not present for.

**Check:** *whose belief-state am I actually addressing right now — and would this survive two of them comparing notes?*

## II · The conversation (in the room)

### 4 · Demonstrate on their problem; never describe yourself

Self-description is evidence of nothing ("we're innovative," "I'm detail-oriented" — costless, therefore weightless). The two working forms:

- **Charm-of-product:** the demo, run on *their* actual case — their menu, their data, their workflow — not the canned one. A demo that can't leave its rails is read as a performance, because it is one.
- **Charm-of-person:** specificity as visible attention. "I looked at how your Thursday service runs before coming" moves more belief than any credential, because target-generated content is expensive to fake per-target and they know it.

Both are needed. The theory's own hard line: **a good object carried by an unattachable person still fails** — the buyer attaches to the person-plus-product, and the person's display is being read the whole time the product is shown. Note the converse hazard: a rehearsed pitch is *fine* if the conviction is real — polish is not performance; absence is. Practice the form, bring the state.

**Check:** *am I about to claim a property, or show one — and is what I'm showing generated for them or replayed?*

### 5 · Exit willing per conversation; return only with something new [GATE]

The chaser gets commoditized; the selective counterpart gets chosen. The full composite, from the theory's insistence verdict (manner × budget × horizon):

- **In the meeting:** make the case once, well. Then make the no cheap to say: *"if it's not a fit, tell me — that's a fine outcome."* No second push in the same scene; in-scene insistence prices you as having no alternatives.
- **Across meetings:** return as often as your budget allows, but **only carrying something new** — a new capability, a changed condition, a result from elsewhere. Re-contact with unchanged content is correlated evidence; it updates nothing and spends standing.
- **Type the no before spending on it:** a *reasoned* no (they processed and declined) — respect it, returns only on changed conditions. A *not-now* — schedule it. A *never-registered* no (they never actually processed you) — a return is legitimate and isn't even really a second ask.

**Check:** *can I genuinely leave this conversation whole on a no — and if I'm returning, what's new in my hand?*

*(Why "something new" works, added 2026-08-26: a declared no is defended by the declarer's own consistency — it can't be reversed, only *re-asked differently*. A return works when it poses a question the old no never answered, judged by their lights: they could say, themselves, "that was then; this is a different thing." If they couldn't say it, it's the same question in new clothes and updates nothing. And never argue with a no — arguing forces them to defend it, and a defended no sets harder. `BDA/60` §11.)*

### 6 · Type the yes before you book it

Receptions are displays too, and the polite fake-yes is the most expensive object in a pipeline. Its three marks (the theory's false-license signals):

1. it arrives **instantly, at politeness-tempo** — no processing gap where real consideration would live;
2. it contains **zero their-side specifics** — nothing about their case attaches to it ("sounds great" vs "this would fix our Thursday bottleneck");
3. at the next contact there is **zero recall** — the yes left no trace in them.

A yes with those marks is a performed reception: don't book it, don't spend return-budget on it, don't count it as pipeline. Convert it honestly by asking for its type — the small concrete next step ("should I come Tuesday with the numbers, or is this a politeness yes? Both are fine") makes the real answer cheap to surface. And accept the reading limit: a first meeting is a short window — read specificity and tempo, log what was implied, **defer the verdict** to the follow-up. Over-reading one meeting is how pipelines fill with ghosts.

**Check:** *does this yes carry their-side specifics — and am I about to spend budget on a politeness?*

## III · The cycle (after the room)

### 7 · Every claim is a forecast; the relationship is the ledger [GATE]

Business runs in checkable cycles: proposal → delivery → renewal. Whatever the pitch claimed, delivery re-prices retroactively — display-credit is always provisional until the cycle closes. So:

- **Under-claim into keepable forecasts.** The small promise kept ("you'll have it Thursday noon" — and they do) is compounding evidence; the big promise missed doesn't just cost that cycle, it re-prices every earlier display as probable performance.
- **Volunteer the ledger.** State your claims in checkable form and reference them when they land ("last month I said X would happen — here's what happened, including the part I got wrong"). Self-audited misses, surfaced by you first, are among the strongest coherence displays available — nobody performing would volunteer them.

**Check:** *what forecast does this claim imply, and will the me of that date be glad I made it?*

### 8 · Attached customers, not captive ones

Lock-in — contracts, switching costs, data hostage — produces compliance, and compliance behaves like compliance: it churns the day the lock weakens, negotiates you down at every renewal meanwhile, and poisons word-of-mouth the entire time. Attachment is the customer who **would re-choose you with a cheap exit available**. Consequences:

- Keep exits deliberately cheap — easy cancellation is a self-positioning display ("we hold you by value, not by paperwork") and it keeps your reading honest: retention behind a lock tells you nothing.
- What they'd *lose* by leaving should be real value, never a penalty — that is fear's only honest commercial form.
- The same principle runs inside the company: an employee retained by vesting handcuffs is complying, not attached; count only what people volunteer.

**Check:** *is this mechanism making them want to stay, or making it expensive to leave?*

### 9 · Be an unexhausted source

Attachment dies toward exhausted sources — a vendor who has shown everything, a product whose roadmap visibly ends, a consultant with no more depth to reveal. Keep the account pointed at a future:

- **Always a visible next thing:** behind every delivery, the next horizon is already in view. Never present the roadmap's end.
- **Reserves, sensed:** answer from one level deeper than the question; leave the impression of stock not yet spent. Don't dump all value in the first engagement — the account that has consumed you completely has no reason to renew.
- **Their trajectory, not just yours:** the account's own arc — what they're becoming as your customer ("first the Thursday fix, then the expansion") — holds individual buyers the way the roadmap holds the logo.

**Check:** *does this interaction imply there's more behind it — or that they've now seen the bottom of me?*

*(The vendor-zone, added 2026-08-26: commercial frames settle exactly like personal ones. Procurement's default label is "just a vendor," and every ordinary delivery cycle **confirms** it — vendor-conduct predicts invoices and deliveries, so invoices and deliveries harden the label; after enough same-shaped cycles the account stops asking what else you could be. Escaping toward "strategic partner" requires **frame-discrepant evidence** — the insight delivered outside the billing relationship, the workshop that isn't a sales call, the introduction that serves them with nothing invoiced — because in-frame excellence, however high, only polishes the vendor label. And know the reopening cost includes their **incumbent-frame value**: the current arrangement works for them, and re-categorizing you puts a working setup at risk — so partner-moves must carry their own weight, not ask the client to gamble the working frame on a label change. Same machinery as every settled frame: `BDA/41` §8.)*

### 10 · Pay visible costs into the relationship before extracting

The cost-trail is the highest-tether channel in commerce — costs actually paid can't be faked without becoming real payment. Paid **first and visibly**, they seed the relationship's asymmetry in your favor:

- the two hours of analysis on their business before any contract existed;
- showing up when it isn't billable;
- turning down misfit revenue, out loud ("we're not right for this — take it to X"), which is simultaneously a paid cost, a selectivity display, and the most-repeated story about you in their network;
- the favor before, and disconnected from, the ask.

One guard: costs read as *choice* only from a held position. The same spec work done from visible desperation reads as chasing and inverts the signal — gate before channel, always (principles 2 and 5 come first).

**Check:** *what have I visibly put in before this ask — and does it read as selection or as chasing?*

## The compressed card

1. Spend only on discount-surviving evidence (paid costs · specificity · kept forecasts).
2. Every number is a belief-display; concessions traded, never given; walk away audibly.
3. People attach, not organizations — map the room per person, keep one story.
4. Demonstrate on their case; the person is being read while the product is shown.
5. Case once → cheap no → leave whole → return only with something new → type the no.
6. Type the yes (tempo · their-side specifics · recall) before spending on it; defer short-window verdicts.
7. Under-claim into keepable forecasts; volunteer the ledger, misses first.
8. Value-held customers, not lock-held; cheap exits keep the reading honest.
9. Next horizon always visible; reserves sensed; the account gets its own trajectory.
10. Pay first, visibly, from position — cost-as-choice, never cost-as-chase.

When principles collide: gates (1, 2, 5, 7) beat everything else.
