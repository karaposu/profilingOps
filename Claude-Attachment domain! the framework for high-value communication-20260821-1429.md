**The paradox of your "attachment domain":**

**What I think is actually happening in your conversations:**

- You're either over-investing (too available, too eager, too many messages) or under-investing (too aloof, conversation dies)
- You're *telling* rather than *showing* value
- There's no clear reason for the conversation to continue—no natural "what's next"
- You're optimizing for their perception of you rather than whether the exchange is genuinely useful

**The real shift:**

The people who are magnetic in text usually share a few traits:

1. **They write with specificity.** Vague = forgettable. "I work in tech" vs "I've been obsessing over why B2B onboarding is so broken" — one is nothing, the other invites a real conversation.

2. **They're not performing.** They say things that are true and interesting to *them*, not calculated for effect. Paradoxically, this reads as higher status.

3. **They imply rather than claim.** Never "I'm really successful at X." Instead, the success is evident from *how* they talk, what they mention offhandedly, what problems they're wrestling with.

4. **They create value in the conversation itself.** The exchange is interesting/useful *now*, not just a pitch for future value. This is what generates the "hope" you mentioned—but organically.

5. **They're comfortable with silence.** They don't need the conversation to continue. They can let it breathe. This reads as having a full life.

6. **They know when to transition.** "This is interesting—want to jump on a call next week?" or "I'm going to be in [city], we should grab coffee." Without this, even good conversations evaporate.

- Are you reacting to them or operating from your own frame?
- Are you filling silence out of discomfort or speaking because you have something to say?
