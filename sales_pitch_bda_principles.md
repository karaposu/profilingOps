# Sales Pitch BDA Principles

**What this is.** Ten operating principles for the pitch scene itself — the walk-in, the cold meeting, the demo, the presentation, the ask — derived from Belief-Driven Attachment (BDA). This is deliberately narrower than `business_bda_principles.md`: that document governs the *relationship* across cycles; this one governs the *scene* — the minutes in the room. Each principle ends with its check-question.

**Why the pitch needs its own derivation.** The pitch is the theory's harshest reading environment, four ways: (1) it is the **maximum-discount scene** — they know exactly why you're here, so every self-serving word is priced at zero before you say it, *and* the pitch is the archetypal performance frame, so their performance-detectors arrive pre-armed; (2) it is the **shortest window with no baseline** — minutes, one shot, and the honest instrument says neither side can read the other deeply here; (3) the position starts **low by construction** — you came to them, which reads as chasing until actively reframed in the first moments; (4) the scene has a **shape** — entry → frame → demonstration → ask → exit — and each phase is a separate display read in sequence, with the exit weighted heaviest (it's the last evidence they hold).

**Priority structure:** **[GATE]** principles multiply — a zero on one zeroes the rest; the others add. Gates win conflicts.

**Provenance:** `BDA/21` (Self-Positioning, Exit-Willingness, backed vs performed displays), `BDA/60` (ask-the-no phrasings, false-license detection, Return Doctrine), `BDA/20` (Signal Specificity, Emotional Congruence), the insistence finding (manner × budget × horizon; the no-typology), the backing-observables finding (the short-window instrument: channels 1–3, the deferred verdict), the masacan outreach work (communicate-not-sell; founder-vs-sales-rep; the visit shape). Status: structural layer, uncalibrated — a lens checked against results.

## I · The standing conditions (won or lost before the pitch starts)

### 1 · You arrive pre-discounted — arm accordingly

Everything you might *say about yourself or your product* is priced at zero before you enter: they have heard every adjective from every seller, and your motive is on your forehead. So walk in carrying only material that survives the discount:

- **Homework-specificity:** evidence you studied *them* before coming ("I watched how your Thursday service runs") — unfakeable per-target, and the single fastest re-pricer of "another salesman" into "someone who paid attention to us."
- **The demo that runs on their case** — not the canned one (principle 4).
- **Paid-cost evidence:** you built this, you've spent on this, you turned down misfit customers for this.

If your prepared pitch is mostly adjectives and claims, you are carrying zeros. Rebuild the load before the next door.

**Check:** *of everything I'm about to say, how much survives them deleting every self-serving sentence?*

### 2 · Posture is read before content [GATE]

You are read in the seconds before you're heard — stance, tempo, and above all *position*: supplicant or peer. The supplicant marks (over-thanking for the time, apologizing for existing, laughing at nothing, visible hunger for the outcome) gate everything after to zero, because no one attaches to what chases them. The peer position is not arrogance; it is the calm of someone with a full pipeline and something genuinely worth these particular minutes — which is why it can't be faked for long if the life behind it is empty (the fix is the pipeline, not the posture). And honor the time-cost out loud: *"two minutes, then I'm gone unless you want more"* — a stated, kept time-budget is a self-positioning display and a paid-cost display in one sentence.

**Check:** *am I entering as someone with something for them, or someone who needs something from them?*

### 3 · Set the frame or inherit "salesman" [GATE]

Every pitch scene opens with an unspoken classification question: *what is this?* If you don't answer it, the default frame — salesman, script, extraction — answers it for you, and every subsequent word is read through it. Set the frame in the first sentences, honestly: *"I'm not a sales rep — I built this thing, and I'm walking to the people it's for, to talk to them."* Founder-frame beats seller-frame not as a trick but because it's a different fact-pattern: a builder communicating an object to its users is *communicate-not-sell*, and the receiver's stance-belief toward those two figures is different at the root. Two conditions: the frame must be **true** (a rep performing "founder energy" is running the exact performance the frame was meant to escape), and it must be **shown, not just claimed** — the depth of answers off-script (principle 4) is what backs it.

**Check:** *what classification is forming in their head right now — and have I given it the true frame to form around?*

*(The wearable form, added 2026-08-31: this whole document compiles into one assumable frame — **"I built this; I'm showing it to the people it's for."** Assume it before the door (dress the part — the outfit manufactures your state; one breath; the premise said once, inwardly, as fact) and it does two things at once: it *retires* the per-door legitimacy questions for the season, and its state *co-generates* everything below — the peer posture, the time-budget, the demo's confidence, the cheap no, the clean exit. You don't run these principles at the door; you wear the frame that produces them. Drop it on the way out — it's for the doors, not the dinner table. `BDA/60` §12.)*

### 4 · Demonstrate on their case; survive leaving the rails

The demo is the pitch's only high-bandwidth evidence — and the canned demo is a performance by construction: polished, target-generic, and brittle off-script. The working form runs on *their* material — their menu, their workflow, their numbers, entered live — and the decisive moments are the **unscripted** ones: the question you didn't plan for, the request to try their weird edge case. Going off-rails competently is the strongest in-moment display the scene offers (it proves the product is real *and* that you know it deeply — the founder-frame's backing in one move); resisting the deviation ("let me get back to the flow") reads as exactly what it is. Welcome the perturbation — invite it: *"throw me your worst case."*

**Check:** *can this demonstration leave its script and land on their actual case — and have I invited it to?*

### 5 · Conviction is the product's second half [GATE]

At close range, they are reading two objects: the product, and *you holding it*. The theory's hard line from the outreach work: **a good object carried by an unattachable person still fails** — and the person's most-read property in a pitch is conviction-backing. Real founder conviction co-generates everything (tempo, precision, the light in the answers, fluency on the unrehearsable — instant depth where composition would need time); performed conviction leaks in the same channels (checking how lines land, enthusiasm arriving a beat late, energy sagging when they look away). This cannot be fixed in the scene. If you don't currently believe the product is right *for this buyer*, the pitch is pre-broken — fix the belief (or the product, or the targeting) before the next door. And where it's genuinely not right for them, say so and leave early: one honest "you're not who this is for" is also a seed (principle 10) — it re-prices every future visit as credible.

**Check:** *do I, right now, actually believe this is right for them — and if not, why am I in this room?*

### 6 · Volunteer a limitation

A pitch with no downsides is a performance by definition — nothing real has no edges, and they know it, so a flawless pitch triggers the detector it was polished to avoid. The counter-display is the **volunteered limitation**, stated plainly and unprompted: *"what it doesn't do is X — if X is your core need, we're not your answer yet."* One honest edge does three jobs at once: it breaks the salesman-frame (sellers hide edges; builders know them), it converts every remaining claim from pitch-noise into testimony (the person who admits X is credible on Y), and it pre-empts the objection on your terms instead of theirs. The gate: it must be a *real* limitation, materially stated — a humble-brag disguised as a flaw ("honestly, we're almost too fast") is the performance squared.

**Check:** *what true limitation have I said out loud in this scene — and if none, what is my flawlessness telling them?*

## III · The ask and the exit

### 7 · One case, once — then make the no cheap [GATE]

The ask is made cleanly, completely, **once**. Then the strongest move in the scene: offer the no as the easy branch — *"and if it's not a fit, tell me straight — that's a fine outcome for me."* Said and meant, this does three things: it displays exit-willingness at the exact moment chasing is expected (maximum contrast, maximum information), it makes any yes you receive *real* (a yes given beside a genuinely cheap no carries information; a pressured yes is theater), and it converts the awkward-exit energy in the room into an honest channel. In-scene insistence — the second push, the "but consider," the discount pulled out at the door — reverses all of it at once: it re-prices you as having no alternatives, converts the scene to compliance theater, and spends standing you'll want for the return visit.

**Check:** *have I made my case exactly once — and is their no genuinely cheap to say to my face?*

*(Timing note, added 2026-08-26: the ask is a conversion event — it demands a stance and samples the room at that moment, and a declared no seals itself. Everything before it — the demo, the conversation, communicate-not-sell — is the **additive mode**: it builds capacity without demanding anything. So place the ask at capacity-peak, read on the gauge (their-side specificity, processed tempo, engaged objections — objections are attention), and when capacity is uncertain, size the ask down: first contact earns a Tuesday-demo ask, not a purchase-verdict. Asking when a yes isn't actable — frozen budget, absent decision-maker — converts "not actable now" into a declared no. Full doctrine: `BDA/60` §11.)*

### 8 · Type the response before spending on it

Three response-types, three treatments — misreading them is how pipelines fill with ghosts:

- **The engaged objection** ("what about our delivery volume?" / "we tried something like this and it broke") — the *best* signal in the room. Objections are attention: they processed you against their real case. Never defend past one; explore it with them — the objection is the demo's next input.
- **The instant unspecific yes** ("sounds great, leave a card") — arriving at politeness-tempo, carrying zero their-side content: a performed reception. Don't book it, don't celebrate it, don't spend return-budget on it. Convert it honestly on the spot: *"should I come Tuesday with your numbers, or is this a politeness-yes? Both are genuinely fine"* — making the real answer cheap to surface.
- **The flat deflection** ("we're all set") — usually a *never-registered* no: they declined the interruption, not the product, which was never actually processed. Log it as unread, not as rejected; a later return with a different entry isn't even a second ask.

And accept the window: minutes, no baseline — read tempo and specificity, log what both sides implied, **defer every deeper verdict** to the next contact.

**Check:** *which of the three was that — and am I about to spend real budget on a politeness?*

### 9 · The exit is the last display — leave clean, leave early [GATE]

The exit is read hardest because it's held longest — it is the evidence they're left holding. Two rules, one per outcome: **on a no, leave whole** — warm, unhurried, zero salt, zero last-gasp push ("thanks for the straight answer — I'll come back when we've built the thing that changes it"); the composure of your exit is the credibility of your return. **On a yes, leave early** — lock the concrete next step and go while the energy is still above the line; overstaying to enjoy the win converts a won scene into a chased one in real time, and every minute past the peak is paid for from the yes itself. Both exits obey the same law: the person who leaves well was never chasing, and the room re-reads the entire visit through how it ended.

**Check:** *is this the moment to go — and will what I do in the next thirty seconds survive being the last thing they remember?*

### 10 · The scene ends; the sequence begins

A pitch is one move in a sequence, and the *sequence-shape* is being read across visits at least as hard as any single scene: it cannot be faked retroactively, which makes it your highest-tether long asset. Before the door closes behind you, bank the move: **type the no** you got (reasoned / not-now / never-registered — each routes differently), **log both forecast-ledgers** (what you implied you'd do — now keep it exactly; what they implied — check it at return), and bind the return rule: **come back only carrying something new** — a new capability, their edge case solved, a result from a competitor's street. The unchanged re-visit ("just checking in!") is correlated evidence — it updates nothing and spends standing; the third visit that each time brought something real has, by then, quietly displayed persistence, seriousness, and growth without ever once insisting. That is the sequence doing the selling.

**Check:** *typed, logged, and — what specifically new will be in my hand when I next walk through this door?*

## The compressed card

1. Carry only what survives the discount: homework-specificity, the live demo, paid costs.
2. Peer posture, stated-and-kept time budget; the position is read before the pitch.
3. Set the true frame in the first sentences — builder communicating, not rep selling — or inherit "salesman."
4. Demo on their case; invite the worst-case; off-rails competence is the scene's strongest display.
5. Real conviction or don't knock; where it's truly not for them, say so and go.
6. Volunteer one real limitation; flawlessness triggers the detector.
7. The case once, then the no offered cheap — said and meant.
8. Objection = engage; instant-vague yes = convert or discard; deflection = unread, not rejected. Defer deep verdicts.
9. On a no leave whole; on a yes leave early; the exit is the evidence they keep.
10. Type the no, keep your implied forecasts, return only with something new — the sequence does the selling.

When principles collide: gates (2, 3, 5, 7, 9) beat everything.
