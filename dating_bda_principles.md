# Dating BDA Principles

**What this is.** Ten operating principles for dating — apps, first meetings, early dating, escalation toward a relationship — derived from Belief-Driven Attachment (BDA). Derived fresh against the dating frame, not filtered from the general list. Each ends with the check-question to run before acting.

**Why dating needs its own derivation.** The dating frame changes four structural things: (1) it is the **no-baseline, short-window** reading condition — neither side has seen the other's ordinary self, which makes early reading weak and early displays cheap; (2) both sides know the other is showing a **curated self** — best-self presentation is frame-expected, so early displays are pre-discounted the way a seller's pitch is; (3) early attachment runs almost entirely on **Hope** — a projected future built on thin evidence — which inflates fast and collides with reality later; (4) it is **symmetric**: you are building attachment in them while deciding whether to attach yourself, and both processes use the same channels.

**Priority structure:** principles marked **[GATE]** multiply — a zero on one zeroes the rest; the others add. Gates win conflicts.

**Provenance:** `BDA/21` (Self-Positioning, Exit-Willingness, backed vs performed displays), `BDA/60` (Return Doctrine, ask-the-no, false-license detection), `BDA/20` (Coherence, Emotional Congruence, Signal Specificity), the insistence finding (manner × budget × horizon; the persistent-suitor cases), the backing-observables finding (channels, baseline, the honest short-window return), the compliance-versus-attachment finding. Status: structural layer, uncalibrated — a lens checked against results, not scripture.

## I · The frame (before anything is said)

### 1 · The curated-frame discount

Both of you know the other brought their best self, so the best self is priced accordingly: looks maintained for the date, stories polished by retelling, opinions sanded for likability — all partially discounted by a competent reader. What moves belief early is what the curation frame **can't produce**:

- specificity about *them* that couldn't be templated ("you said your sister's move was this week — how did it go?"),
- unstaged moments — how you treat the waiter, what you do in the lull, the reaction you didn't prepare,
- small forecasts kept across dates (you said you'd send the article; you did, once, without ceremony).

Corollary: stop over-investing in the discounted channels. The perfect outfit and the rehearsed anecdotes are entry stakes, not evidence — nobody ever attached to a résumé delivered over dinner.

**Check:** *is this impressive-in-frame, or informative-despite-frame?*

### 2 · Only display what the everyday you can maintain [GATE]

Whatever self you show early, the ordinary you inherits the debt. Dating's most common collapse is not rejection — it is the **coherence debt** coming due: the curated self of dates one through three (endlessly available, effortlessly agreeable, permanently fascinating) gets contradicted by the real schedule, the real moods, the real opinions, and the contradiction doesn't just disappoint — it **re-prices everything retroactively** ("what else was staged?"). The person they attach to is the person you must keep being. So display at sustainable amplitude from the start: your actual availability, your actual tastes, your actual energy. It filters faster — which is the point. Filtering slowly is the expensive way.

**Check:** *can the me of a random Tuesday in month four honor what I'm showing right now?*

### 3 · Your life stays primary [GATE]

Exit-willingness is dating's master gate: the over-available chaser is commoditized on contact, and no charm survives it — the gate multiplies everything by zero. Concretely:

- Your existing life (work, friends, training, projects) does not reschedule itself around a stranger's maybe. Fitting them into a full life *is* the display; emptying the calendar for them is the anti-display.
- Initiation has a budget. Make the approach cleanly, propose the concrete plan once, and let silence answer if it wants to. Double- and triple-texting spends standing at the worst exchange rate in the theory.
- Be genuinely able to hear no and leave whole — in every single exchange. Not as a tactic (a performed exit that monitors for pursuit is *leaving-to-be-chased*, and it gets read) — as a fact about your position, which only holds if the life underneath it is real.

**Check:** *am I about to rearrange my life, or my evening's composure, around someone who hasn't yet earned either?*

## II · In the interaction

### 4 · Attention beats declarations

Early declarations are the cheapest currency in dating and are priced at zero: "I really like you," "you're amazing," "I've never met anyone like you" — anyone wanting anything says these. The high-value form of interest is **demonstrated attention**: the remembered detail, the follow-up on the thing they mentioned in passing, the question that proves you processed their last answer instead of loading your next story. Target-generated content is expensive to fake per-person, and — decisive in dating — it *can't have been reused from the last date*, and they know it.

**Check:** *am I about to tell them my interest, or show them evidence of it?*

### 5 · Bring the state or end it [GATE]

Performed fascination gets caught — in the moment (your attention flicking to check how you're landing; reactions arriving a beat late because they're computed; enthusiasm misaligned with your eyes) or across dates (performance is effortful and sags by date three; real interest is free to maintain). So don't manufacture the display: either find the genuine thread of curiosity about this specific person and respond from *that*, or accept the date is a polite miss and end it well. One clarification the theory insists on: nervousness is not performance — real interest produces awkwardness, and awkward-but-genuine outperforms smooth-but-hollow on every channel that matters. Polish is not the goal; presence is.

**Check:** *is what I'm showing right now actually happening in me?*

### 6 · Meet them, don't mirror them

Mirroring — agreeing with everything, liking everything they like, laughing on cue — feels like rapport-building and is actually a double failure: it reads as performance (template reception: instant agreement, zero friction, no distinct self detectable), and any attachment it does build attaches to a false self whose coherence debt you'll pay later (principle 2). Resonance that binds is **discovered**, not performed: the genuinely shared thing found, plus the honest divergence delivered kindly. A friendly "I actually disagree — here's my take" on the second date is double evidence: of a real self, and of real attention. Interchangeable agreement makes you interchangeable.

**Check:** *am I about to echo them, or meet them?*

### 7 · Escalate only through cheap no's

Every rung of the ladder — the number, the first date, the second, exclusivity — is a consent-gate. A yes extracted under pressure, persistence, or awkwardness-avoidance is **compliance-dating**: they keep showing up to avoid the discomfort of stopping, the attachment never forms, and the exit, when it comes, is a ghost. The strong move at every rung is making the no cheap: "no pressure either way — if this isn't it, that's completely fine," said and meant. It costs you the compliance-yeses (which were worthless pipeline) and it makes every yes you do get informative — a yes given with a genuinely free no available is the only romantic yes that means anything.

**Check:** *would a no, right now, be cheap for them to say — and can I actually take it well?*

*(Rung-timing, added 2026-08-26: each rung is an ask that samples where things are — sample below capacity and you manufacture a no that then defends itself. So size each rung to the evidence (a carried ask), and check a yes is actable from their life before demanding it. But the window is two-sided: a frame left unnamed long enough **crystallizes by default** — "we're friends" settles in with no ask anywhere, because never-asking is itself conduct they read. Delay is legitimate only as readiness-waiting (a rising trajectory plus a planned moment); near the peak, the deliberate ask beats waiting, since some awkward moment or third party will force the verdict anyway under worse conditions. `BDA/60` §11.)*

*(The close, developed — added later 2026-08-26: the friend-zone is now mechanized, and it is worth knowing exactly how it works. Your non-asking is **positive evidence** for "friend" — that label *predicts* no move, so every quiet week confirms it; her acting on the label (confiding in you, introducing you as "my friend") records it in grains; her circle settles on the label even faster than she does and feeds it back; and eventually the question doesn't get answered "no" — **it stops being a question at all**. The close is silent: no signal marks it, which makes **window-position mismatch** the standard tragedy — your question still live, hers settled months ago, and nothing tells you until an ask samples it. The mitigation is the light liveness-read (her clarifying probes, the contexts she creates, her unprompted messages — present: open; fading: closing; long-gone: likely settled). And know the reopening price before you defer: a settled frame needs the question *revived* (only frame-discrepant evidence does it — seeing each other genuinely outside the usual context), then re-answered against her accumulated acting-on-the-label — plus the real third cost: the friendship itself is value she'd be risking. Deferred asks don't stay at their old price. `BDA/41` §8.)*

## III · The read and the arc

### 8 · Type their interest before spending on it

Receptions are displays too, and dating's polite fake-yes has the standard three marks: it arrives **instantly at politeness-tempo**, it carries **zero their-side specifics** (no counter-proposal, no reference to anything you actually said, "yeah we should totally do that sometime"), and there is **zero recall later** (the plan never surfaces from their side). Don't spend initiation-budget on it. The clean reads:

- **The re-initiation test:** ease off once and watch. Real interest comes back on its own across a cycle or two — an unprompted message, their proposal, their question. Silence that stays silent is your answer, received cheaply.
- **The window rule:** one date is a short window with no baseline — read specificity and reciprocity, log what they implied, and *defer the verdict* to the next contact. Over-reading a single evening — in hope or in despair — is dating's version of over-reading a first meeting.

**Check:** *does their yes carry specifics and survive a pause — or am I about to spend on a politeness?*

*(One hazard added 2026-08-26 — breadcrumbing, read structurally: interest that arrives cheap, perfectly timed, and one-sided (the effortless late-night message exactly when you were moving on), that keeps "what is this?" permanently unresolved while no ask ever comes, and that deflects your direct clarifying question instead of answering it — three flags of **engineered liveness**: someone maintaining your question, not building toward an answer. Your clarifying question is your verdict-moment; a deflection at it is itself the answer. Close the question yourself and spend elsewhere. `BDA/60` §11.)*

### 9 · Promise small, keep visibly

Early dating is a forecast-ledger with very few entries, so each entry is heavy. The implied "I'll text you tomorrow," the stated "I'll get the tickets," the casual "next time we'll try that place" — all are logged, and kept-vs-missed is the fastest coherence evidence available in the no-baseline zone. Two rules: **under-promise into keepable forecasts** (the small promise kept beats the grand projection every time), and **never inflate the future** — painting date-five futures on date one is Hope-inflation on margin: it feels like momentum and it books a debt, because attachments built on projected futures collide with the delivered present. Let the future arrive slightly better than advertised.

**Check:** *what did I just implicitly promise — and will the me of tomorrow deliver it without being reminded?*

### 10 · Real scarcity only — reserves, never games

Two disciplines that look similar and are opposites:

- **Reserves (do):** pace disclosure — don't spend your whole story, your whole intensity, your best everything in week one; a person fully consumed early has no gradient left to pull along, and a life visibly going somewhere is the strongest standing display in the domain. Being genuinely selective — because your time is really allocated — makes being chosen by you carry information.
- **Games (never):** manufactured jealousy, strategic silence-as-punishment, fake busyness, orchestrated competition. Manufactured fear produces anxious compliance — clinging that mimics attachment while poisoning it — and when the manufacturing is detected (it decays like every performance), it re-prices you wholesale. The theory's line: what they'd lose by losing you must be *real value*, never an engineered anxiety.

**Check:** *is this scarcity a fact about my life, or a production for their consumption?*

## The compressed card

1. Impress less; inform despite the frame (specifics · unstaged conduct · kept micro-forecasts).
2. Display at sustainable amplitude — the everyday you inherits every debt.
3. Life stays primary; initiate once, within budget; able to leave whole, every exchange.
4. Evidence of attention beats declarations of interest.
5. Bring the real state or end it well; nervous-genuine beats smooth-hollow.
6. Discovered resonance plus kind disagreement; mirroring is performance.
7. Every escalation through a cheap no; pressured yeses are ghosts in waiting.
8. Type their yes (tempo · specifics · recall); ease off once and let re-initiation speak; defer one-date verdicts.
9. Small promises, visibly kept; never inflate the future.
10. Reserves and real selectivity — never manufactured scarcity or jealousy.

When principles collide: gates (2, 3, 5) beat everything.
